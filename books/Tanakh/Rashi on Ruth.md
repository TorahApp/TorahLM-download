## Chapter 1


### Verse 1

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:1:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:1:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:1:1)


**וַיְהִי בִּימֵי שְׁפֹט הַשֹּׁפְטִים.** לִפְנֵי מְלֹךְ מֶלֶךְ שָׁאוּל, שֶׁהָיוּ הַדּוֹרוֹת מִתְפַּרְנְסִים עַל יְדֵי שׁוֹפְטִים. וּבִימֵי אִבְצָן הָיָה, שֶׁאָמְרוּ רַבּוֹתֵינוּ, אִבְצָן זֶה בֹּעַז:


**And it happened in the days when judges judged.** [During the period] before King Shaul reigned, when the generations were administered by judges;¹ and this occurred in the days of Ivtzan, as our sages said, “Ivtzan is Bo’az.”²

footnotes:
¹ There was no individual judge who could exercise control over all of Yisroel, rather there were many judges. (Malbim) Alternatively, this was a period when God judged the judges. (Gra)
² Maseches Bava Basra 91a; and Bo’az is a central figure in Megillas Rus.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:1:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:1:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:1:2)


**וַיֵּלֶךְ אִישׁ.** עָשִׁיר גָּדוֹל הָיָה וּפַרְנַס הַדּוֹר. וְיָצָא מֵאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל לְחוּץ לָאָרֶץ מִפְּנֵי צָרוּת הָעָיִן, שֶׁהָיְתָה עֵינוֹ צָרָה בָעֲנִיִּים הַבָּאִים לְדָחְקוֹ, לְכַךְ נֶעֱנַשׁ:


**And a man went.** He was very wealthy,³ and the leader of the generation. He left Eretz Yisroel for regions out of the land because of stinginess, for he was miserly toward the poor who came to press him;⁴ therefore he was punished.⁵

footnotes:
³ איש denotes an important person, as Targum renders, גברא רבא, “a great man.”
⁴ He was the only man to leave Eretz Yisroel because of the famine. Because of his stature and greatness he was severely punished for leaving Eretz Yisroel and remaining in Mo’av. (Malbim)
⁵ He was afraid that the poor would unite and confiscate all of his possessions. (Malbim)



### Verse 2

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:2:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:2:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:2:1)


**אֶפְרָתִים.** חֲשׁוּבִים. וְכֵן "בֶּן תֹּחוּ בֶן צוּף אֶפְרָתִי" אֶבְגִּינוֹס. רְאֵה חֲשִׁיבוּתָם, שֶׁהֲרֵי הִשִּׂיא עֶגְלוֹן מֶלֶךְ מוֹאָב אֶת בִּתּוֹ לְמַחְלוֹן, דְּאָמַר מַר, רוּת בִּתּוֹ שֶׁל עֶגְלוֹן הָיְתָה. דָּבָר אַחֵר, "אֶפְרָתִים", בֵּית לֶחֶם קְרוּיָה אֶפְרָת:


**Ephrathites.** Important people, and similarly, “the son of Tochu, the son of Tzuph, an Ephrathite,”⁶ [meaning] an aristocrat. Look how important they were, for Eglon the King of Mo’av married off his daughter to Machlon, as the Master said, “Rus was the daughter of Eglon.”⁷ Another explanation of “Ephratites,” [they were from Beis Lechem,] because Beis Lechem is called Ephros.⁸

footnotes:
⁶ I Shmuel 1:1.
⁷ Maseches Sanhedrin 105b.
⁸ See Bereishis 48:7, “and I buried her [Rachel] on the way to Ephros which is Beis Lechem.”



### Verse 3

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:3:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:3:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:3:1)


**אִישׁ נָעֳמִי.** לָמָּה נֶאֱמַר? מִכַּאן אָמְרוּ, אֵין אִישׁ מֵת אֶלָּא לְאִשְׁתּוֹ. (וְאָמַר "אִישׁ נָעֳמִי" כְּלוֹמַר, לְפִי שֶׁהוּא הָיָה אִישׁ נָעֳמִי וְשׁוֹלֵט עָלֶיהָ וְהִיא טְפֵלָה לוֹ, לָכֵן, פָּגְעָה בוֹ מִדַּת הַדִּין וְלֹא בָהּ):


**Naomi’s husband.** Why is this stated again? From here they derived, “A man does not die except for his wife [i.e., she feels the loss more than anyone].⁹ ([Another interpretation:] And it states, “Naomi’s husband,” i.e., that because he was Naomi’s husband and ruled over her¹⁰ and she was subordinate to him, therefore the Divine Attribute of Justice struck him and not her.)¹¹

footnotes:
⁹ Maseches Sanhedrin 22b.
¹⁰ See Rashi in Bereishis 9:20.
¹¹ Because once he decided to leave, his wife and two sons were compelled to follow.



### Verse 5

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:5:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:5:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:5:1)


**גַם שְׁנֵיהֶם.** מַהוּ "גַם"? בַּתְּחִלָּה לָקוּ בְמָמוֹנָם וּמֵתוּ גְמַלֵּיהֶם וּמִקְנֵיהֶם, וְאַחַר כָּךְ מֵתוּ גַם הֵם:


**Both.** What is the meaning of “also” גַם? First they were struck by financial loss and their camels and their cattle died; afterwards they “also” died.¹²

footnotes:
¹² When God punishes man He first deprives him of his property, and if he does not repent, God smites him in person. Alternatively, “also” indicates that their death was punishment “also” for having remained outside of Eretz Yisroel.



### Verse 7

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:7:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:7:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:7:1)


**וַתֵּצֵא מִן הַמָּקוֹם.** לָמָּה נֶאֱמַר? הֲרֵי כְבָר נֶאֱמַר: "וַתָּשָׁב מִשְּׂדֵי מוֹאָב", וּמֵהֵיכָן תָּשׁוּב אִם לֹא תֵצֵא מִן הַמָּקוֹם שֶׁהָיְתָה שָׁם? אֶלָּא מַגִּיד שֶׁיְּצִיאַת צַדִּיק מִן הַמָּקוֹם נִכֶּרֶת וְעוֹשָׂה רשֶׁם. פָּנָה זִיוָהּ, פָּנָה הֲדָרָהּ, פָּנָה שִׁבְחָהּ שֶׁל עִיר. וְכֵן "וַיֵּצֵא יַעֲקֹב מִבְּאֵר שָׁבַע":


**She left the place.** Why is this stated? It is already stated, “and she returned from the fields of Mo’av,”¹³ and from where could she return if not from the place where she had been? Rather the phrase tells us that the departure of a righteous person from a place is noticeable and makes an impression; its splendor departs, its glory departs, the praise of the city departs. And similarly, “And Yaakov left Be’er Sheva.”¹⁴

footnotes:
¹³ Above, verse 6.
¹⁴ Bereishis 28:10.



### Verse 12

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:12:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:1)


**כִּי זָקַנְתִּי מִהְיוֹת לְאִישׁ.** שֶׁאֶנָּשֵׂא לוֹ וְאוֹלִיד בָּנִים וְתִנָּשְׂאוּ לָהֶם, שֶׁאֵינָם אֲסוּרִים לָכֶם וְאֵינְכֶם אֲסוּרוֹת לָהֶם מִשּׁוּם אֵשֶׁת אָחִיו שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה בְעוֹלָמוֹ שֶׁאֵינָהּ זְקוּקָה לְיָבָם, לְפִי שֶׁלֹּא הָיוּ לְמַחְלוֹן וְכִלְיוֹן קִדּוּשִׁין בָּהֶן שֶׁנָּכְרִיּוֹת הָיוּ וְלֹא נִתְגַּיְּרוּ וְעַכְשָׁיו הֵן בָּאוֹת לְהִתְגַּיֵּר כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר, "כִּי אִתָּךְ נָשׁוּב לְעַמֵּךְ". מֵעַתָּה, נִהְיֶה לְעַם אֶחָד:


**For I am too old to have a husband.** That I should marry him and bear sons, that you would marry them, for they would not be forbidden to you [to marry] and you would not be forbidden to them as far as [the prohibition against marrying] the wife of an older brother who died before he was born, for she [the widow] does require a levirate marriage because Machlon and Chilyon were not [halachically] married to them because they were gentiles and had not converted,¹⁵ and now they were coming to convert as it is stated, “We will return with you to your people.”¹⁶ From now on we will become one people.

footnotes:
¹⁵ Had they converted before, their marriage would be binding and Rus would be prohibited from marrying Machlon’s yet unborn brother. The law of levirate marriage does not apply to a person born after his brother’s death. Marrying one’s brother’s wife constitutes incest when levirate marriage does not apply. However, since there was no binding marriage, Rus would be permitted to marry Machlon’s yet unborn brother.
¹⁶ Above, verse 10.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:12:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:2)


**כִּי אָמַרְתִּי יֶשׁ לִי תִקְוָה.** כִּי אֲפִלּוּ אָמַר לִי לִבִּי יֶשׁ לִי תִקְוָה לִנָּשֵׂא עוֹד וְלָלֶדֶת בָּנִים:


**Even if I could say, “I have yet hope.**” For even if my heart told me that I have hope to marry again and to bear sons.

**גַּם הָיִיתִי הַלַּיְלָה לְאִישׁ.** וְיוֹתֵר מִכֵּן, אֲפִלּוּ הָרִיתִי הַלַּיְלָה זְכָרִים:


**Even if I were to have a husband tonight.** And more than this, even if I were to conceive male offspring tonight.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:12:4

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:4](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:12:4)


**וְגַם יָלַדְתִּי בָנִים.** אוֹ אֲפִלּוּ כְּבָר יָלַדְתִּי בָנִים:


**And even if I were to bear sons.** Or even if I had already borne sons.



### Verse 13

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:13:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:1)


**הֲלָהֵן תְּשַׂבֵּרְנָה.** בִּתְמִיהַּ, שֶׁמָּא לָהֶם תְּצַפֶּינָה עַד אֲשֶׁר יִגְדָּלוּ? לְשׁוֹן "שִׂבְרוֹ עַל ה' אֱלֹהָיו":


**Would you wait for them.** This is a question posed in wonder, “Would you perhaps wait for them until they grew up?”¹⁷ This תְּשַׂבֵּרְנָה is an expression similar to, “whose hope שִׂבְרוֹ is in Adonoy his God.”¹⁸

footnotes:
¹⁷ The feminine form of them [להן] is used, because Naomi was hinting that perhaps she would bear daughters instead of sons, so why wait for “them”?
¹⁸ Tehillim 146:5. Also, see Megillas Esther 9:1.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:13:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:2)


**תֵּעָגֵנָה.** לְשׁוֹן אָסוּר וְכָלוּא כְּמוֹ "עָג עוּגָה וְעָמַד בְּתוֹכָהּ". וְיֵשׁ פּוֹתְרִין לְשׁוֹן עִגּוּן, וְלֹא יִתָּכֵן, שֶׁאִם כֵּן הָיָה לוֹ לִנָּקֵד הַנּוּ"ן דָּגֵשׁ אוֹ לִכְתֹּב שְׁנֵי נוּנִי"ן:


**Would you shut yourselves in.** An expression of עוֹג, being bound and imprisoned, as in, “He made a [confining] circle and stood within it.”¹⁹ Others interpret it as an expression of עִגּוּן, anchoring [i.e., being unable to marry], but that cannot be, for if so the *nun* should have been punctuated with a *dagesh* or it should have been spelled with two nuns.²⁰

footnotes:
¹⁹ Maseches Ta’anis 19a.
²⁰ See Maseches Bava Basra 73a and Rashi’s commentary there.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:13:3

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:3](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:13:3)


**כִּי יָצְאָה בִי יַד ה'.** אָמַר רַבִּי לֵוִי, כָּל מָקוֹם שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "יַד ה'" מַכַּת דֶּבֶר הוּא. וּבִנְיַן אָב לְכֻלָּם "הִנֵּה יַד ה' הוֹיָה":


**For against me is directed the hand of Adonoy.** Rabbi Leivi said, “Wherever it mentions “the hand of Adonoy,” it refers to a plague of pestilence, and the classic example is, “Behold the hand of Adonoy is directed [at your livestock ... a very heavy pestilence].”²¹

footnotes:
²¹ Shemos 9:3.



### Verse 15

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:15:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:15:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:15:1)


**הִנֵּה שָׁבָה יְבִמְתֵּךְ.** זֶה טַעְמוֹ לְמַעְלָה תַּחַת הַשִּׁי"ן לְפִי שֶׁהוּא לְשׁוֹן עָבַר. "וּבַבֹּקֶר הִיא שָׁבָה" טַעְמוֹ לְמַטָּה בַּבֵי"ת לְפִי שֶׁהוּא לְשׁוֹן הֹוֶה, וְכֵן כָּל כַּיּוֹצֵא בָהֶם:


**See, your sister-in-law has gone back.** In this [word], the accent is at the beginning [on the first syllable] under the *shin*, because it is in the past tense. [But,] “and in the morning she would return,”²² the accent is at the end [on the last syllable], on the *bais*, because it is in the present tense, and so it is in all similar cases.²³

footnotes:
²² Megillas Esther 2:14.
²³ See Rashi in Bereishis 29:6.



### Verse 16

###### Rashi on Ruth 1:16:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:16:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:16:1)


**אַל תִּפְגִּעִי בִי.** אַל תִּפְצְרִי בִי:


**Do not urge me.** Do not press me.


###### Rashi on Ruth 1:16:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:16:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Ruth/r/1:16:2)


**כִּי אֶל אֲשֶׁר תֵּלְכִי אֵלֵךְ.** מִכַּאן אָמְרוּ רַבּוֹתֵינוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה, גֵּר שֶׁבָּא לְהִתְגַּיֵּר מוֹדִיעִין לוֹ מִקְצַת עֳנָשִׁים, שֶׁאִם בָּא לַחֲזֹר בּוֹ יַחֲזֹר, שֶׁמִּתּוֹךְ דְּבָרֶיהָ שֶׁל רוּת אַתָּה לָמֵד מַה שֶּׁאָמְרָה לָהּ נָעֳמִי. "אָסוּר לָנוּ לָצֵאת חוּץ לַתְּחוּם בַּשַּׁבָּת". אָמְרָה לָהּ, "בַּאֲשֶׁר תֵּלְכִי אֵלֵךְ". "אָסוּר לָנוּ לְהִתְיַחֵד נְקֵבָה עִם זָכָר שֶׁאֵינוֹ אִישָׁהּ". אָמְרָה לָהּ, "בַּאֲשֶׁר תָּלִינִי אָלִין". "עַמֵּנוּ מֻבְדָּלִים מִשְּׁאָר עַמִּים בְּתַרְיַ"ג מִצְוֹת", "עַמֵּךְ עַמִּי". "אָסוּר לָנוּ עֲבוֹדַת כּוֹכָבִים, "אֱלֹהַיִךְ אֱלֹהָי". "אַרְבַּע מִיתוֹת נִמְסְרוּ לְבֵית דִּין, "בַּאֲשֶׁר תָּמוּתִי אָמוּת". "שְׁנֵי קְבָרִים נִמְסְרוּ לְבֵית דִּין, אֶחָד לְנִסְקָלִין וְנִשְׂרָפִין וְאֶחָד לְנֶהֱרָגִין וְנֶחְנָקִין". אָמְרָה לָהּ, "וְשָׁם אֶקָּבֵר":


**For wherever you go, I shall go.** From here our Rabbis of blessed memory derived²⁴ that if a [prospective] proselyte comes to convert, we inform him of some of the punishments [for violating the commandments] so that is he decides to renege [from his intention to convert], he can renege; for out of the words of Rus, you can learn what Naomi said to her. [Naomi said.] “We may not venture outside the boundary [of 2000 cubits beyond city limits] on Shabbos.” She [Rus] replied to her, “For wherever you go I shall go.”²⁵ [Naomi then said,] “We are prohibited to allow a woman to be secluded with a man who is not her husband.” She [Rus] replied, “Where you lodge, I will lodge.” [Naomi said,] “Our nation is separated from other nations by 613 commandments,” [and Rus replied,] “Your people are my people.” [Naomi said,] “Idolatry is forbidden to us,”²⁶ [to which Rus replied,] “Your God is my God.” [Naomi then said,] “Four [types of] death penalties were delegated to Beis Din [to punish transgressors],” [and Rus replied,] “Where you die, I will die.” [Naomi continued,] “Two burial plots were delegated to Beis Din [to bury those executed], one for those stoned and those burned, and one for those decapitated and those strangled.” She [Rus] replied, “And there I shall be buried.”

footnotes:
²⁴ In Maseches Yevamos 47b.
²⁵ Alternatively, just as you are going to Eretz Yisroel for the sake of your religion, so it is my purpose to go there in order to be able to keep the Torah’s commandments. (Malbim)
²⁶ Although idolatry is forbidden to Noahites, they are forbidden to worship idols only if they do so exclusively, but not if they worship God in conjunction with their idolatry. (Torah Temimah)

###### Rashi on Ruth 3:12:1

**As Adonoy lives.** She said to him, “You are dismissing me with words [i.e., excuses].” He jumped up and swore to her¹⁸ that he would not dismiss her with words. ### Verse 15

###### Rashi on Ruth 3:15:1

**שֵׁשׁ שְׂעֹרִים.** אִי אֶפְשַׁר לוֹמַר שֵׁשׁ סְאִין, שֶׁאֵין דַּרְכָּהּ שֶׁל אִשָּׁה לָשֵׂאת כְּמַשּׂאוֹי זֶה, אֶלָּא שֵׁשׁ שְׂעוֹרִים מַמָּשׁ. וְרָמַז לָהּ שֶׁעָתִיד לָצֵאת מִמֶּנָּה בֵּן שֶׁמִּתְבָּרֵךְ בְּשֵׁשׁ בְּרָכוֹת: "רוּחַ חָכְמָה וּבִינָה, עֵצָה וּגְבוּרָה, רוּחַ דַּעַת וְיִרְאַת ה'":


**Six measures of barley.** It is impossible to say [that it means] six *se’ah*, because it is not customary for a woman to carry such a [large] load; rather, [it means] literally, six barleycorns; and he hinted to her that there was destined to emerge from her a son who would be blessed with six blessings: “the spirit of wisdom and understanding, counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and fear of Adonoy.”²⁰ which is one sixth of a *se’ah*, also referred to as a שש and mentioned in Yechezkel 45:13. The Mishnah in Maseches Pe’ah (8:7) states that a poor person who is traveling from place to place must be given not less than a half *kav* of wheat from which to make bread. Bo’az therefore gave Naomi a whole *kav*, one half for Rus and the other half for herself. (Malbim) The six barleycorns may allude to the six righteous men, each blessed with six virtues are destined to descend from this marriage: Dovid, Daniel, Chananya, Mishaeil, Azaryah and Moshiach. (Targum)

²⁰ Yeshaya 11:2. Alternatively, this is the equivalent of a kav
