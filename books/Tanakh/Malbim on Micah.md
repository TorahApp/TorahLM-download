## Chapter 1


### Verse 2

###### Malbim on Micah 1:2:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:2:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:2:1)


**שמעו עמים כולם** ר"ל כל השבטים, וגם **הקשיבי ארץ ומלואה** כלל הארץ כי משום התפשטה הרעה על כל הארץ, **ויהי ה' בכם לעד** ה' הוא המתרה בכם, **ה'** מתרה בכם **מהיכל קדשו** בעודהו בהיכלו, טרם יצא ממקומו לשפוט את הארץ:


**Hear, all you peoples**—meaning all the tribes; and also, **Give ear, O earth and all that fills it**—the entire earth, for from there the evil spread throughout the earth. **And let Hashem be a witness against you**—Hashem is the One warning you. **Hashem** warns you **from His holy sanctuary**, while He is still in His sanctuary, before He goes forth from His place to judge the earth.



### Verse 3

###### Malbim on Micah 1:3:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:3:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:3:1)


**כי הנה** בעת **אשר ה' יוצא ממקומו,** (שזה ממליץ שילך בעצמו להעניש בעונש השגחיי, שאינו מכה ע"י שליח רק בעצמו כמו כחצות הלילה אני יוצא בתוך מצרים).


**For behold**—at the time **when Hashem goes forth from His place** (this expression signifies that He Himself will go forth to inflict punishment through His Providence, striking not through an emissary but in person, as in, “About midnight I will go forth into the midst of Egypt”).


###### Malbim on Micah 1:3:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:3:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:3:2)


**וירד ודרך על במתי ארץ,** מצייר בדרך ההגשמה שיעמוד תחלה על הרים הגבוהים:


**And He will descend and tread upon the high places of the earth**—using corporeal imagery, he depicts Him as first standing upon the lofty mountains.



### Verse 4

###### Malbim on Micah 1:4:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:4:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:4:1)


**ונמסו,** ואז ימסו ההרים מפחד ה' ומהדר גאונו, והמאמרים מקבילים, ונמסו ההרים **תחתיו כדונג מפני האש,** שימסו בקל כדונג, **והעמקים יתבקעו כמים מוגרים במורד,** כן יתהפכו המוצקים לדבר רך ונוזל, שזה משל אל חורבן הארץ, שתשוב להיות כמים כמו שהיתה בעת התהו (וכ"ז מליצה, ע"ד הרים נזלו מפני ה', ותגעש ותרעש הארץ, וכדומה):


**And they will melt**—then the mountains will melt from dread of Hashem and the splendor of His majesty. The clauses correspond: the mountains will melt **beneath Him like wax before fire**, melting as readily as wax; **and the valleys will split open like water poured down a slope**—thus solid substances will become soft and fluid. This is a metaphor for the devastation of the land, which will become water again, as it was in the time of primordial chaos. (All this is figurative language, like “The mountains flowed before Hashem,” “The earth shook and quaked,” and similar expressions.)



### Verse 5

###### Malbim on Micah 1:5:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:5:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:5:1)


**בפשע, כ"ז יהיה בפשע יעקב** שהם עשרת השבטים, שהם פשעו ומרדו בה' דרך מרידה ועבדו עצבים וחמנים, **ובחטאת בית ישראל** הם שבט יהודה ובנימין, שהם לא מרדו רק חטאו מצד התאוה או השוגג כמו מה שהקריבו בבמות שהגם שהיה חטא לא היה מרידה כי רובן היה לשמים, ומפרש **מי** הגורם **פשע יעקב** מה שהשבטים עבדו ע"ז, **הלא שומרון,** שהמלכות אשר בשומרון הם עשו את העגלים והסיתו את העם לזה, **ומי** הגורם **במות יהודה הלא ירושלם,** שמלכות יהודה שהיתה בירושלים גרמו זאת על שלא בטלו הבמות עד ימי חזקיה:


**For the transgression—all this will be for the transgression of Jacob**—these are the ten tribes, who transgressed and deliberately rebelled against Hashem, worshiping idols and sun pillars. **And for the sin of the house of Israel**—these are the tribes of Judah and Benjamin, who did not rebel but sinned out of desire or error, as when they offered sacrifices at the high places. Although this was a sin, it was not rebellion, since most of these offerings were intended for Heaven. He explains: **Who** caused **the transgression of Jacob**, whereby the tribes worshiped idols? **Was it not Samaria?** For the monarchy in Samaria made the calves and enticed the people into this worship. **And who** caused **the high places of Judah? Was it not Jerusalem?** For the monarchy of Judah, situated in Jerusalem, brought this about by failing to abolish the high places until the days of Hezekiah.



### Verse 6

###### Malbim on Micah 1:6:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:6:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:6:1)


**ושמתי,** זה מוסב עמ"ש ויהי ה' בכם לעד, שה' מתרה בם ואומר אני בעצמי **אשים שומרון לעי השדה** כי יפלו הבתים ויחרבו, ואח"ז אשים אותה **למטעי כרם,** ואז **והגרתי לגי אבניה,** שנוטעי כרמים יסקלו האבנים ויפנו את העיים שנשארו ממפולת הבתים עד שגם **יסודיה אגלה** כי יפנו גם אבני היסודות כדי שיהיה ראוי למטעי כרמים:


**And I will make**—this refers back to “And let Hashem be a witness against you,” for Hashem warns them, saying: I Myself **will make Samaria a heap of ruins in the field**, as its houses will fall and be destroyed. Afterward, I will make it **a place for planting vineyards**, and then **I will pour its stones into the valley**, for those planting vineyards will clear away the stones and remove the heaps left by the fallen houses, until **I will lay bare even its foundations**, as they will remove even the foundation stones to make the ground suitable for planting vineyards.



### Verse 7

###### Malbim on Micah 1:7:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:7:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:7:1)


**וכל פסיליה,** הפסילים היו מעץ או אבן והיו נותנים להם אתנן ומתנות, ותחתיהם היו העצבים, וכשיוכתו הפסילים ישארו העצבים שממה, **כי מאתנן זונה קבצה** שומרון את כל עושרה, שלדעתה היה כל הצלחתם ע"י הע"ז, כמ"ש (הושע ב׳:י״ד) אשר אמרה אתנה המה לי אשר נתנו לי מאהבי, ולכן **ישובו עד אתנן זונה,** יקח אותם מלך אשור כאלו ע"ז שלו נותנות אותם לו לאתנן. ר"ל על שומרון וחורבנה איני מצטער כלל, אחר שעבדו פסילים ועצבים וזנו מאחרי:


**And all her carved images**—the carved images were made of wood or stone, and people gave them hire and gifts; beneath them stood the idols. When the carved images are smashed, the idols will remain desolate. **For she gathered it from a harlot’s hire**—Samaria gathered all her wealth in this way, for she believed that all her prosperity came through idol worship, as it says, (Hosea 2:14) “Of which she said: These are my hire, which my lovers have given me.” Therefore, **to a harlot’s hire they shall return**—the king of Assyria will take them, as though his idols were giving them to him as hire. That is to say: I do not grieve at all over Samaria and its destruction, since they worshiped carved images and idols and strayed after others, forsaking Me.



### Verse 8

###### Malbim on Micah 1:8:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:8:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:8:1)


(ח-ט) **על זאת,** אבל **על זאת אספדה ואילילה** עד שאלך, כמו משוגע וכאיש שמחמת שגעונו ילך ערום, על **כי אנושה מכותיה** שהיא חולי מסוכנת מתדבקת, ועברה מן שומרון גם לארץ יהודה וגם הם הוכו במכות הגם שהם לא עבדו ע"ז בימי יותם ועוזיה והיה סחרם ואתננם קדש לה'. ויותר נראה שמ"ש אנושה מכותיה היינו המכות שהכתה שומרון את יהודה, ודבר פה על המכות הגדולות שהכו עשרת השבטים את יהודה בימי אחז שאז הרג פקח בן רמליה ביהודה מאה ועשרים אלף ביום אחד, וגם אז פשטו פלשתים בערי השפלה והנגב ליהודה וילכדו כמה ערים וישבו שם (דה"ב כ"ח) (כי בימי עוזיה ויותם היה שלום ביהודה, וכן קשה לפרשו על מה שבא סנחריב בימי חזקיה שא"כ היה מזכיר גם תשועתם אז), ור"ל המכות שהכתה שומרון את יהודה הם אנושים ומסוכנים כי **באה עד יהודה** מה שנפלו אז אפרים וארם ואדום ופלשתים ביהודה **ונגע עד ירושלים** כי את ירושלים לא יכלו לכבוש כמ"ש ישעיה (סי' ז'):


(8–9) **For this**—but **for this I will lament and wail**, until I walk about like a madman, like someone who goes naked in his madness, because **her wounds are grievous**—a dangerous, contagious sickness that spread from Samaria to the land of Judah. They too suffered blows, although they did not worship idols in the days of Jotham and Uzziah, and their merchandise and hire were holy to Hashem. It seems more likely, however, that “her wounds are grievous” refers to the wounds Samaria inflicted upon Judah. He speaks here of the severe blows the ten tribes dealt Judah in the days of Ahaz, when Pekah son of Remaliah killed 120,000 in Judah in a single day. At that time, the Philistines also raided the cities of Judah’s lowlands and southern region, capturing several cities and settling in them (II Chronicles 28). (For in the days of Uzziah and Jotham there was peace in Judah. It is likewise difficult to interpret this as referring to Sennacherib’s invasion in the days of Hezekiah, since he would then have mentioned their deliverance as well.) He means that the wounds Samaria inflicted upon Judah were grievous and dangerous, for **it has reached Judah**—when Ephraim, Aram, Edom, and the Philistines invaded Judah—**and reached as far as Jerusalem**; but Jerusalem itself they could not conquer, as stated in Isaiah (chapter 7).



### Verse 10

###### Malbim on Micah 1:10:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:10:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:10:1)


**בגת אל תגידו,** אחר שאמר שיעשה מספד כתנים, חוזר ואומר שישתקו ולא יצעקו כדי שלא יגידו בגת, כי גת של פלשתים לקחה עוזיה מיד פלשתים (דה"ב כ"ו) והיתה תחת יד יהודה, ופלשתים היו מצפים לקחתה מידם, ולכן **בכו אל תבכו** כדי שלא יודע לפלשתים ויתחזקו עליכם, רק **בבית לעפרה** שהיתה מנחלת בנימין (יהושע י"ח כ"ג) שם **עפר התפלשי** כי היא רחוקה מפלשתים ולא יודע להם האבל והמספד שלכם:


**Tell it not in Gath**—after saying that he would lament like jackals, he now says that they should remain silent and not cry out, lest word be told in Gath. For Uzziah had taken Gath of the Philistines from them (II Chronicles 26), and it was under Judah’s control, while the Philistines were waiting to recapture it. Therefore, **weep not at all**, lest the Philistines learn of it and gain strength against you. Only **in Beth-leaphrah**, which belonged to Benjamin’s inheritance (Joshua 18:23), there **roll yourself in the dust**, for it is far from the Philistines, and your mourning and lamentation will not become known to them.



### Verse 11

###### Malbim on Micah 1:11:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:11:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:11:1)


**עברי,** עיר ששמה שפיר שבא לשם האויב הפשיט את כולם ערומים והניח אותם בחיים, כי לא לחמו אותו, אבל עיר צאנן סגרו חומות העיר ולחמו עם האויב, והגם שהרג האויב כל היושבים בבתים שסביבות העיר חוץ מן החומה לא רצו לפתוח שעריהם, ולכן עת נכבשו הרג האויב את כולם ולא הסתפק בשללם לבד, עפ"ז יאמר במליצתו, **עברי, עריה בושת לכם יושבת שפיר,** עריה בושת היינו גילוי הערוה, ומציין עריה וגילוי הבושת כעצם מופשט, את עריה בושת לא תלכי לצאנן, (כי שם יהרגו כולם) רק עברי אל יושבת שפיר, שאתם יושבת שפיר תלכו ערומים וחשופי שת, ולכם צויתי לעריה בושת לעבור לכם, כי אצלכם תמצא ערומים גלויי בושת, אבל **יושבת צאנן לא יצאה** מן החומה גם **אל מספד בית האצל** הגם שכל הבתים שאצלה עשו מספד גדול על הרוגיהם בכ"ז לא רצו בני צאנן לצאת ולפתוח שערי העיר, ע"כ **יקח מכם עמדתו,** המספד יקח מקום עמידתו מכם, כי בעירכם יהיה הרג רב, וברחוב העיר יעשו המעמד ששם יעמוד המספיד לספוד על ההרוגים שנהרגו בעיר ובבית האצל היינו בבתים שאצל העיר:


**Pass over.** When the enemy came to the city named Shaphir, he stripped all its inhabitants naked but left them alive, because they did not fight him. The city of Zaanan, however, shut its gates and fought the enemy. Although the enemy killed everyone living in the houses surrounding the city outside its walls, they refused to open their gates. Therefore, when they were conquered, the enemy killed them all, rather than contenting himself with their spoil. Accordingly, he says figuratively, **Pass over—shameful nakedness—to you, inhabitants of Shaphir.** “Shameful nakedness” means the exposure of the private parts. He personifies nakedness and shameful exposure as an abstract entity: You, shameful nakedness, shall not go to Zaanan, for everyone there will be killed; instead, pass over to the inhabitants of Shaphir. For you, inhabitants of Shaphir, will go naked, with your buttocks exposed, and it is to you that I have commanded shameful nakedness to pass, for among you it will find people naked and shamefully exposed. But **the inhabitants of Zaanan did not come out** from within the walls, even **to the mourning of Beth-haezel**. Although all the neighboring houses raised a great lament over their dead, the people of Zaanan nevertheless refused to come out and open the city gates. Therefore, **it will take its standing place from you**—the lamentation will take its place among you, for there will be great slaughter in your city. In the city square they will hold the gathering where the mourner stands to lament those killed in the city and in Beth-haezel—that is, in the houses beside the city.



### Verse 12

###### Malbim on Micah 1:12:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:12:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:12:1)


**כי חלה,** מבאר מדוע לא יצאה יושבת צאנן ועל מי בטחו, כי **יושבת מרות** היינו צאנן שמרתה ולא רצתה להמסר ביד האויב **קותה לטוב,** שהיה לה תקוה שיבואו חיל מירושלים לעזור לה ולכן מרתה ולא פתחה החומה, ועתה מפרש ומה היה הסבה שהמספד לקח עמידתו אצלם ולא נושעו, **כי ירד רע מאת ה' לשער ירושלים** שע"י השגחת ה' ירד רע לירושלים ולא יכלו לשלוח חיל לעזור לבני צאנן:


**For she yearned.** He explains why the inhabitants of Zaanan did not come out and upon whom they relied. For **the inhabitants of Maroth**—meaning Zaanan, which rebelled and refused to surrender to the enemy—**hoped for good**, expecting troops to come from Jerusalem to help them. Therefore, they rebelled and did not open their gates. He now explains why lamentation took its place among them and they were not delivered: **For evil came down from Hashem to the gate of Jerusalem**—through Hashem’s Providence, calamity descended upon Jerusalem, and they could not send troops to help the people of Zaanan.



### Verse 13

###### Malbim on Micah 1:13:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:13:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/1:13:1)


**רתום המרכבה לרכש יושבת לכיש,** בלכיש התחילו לעבוד ע"ז בתחלה, שכן תמצא שאמציה מלך יהודה שהתחיל לעבוד את אלהי שעיר ומרדו בו עבדיו נס לכישה ומלך שם י"ב שנה, ועוזיה מלך בירושלים, (כמו שבארתי זה מ"ב סי' י"ד), מבואר שבירושלים קנאו כהני ה' על שעבד ע"ז ובלכיש החזיקו בידו ומלך שם י"ב שנה עד שהתגברו יושבי ירושלים וימיתוהו, ושם כתוב וישאו אותו על הסוסים ויקבר בירושלים, מ"ש שנשאוהו על הסוסים ר"ל מפני שעובדי ע"ז (שראש עבודתם היה אל השמש שחשבוה למולכת השמים) היו מיחדים אל עבודתה סוסים ומרכבה, שבעת צאת השמש היו הולכים לקראתה לצד מזרח במרכבה וסוסים, דוגמת שציירו כן הליכת השמש על מעגלה, כנודע בקורות ימי קדם, וכמ"ש (מ"ב כ"ג י"א) וישבת את הסוסים אשר נתנו מלכי יהודה לשמש ואת מרכבות השמש שרף באש, ואמציה היה לו בלכיש סוסים מיוחדים אל עבודת השמש, לכן כאשר גברו יד עבדי ה' וימיתוהו נשאו את גוייתו על הסוסים של השמש לחרפה ובוז, והיה דרכם לעשות את מרכבות השמש מעצי רתמים השומרים חום האש בתוכם ימים רבים (כמ"ש חז"ל בפ' הספינה) לציין בו את חום השמש ואשה המתמיד, כי עבודת השמש היה באש כנודע, ועל זה אמר **רתום המרכבה** שתחפה את המרכבה הידועה שהיא מרכבת השמש בעצי רותם, אבל **לרכש,** אל תאסור אותה אל הסוסים המיוחדים אל השמש, רק תאסור לרכש, שהוא קל ברגליו לברוח, שיברחו מפני האויב, על זה אמר **ראשית חטאת היא לבת ציון** הוא המקום הראשון לבת ציון היינו למלכות יהודה ששם התחילו בחטאת זה לעבוד את השמש.


**Harness the chariot to the swift steed, O inhabitant of Lachish.** Idolatry first took root in Lachish. Thus we find that Amaziah king of Judah, who began worshiping the gods of Seir and whose servants rebelled against him, fled to Lachish and reigned there for twelve years, while Uzziah reigned in Jerusalem (as I explained in II Kings, chapter 14). Evidently, the priests of Hashem in Jerusalem opposed his idolatry zealously, whereas the people of Lachish supported him, and he reigned there for twelve years until the inhabitants of Jerusalem prevailed and killed him. There it says, “They carried him on the horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem.” The statement that they carried him on the horses means the following: Idol worshipers, whose principal worship was directed toward the sun, which they regarded as the queen of heaven, dedicated horses and a chariot to its worship. At sunrise, they would ride eastward to meet it with a chariot and horses, corresponding to their depiction of the sun’s journey along its circuit, as is known from ancient histories. Thus it says, (II Kings 23:11) “He abolished the horses that the kings of Judah had dedicated to the sun, and burned the chariots of the sun with fire.” Amaziah had horses in Lachish dedicated to sun worship. Therefore, when the servants of Hashem prevailed and killed him, they carried his corpse upon the horses of the sun as an act of disgrace and contempt. It was their custom to make the sun’s chariots from broom wood, which retains the heat of a fire for many days (as our Sages stated in the chapter *HaSefinah*), symbolizing the sun’s heat and its enduring fire, for sun worship involved fire, as is known. Accordingly, he says, **Cover the chariot with broom wood**—cover the familiar chariot, the chariot of the sun, with broom wood. But **to the swift steed**—do not harness it to the horses dedicated to the sun; instead, harness it to a swift steed, fleet-footed for escape, so that they may flee from the enemy. Concerning this he says, **She was the beginning of sin for the daughter of Zion**—this was the first place belonging to the daughter of Zion, meaning the kingdom of Judah, where they began committing this sin of sun worship.

**Therefore you shall give farewell gifts for Moresheth-gath.** Gath had originally belonged to Israel from the days of David, who took Metheg-ammah from the Philistines, meaning the city of Gath (II Samuel 8:1). Rehoboam built it into a fortified city (II Chronicles 11), but afterward the Philistines took it from Israel. Uzziah king of Judah then conquered it from the Philistines and breached its walls (II Chronicles 26:6). In Rehoboam’s days, he also fortified Mareshah near Gath (II Chronicles 11:8). Once Gath’s wall had been breached, Mareshah protected Gath against recapture by the Philistines. It was called Moresheth-gath because it served as Gath’s defense. When the Philistines raided the cities of the lowlands in the days of Ahaz and sought to conquer Moresheth-gath so that they could take Gath, Pekah son of Remaliah was also waging war against Judah. The people of Lachish then reached an agreement with him to give him the city of Achzib, which was near Mareshah (as stated in Joshua 15:44), so that they could retain Mareshah, which protected Gath, and prevent Gath from falling into Philistine hands. Thus he says, **Therefore you shall give gifts**—meaning that you will give a present **for**, and on behalf of, **Moresheth-gath**. You will give as gifts **the houses of Achzib to a deceiver**—that is, to a deceitful man. He then explains who the deceiver is: **to the kings of Israel**. You gave the houses of Achzib as a present to the kings of Israel, who are false to their word, in order to save Moresheth-gath.

**Do not preach.** He begins to dispute with them over their refusal to listen to the true prophets. The sense of the verse is: **Is it said, O house of Jacob, “Do not preach what they preach”?** This was a common saying among the house of Jacob: They would tell the prophets, **Do not preach** the things **that they preach**—do not utter prophecies of rebuke or predictions of calamity, which the prophets customarily proclaim and prophesy, for we do not wish to hear such prophecies. Secondly, they say, **They shall not preach to these; disgrace shall not depart.** The sense is: **They shall not proclaim a disgrace that will not depart.** At the very least, they should not preach rebukes concerning sins, thereby publicizing the people’s disgrace and evil deeds, for these humiliations and reproaches will never recede; rather, they will remain forever as a source of shame, as in, “His reproach shall not be blotted out.” They should not utter prophecies that will subject the people to everlasting humiliation.

Or, **Are these His deeds?** If you say that this is not because He becomes emotionally aroused to anger against those who transgress His will, as though He cannot remain patient, they ask: Are these truly His deeds—to inflict evil upon His creatures? Surely the deeds of Hashem are goodness, kindness, mercy, and grace, not the decreeing of evil and calamity. All this the house of Jacob says to the prophets. He answers them: **Do not My words benefit one who walks uprightly?** Why do you complain that My words are evil and announce calamity? For one who walks along the upright path, My words bring good and are not evil; rather, he rejoices in them, and they are words of consolation to him. The words of prophecy testify that Hashem watches over human conduct and repays each person according to his deeds. One who walks uprightly rejoices in this, for it allows him to hope in Hashem for righteousness and kindness in accordance with his conduct, as in, “It is a joy to the righteous when justice is done.”

**Then.** Since they commit such evil deeds, if **then**, at the time of calamity, **they cry out to Hashem and He does not answer them**, and **He hides His face from them at that time**, withdrawing His Providence and abandoning them to chance, is this injustice on His part? Surely, justice requires it, **because they have made their deeds evil.** How, then, can you ask, “Are these His deeds?” In accordance with the evil of their own deeds, He hides His face from them, and calamity comes upon them of itself, as determined by their conduct and the condition they have brought upon themselves.

This means that calamity will overtake them before its appointed time.

Therefore, since **its leaders**, who are appointed to administer justice between one person and another, **judge for bribes; its priests**, who are appointed to issue religious rulings, **teach for a price**—declaring impure or pure, forbidden or permitted, according to the payment they receive—and **its prophets** do not prophesy but rather **practice divination for money given to them**, how can **they rely upon Hashem, saying, “Is not Hashem in our midst?”** How can they expect Him to protect them so that no calamity will come upon them?

Concerning Judah He says, **And her whom I have afflicted**—meaning the tribe of Judah, whom I afflicted, whether during the Second Temple period through the Greeks and afterward through the Romans, or throughout the duration of their exile.

**But they do not know the thoughts of Hashem.** Hashem’s counsel is the intended purpose of His action, whereas His thoughts are the means He employs to prepare for that counsel, which is the ultimate purpose. The prophet explains that every gathering of the nations against Zion forms part of Hashem’s thoughts—that is, these gatherings serve as means through which His counsel, His intended purpose, will be accomplished. All their gatherings will be preparations and means leading to the gathering in the latter days, which itself will be a preparation through which He will accomplish His counsel. There He will judge them for their deeds, and there they will recognize the true religion. Through the signs and wonders they witness at that time, the survivors will return to the true faith, while the wicked will perish like chaff swept from the threshing floor. Thus he says, **They do not know the thoughts of Hashem, nor do they understand His counsel**—that all these things are means through which His counsel will be accomplished, namely, that **He has gathered them like sheaves to the threshing floor.** Just as sheaves are gathered to the threshing floor to be threshed, so that the chaff and husk may be removed and the pure grain left behind, so will He thresh them in the war of Gog and Magog. He will remove the chaff—the wicked who acted evilly against the holy—and refine the grain, from among whom righteous converts and those who recognize the true religion will emerge.

**But you, Bethlehem.** It has already been explained in Ezekiel, chapter 37, that at the time of the end, the ten tribes will first awaken, with the Messiah son of Joseph as their king, and they will achieve great strength. Afterward, the Messiah son of David will arise through the might of Hashem, and they will all accept the kingdom of the house of David. Wars will then cease, for nations will seek him, and his resting place will be glorious. Our visionary prophet explains these matters to us, saying: **But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah**, from which David, son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, emerged and the kingdom of the house of David arose, are **too young to be among the thousands of Judah; from you shall come forth for Me one who is to rule Israel.** That is, at present you are still too young and insignificant, even together with the thousands of Judah who accompany you, to attain the stature required for the man designated for Me to come forth from you and rule Israel. You are still too small and weak, even with the thousands of Judah beside you, to prevail in this manner—until you exercise great dominion and the Messiah who emerges from you, the Messiah son of David, gains the strength to rule Israel, **whose origins are from ancient times, from days of old.** That is, he will attain a powerful kingdom corresponding to his ancient origins in the days of David and Solomon, who ruled a great and mighty kingdom. For this, you are still too young, weak, and lacking in strength, even together with the thousands of Judah who are with you.

(4–5) **And he shall be**, etc. This means that he will now become great to the ends of the earth, and this will be achieved through peace. The greatness of the Messiah will extend to the ends of the earth, not through wars but through the power of peace. This itself will constitute his greatness: he will have no need for war, for all the peoples will accept his Torah, his religion, and his sovereignty over them. The sense of the verses is: **And this shall be peace, and it shall deliver us from Assyria when he comes into our land.** Peace will then save us from Assyria, so that we will not need to fight against him as in the past; for the peace prevailing at that time will save us from Assyria, since he will submit to us and seek a covenant of peace. The passage from **Assyria, when he comes into our land**, etc., through **and the land of Nimrod at its entrances**, is parenthetical. That is, until now, before the Messiah son of David arose, while the Messiah son of Joseph reigned, we required warfare throughout the wars of Gog and Magog. **If Assyria came into our land** during that period, or even **when he trod within our borders**, we had to **raise against him seven shepherds** to fight him. **They shepherded the land of Assyria with the sword**, for sword and warfare were necessary; and they also **shepherded the land of Nimrod**—the country of Babylonia, where Nimrod was the first king—**at its entrances**, for they had to advance as far as the entrance to their land and subdue and break them there through warfare and siege. But now it will no longer be so. Rather, **this shall be peace, and it shall deliver**—peace will deliver us **from Assyria when he comes into our land.** Regarding the seven shepherds and eight princes, the commentators have offered many interpretations. It may also be said that this alludes to the earlier period: From the time the enemy began to cut Israel down—which occurred in the days of Jehoahaz son of Jehu (II Kings 13:7)—until the king of Assyria came and exiled them in the days of Hoshea son of Elah, there were eight princes, meaning eight kings of the ten tribes: Jehoahaz; his son Joash; his son Jeroboam; his son Zechariah; Menahem; Pekahiah; Pekah; and Hoshea—eight in all. (Shallum was killed immediately and is therefore not included in the count.) Afterward there were seven shepherds from the house of Judah: Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amon, Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jeconiah, and Zedekiah. It may also be interpreted as referring to the future. According to Ezekiel’s prophecies concerning the wars of Gog and Magog in chapters 32 and 38, the war prophesied in chapter 32—in which Assyria will come against the Land of Israel to wage war with Edom, as I explained there—will involve seven shepherds: Egypt, Elam, Meshech, Tubal, Edom, the princes of the north, and the Sidonians. The war prophesied in chapter 38 will involve eight princes: Gog, prince of the land of Magog; Meshech; Tubal; Persia; Cush; Put; Gomer; and the house of Togarmah. I explained there that on one occasion he will come into the land but will not enter Jerusalem; concerning this he says, “When he comes into our land.” On another occasion he will come as far as Jerusalem; concerning this he says, “When he treads within our palaces.”

**And the remnant of Jacob shall be.** With regard to their strength against their oppressors—whereas until now Israel was like a scattered sheep among lions, as it says, “Israel is a scattered sheep; lions have driven him away”—they will now be like a lion and a young lion. There is a distinction between “nations” and “peoples”: nations are of lower standing than peoples and have no king, whereas peoples are distinguished and possess a king. Conversely, “nation” denotes a larger collective than “people.” The lion is mature, while the young lion is youthful. A lion customarily goes out into the forest in search of prey and tears apart large animals, whereas a young lion lies in ambush in its lair, does not go out in search of prey, and tears apart only small animals. He therefore depicts Israel as being **among the nations**—the larger but less distinguished collectives—**like a lion among the beasts of the forest.** Although the nations resemble large wild animals, Israel will be like a great lion in comparison with them, going out to the lands of the nations to wage war against them and seize prey among them. But **among many peoples**, which are smaller than nations but more distinguished, Israel will be **like a young lion among flocks of sheep.** Just as a young lion does not go out in search of prey, Israel will not go forth to fight them in their own lands, for most of them will accept the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven. Only those who come to Israel’s dwelling place to wage war against them in the Land of Israel—such as the peoples who will march against Mount Zion in the army of Gog and Magog—will be torn apart like prey seized by a young lion in its lair, while they will be as weak before Israel as flocks of sheep. He explains: **Which, if it passes through, tramples**—the mature lion passes through the forest outside its lair and tramples, whereas the young lion **tears its prey** within its lair, **and none can deliver** from their grasp.

**שמעו נא,** אחר שאמר (למעלה ג' א') שמעו נא ראשי בית יעקב, שרצה להתוכח עם ישראל לפניהם והם יהיו השופטים בדבר, כמ"ש הלא לכם לדעת את המשפט, והם לא רצו לשמוע, יאמר להם שה' בחר תחתיהם שופטים אחרים שישמעו טענתו וריבו ווכוחו, שהם ההרים והגבעות בפניהם יתוכח, וה' אמר להנביא **קום ריב את ההרים,** ר"ל שיריב ריב ה' בפניהם, **ותשמענה הגבעות קולך** הגם שראשי בית יעקב אינם רוצים לשמוע, הגבעות הם ישמעו קול הנביא:


**Hear now.** After saying above (3:1), “Hear now, O heads of the house of Jacob,” because he wished to present his dispute with Israel before them so that they might serve as judges in the matter—as he said, “Is it not for you to know justice?”—they refused to listen. He therefore tells them that Hashem has chosen other judges in their place to hear His claim, dispute, and argument: the mountains and hills, before whom He will present His case. Hashem said to the prophet, **Arise, contend before the mountains**—that is, present Hashem’s case before them—**and let the hills hear your voice.** Although the leaders of the house of Jacob refuse to listen, the hills will hear the prophet’s voice.

### Verse 5

###### Malbim on Micah 6:5:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/6:5:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/6:5:1)


(ה-ח) **עמי,** עתה מפרש מה הלאיתיך, **זכר נא מה יעץ בלק מלך מואב,** שרצה שבלעם יקטרג על ישראל ויקללם, **ומה ענה אותו בלעם** שטוב בעיני ה' לברך את ישראל מפני שההשגחה דבוקה בם לטובה, ועי"ז יעץ בלעם לבלק להכשילם בבנות מואב שעי"ז תסור השגחת ה' מהם, ומזה תראה כי לא הלאיתיך, ומביא ע"ז מה שנעשה אז **מן השטים ועד הגלגל,** שיעור הכתובים וסדרם, **מן השטים ועד הגלגל למען דעת צדקות ה' הגיד לך אדם מה טוב** וכו', ושני הפסוקים במה אקדם ה' וכו' הירצה ה' באלפי אילים וכו' באו כמאמר מוסגר כמו שיבואר, וענינו, שאחרי שחטאו ישראל בשטים ע"י עצת בלעם, נחמו על מעשיהם, ושאלו במה יתרצה ה' לכפר פשע ולהתם חטאת, וחשבו כי יצטרכו לפייסו בקרבנות גדולות עד שיקריבו את בניהם ופרי בטנם לכפר חטאתם, ואז **מן השטים ועד הגלגל הגיד לך אדם,** והודיע להם תשובה על שאלתם **מה טוב,** שאין ה' רוצה בעבודות קשות **כי אם עשות משפט** וכו', ר"ל כי אז הואיל משה באר את התורה וכתב להם את משנה התורה והגיד להם מה טוב ומה ה' דורש מאתם שאינו דורש עבודות קשות וקרבנות וזבחים, (כי בספר משנה תורה לא האריך בדיני הקרבנות) ואינו רוצה רק שיעשו משפט וחסד, וזה היה מן השטים ועד הגלגל, כי בשטים החל משה לבאר להם את משנה התורה ואחרי מות משה עד בואם לגלגל שנעשו להם נסי הירדן וראו ששב שכינת ה' להיות אתם, דבר ה' אל יהושע וצוהו על משמרת התורה ושמירת מצותיה ולא הלאה אותם בשום עבודה קשה, עד שבאו לגלגל ששם צוה שימולו שנית ושיעשו את הפסח, שהיה עבודה קשה, אבל עד שבאו לגלגל לא בקש מהם שום דבר עבודה, וז"ש **מן השטים ועד הגלגל הגיד לך אדם מה טוב** ושה' אינו דורש מעמך שום דבר עבודה קשה כי אם עשות משפט וכו', **למען דעת צדקות ה'** ובזה תדע צדקות ה' שאינו מכביד על האדם עבודות קשות להלאותו:


(5–8) **My people.** He now explains, “How have I wearied you?” **Remember, please, what Balak king of Moab devised**—he wanted Balaam to denounce Israel and curse them—**and what Balaam answered him**: that it was good in the eyes of Hashem to bless Israel, because Divine Providence was attached to them for their benefit. Balaam therefore advised Balak to cause them to stumble through the daughters of Moab, so that Hashem’s Providence would depart from them. From this you can see that I did not weary you. As evidence, he cites what occurred at that time **from Shittim to Gilgal.** The proper sense and sequence of the verses is: **From Shittim to Gilgal, in order that you may know the righteous acts of Hashem: He has told you, O man, what is good**, etc. The two verses, “With what shall I approach Hashem?” etc., and “Will Hashem be pleased with thousands of rams?” etc., form a parenthetical statement, as will be explained. The meaning is that after Israel sinned at Shittim through Balaam’s counsel, they regretted their deeds and asked what would appease Hashem, atone for transgression, and bring sin to an end. They imagined that they would need to appease Him with enormous sacrifices, even offering their children and the fruit of their bodies to atone for their sins. Then, **from Shittim to Gilgal, He told you, O man**, answering their question by revealing **what is good**: Hashem does not desire burdensome forms of service, **but only to practice justice**, etc. That is, Moses then undertook to explain the Torah and wrote Deuteronomy for them, telling them what is good and what Hashem requires of them: He does not demand burdensome service, sacrifices, and offerings. Indeed, Deuteronomy does not elaborate upon the laws of the sacrifices. He desires only that they practice justice and kindness. This occurred from Shittim to Gilgal: At Shittim, Moses began explaining Deuteronomy to them; after Moses’ death, until their arrival at Gilgal, the miracle of the Jordan was performed for them and they saw that Hashem’s Divine Presence had returned to dwell among them. Hashem spoke to Joshua and commanded him to guard the Torah and observe its commandments, without burdening them with any difficult service, until they reached Gilgal, where He commanded them to circumcise themselves again and to offer the Passover sacrifice, which was a demanding service. But until they arrived at Gilgal, He required no such service from them. Thus he says, **From Shittim to Gilgal, He told you, O man, what is good**—that Hashem requires no burdensome service from you, but only that you practice justice, etc.—**so that you may know the righteous acts of Hashem.** Through this, you will recognize Hashem’s righteousness: He does not burden a person with difficult forms of service intended to weary him.

**(במה אקדם ה',** מספר במאמר מוסגר, מה שאמרו ישראל אז אחרי שנכשלו בשטים בבעל פעור, הם שאלו, **במה אקדם ה'** באיזה מנחה ונדבה אקדמה פניו מצד שהוא ה' מהוה כל המציאות, עוד שאלו **(במה) אכף לאלהי מרום** אחר שחטאתי לפניו ואצטרך לכפוף קומתי ולהכנע ולבקש סליחה, במה אכף לפניו מצד שהוא אלהי מרום, שמצד דינו הקשה נקרא בשם אלהים, והוא אלהי מרום, והחוטא לפניו ראוי לעונש גדול, ומפרש נגד מ"ש במה אקדם ה' **האקדמנו בעולות** במה שאביא אליו **עגלים בני שנה** לעולה:


**With what shall I approach Hashem?** In a parenthetical passage, he relates what Israel said after they stumbled at Shittim through the worship of Baal Peor. They asked, **With what shall I approach Hashem?** With what offering or gift shall I come before Him, inasmuch as He is Hashem, Who brings all existence into being? They further asked, **With what shall I bow before the God Most High?** Since I have sinned before Him, I must bend myself low, submit, and seek forgiveness. With what shall I bow before Him, inasmuch as He is the God Most High? On account of His strict judgment, He is called “God,” and He is the God Most High; one who sins before Him therefore deserves severe punishment. Corresponding to their question, “With what shall I approach Hashem?” he explains: **Shall I approach Him with burnt offerings**, by bringing Him **year-old calves** as burnt offerings?

**הירצה ה',** הלא לא ירצה **גם באלפי אילים** שטובים מעגלים, וגם **ברבבות נחלי שמן** שאביא לנסכי העולות?, ועתה מפרש נגד מ"ש במה אכף לאלהי מרום, **האתן בכורי פשעי,** הלא ראוי שבעד פשעי שהוא המרד במזיד אתן בני הבכור לקרבן, ובעד **חטאת נפשי** שהוא השוגג אתן **פרי בטני** שהם בנים הקטנים, כן שאלתם אז):


**Will Hashem be pleased?** Surely He will not be pleased even **with thousands of rams**, which are superior to calves, or **with tens of thousands of rivers of oil** that I might bring for the libations accompanying the burnt offerings. He now explains their corresponding question, “With what shall I bow before the God Most High?” **Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression?** It would seem that for my transgression—deliberate rebellion—I should offer my firstborn son as a sacrifice; and for **the sin of my soul**—an inadvertent sin—I should offer **the fruit of my body**, meaning my younger children. This is what they asked at that time.



### Verse 8

###### Malbim on Micah 6:8:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/6:8:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/6:8:1)


**הגיד** חוזר למעלה שעל שאלתכם זאת, הגיד לך מן השטים ועד הגלגל, מה טוב ובמה תתרצה אל ה', **הגיד** ר"ל שהגיד לך, א] **מה שטוב לך** מצד שאתה **אדם,** במצות שבין אדם לחברו, מה שטוב לפני האדם עצמו בחיים המדיניים, וגם הגיד **מה ה' דורש ממך** במצות שבין אדם למקום, ומפרש נגד הגיד לך אדם מה טוב **כי אם עשות משפט ואהבת חסד,** שזה הטוב לך מצד שאתה אדם שהמשפט ישמור החיים המדיניים מן הגזל והעשק, ועשיית החסד בזה יעזור איש לאחיו, ונגד מה ה' דורש ממך אמר **והצנע לכת עם אלהיך,** מה שאתה צריך ללכת עם אלהיך (כמו את האלהים התהלך נח), שזה שורש המצות שבין אדם למקום שילך עם אלהים, ללכת בדרכיו ולהתקדש בקדושתו, כמ"ש והייתם קדושים כי קדוש אני, זה יהיה **בהצנע** ובסתר, כי הם דברים שבינך ובין קונך ובזה טובה הצניעות בל יזיד וירים לבו בעניני החקירה באלהות וכן בל יתחכם על מצותיו, רק ילך בהצנע לכת עם אלהים:


**He has told.** This returns to the preceding passage: In response to your question, He told you, from Shittim to Gilgal, what is good and how you may find favor with Hashem. **He has told** means that He told you: 1] **What is good for you** insofar as you are **a human being**—the commandments governing relations between one person and another, which are beneficial to human beings themselves in civil society. He also told you **what Hashem requires of you**—the commandments governing the relationship between man and God. Corresponding to “He has told you, O man, what is good,” he says, **only to practice justice and to love kindness.** These are good for you insofar as you are human: justice protects civil society from robbery and oppression, while through acts of kindness one person assists another. Corresponding to “What does Hashem require of you?” he says, **and to walk humbly with your God.** You must walk with your God—as in, “Noah walked with God”—and this is the foundation of the commandments between man and God: to walk with God, follow His ways, and sanctify oneself with His holiness, as it says, “You shall be holy, for I am holy.” This must be done **humbly** and in private, for these are matters between you and your Creator. Modesty is proper here, so that a person does not act presumptuously or become prideful in investigating matters concerning Divinity, nor presume to be wiser than His commandments; rather, he should walk humbly with God.



### Verse 9

###### Malbim on Micah 6:9:1

**קול ה'** שיעור הכתוב **קול ה' ותושיה יקרא לעיר יראה שמך,** וכונתו עמ"ש ויקרא אברהם שם המקום ההוא ה' יראה, שתחלה היתה נקראת בשם שלם, כמ"ש ומלכי צדק מלך שלם, ואברהם הוסיף לה שם יראה, ע"ש שה' יראה וישגיח תמיד על העיר הזאת, ועתה יזכיר הנביא את ירושלים, שתזכור ששמה **יראה** כי עיני ה' בה תמיד, ותשמור את עצמה מלמרות עיני כבודו, וז"ש **קול ה'** וגם **תושיה** שהיא התורה, הם **קראו לעיר** הזאת **ששמה יראה,** כי ה' הסכים על השם הזה וכתבו בתורתו וא"כ **שמעו מטה ומי יעדה,** אחר שה' משגיח על מעשיכם שמעו את המטה שהוא העונש המיועד על עונותיכם ומי יעד את העונש הלא הוא ה' אשר לו היכולת להעניש, ר"ל כי מה שתקשו ערפכם יהיה או מצד שתחשבו שאין ה' רואה מעשיכם, על זה אמר **יראה שמך,** או שתחשבו שאין רצונו להעניש על זה אמר **שמעו מטה,** או שתחשבו שאין ביכלתו להעניש, על זה אמר **מי יעדה**:


**The voice of Hashem.** The sense of the verse is: **The voice of Hashem and sound wisdom call to the city whose name is Yir’eh.** He refers to the verse, “And Abraham called the name of that place ‘Hashem Will See.’” The city was originally called Shalem, as it says, “Melchizedek, king of Shalem,” and Abraham added the name Yir’eh, because Hashem would always see and exercise Providence over this city. The prophet now addresses Jerusalem, telling it to remember that its name is **Yir’eh**, for the eyes of Hashem are always upon it, and therefore it must guard itself against rebelling before His glorious eyes. Thus, **the voice of Hashem**, together with **sound wisdom**—meaning the Torah—**called this city** by **the name Yir’eh**, for Hashem approved this name and recorded it in His Torah. If so, **hear the rod and Who has appointed it.** Since Hashem watches over your deeds, heed the rod—the punishment appointed for your sins—and consider Who appointed that punishment: surely it is Hashem, Who possesses the power to punish. That is, your stubbornness may arise from one of three thoughts: You may think that Hashem does not see your deeds; against this he says, **Your name is Yir’eh.** You may think that He does not intend to punish; against this he says, **Hear the rod.** Or you may think that He lacks the power to punish; against this he says, **Who has appointed it.**

**האזכה** וע"י שקבעו איפה קטנה יעשו גם השקל מזויף, שלפי שהקטינו את האיפה יקטינו הקונים את השקל, ובכ"ז לא יזכו בזה, כי ע"ז אמר **אבן ואבן איפה ואיפה תועבת ה' גם שניהם,** ר"ל אף שנמצאו שניהם יחד, שע"י שיש אבן ואבן יעשה המוכר איפה ואיפה שמי שנותן לו שקל קטן מזויף נותן לו לעומתו איפה קטנה, ובכ"ז היא תועבה בעיני ה' הגם שנמצאו שניהם, לא יזכה בזה לאמר שהקטין השקל בעבור שכנגדו הקטין האיפה או בהפך, כי ה' הזהיר על אבן ואבן ועל איפה ואיפה בכל אופן, וז"ש הכי **אזכה** עי"כ **במאזני רשע,** ובמה שיש **בכיס אבני מרמה** להקטין גם השקל ע"י שהקטינו האיפה, אחר שה' שונא גם שניהם:


**Shall I be innocent?** Because they established a smaller ephah, they would also falsify the shekel: since the sellers reduced the ephah, the buyers would reduce the shekel. Nevertheless, they would not thereby be innocent, for concerning this it says, “Differing weights and differing measures—Hashem abhors them both.” This means that even when both are found together—when, because there are differing weights, the seller also uses differing measures, giving a reduced ephah to one who pays him with a small, falsified shekel—it nevertheless remains an abomination in the eyes of Hashem. The presence of both does not justify him in saying that he reduced the shekel because the other party reduced the ephah, or vice versa, for Hashem prohibited differing weights and differing measures under all circumstances. Thus he says: Shall I thereby **be innocent with wicked scales** and by having **deceitful weights in the bag**, reducing the shekel because the ephah was reduced, when Hashem abhors them both?

## Chapter 7


### Verse 1

###### Malbim on Micah 7:1:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/7:1:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/7:1:1)


**אללי לי,** הנביא מקונן על שפלת הדור, שכל החסידים והישרים ספו תמו מן הארץ ולא נשאר רק הוא לבדו אשר עודו רד עם אל, ומדמה את הדור שלפניו ככרם של גפנים או של תאנים, שכבר עבר זמנם, והוא דומה בין התאנים **כאספי קיץ** שכבר נלקחו התאנים הבכורות ונשאר הוא לבדו כתאנה שנשארה בסוף הקיץ, ודומה בין הענבים **כעוללות** שנשארו אחרי **הבציר,** ונגד עוללות בציר אמר **אין אשכול לאכול,** ונגד אספי קיץ אמר **(אין) בכורה** (לאכול) אשר **אותה נפשי,** לאכול אשכול או בכורה ואין:


**Woe is me.** The prophet laments the low state of the generation: all the pious and upright have vanished and disappeared from the land, and he alone remains faithful to God. He compares the generation before him to a vineyard of grapes or a grove of figs whose season has already passed. Among the figs, he is **like one who gathers the summer fruit** after the first-ripe figs have already been taken, while he alone remains like a fig left at the end of summer. Among the grapes, he is like **the gleanings** remaining after **the vintage.** Corresponding to the gleanings of the vintage, he says, **There is no cluster to eat**; and corresponding to the gathering of summer fruit, he says, **There is no first-ripe fig** to eat, **which my soul desires.** He longs to eat a cluster or a first-ripe fig, but there is none.



### Verse 2

###### Malbim on Micah 7:2:1

**אבד** מפרש נגד אין אשכול לאכול, **אבד חסיד מן הארץ** שהחסיד שעשה כל מעשהו יותר על החיוב בין במצות שבין אדם למקום בין במצות שבין אדם לחברו וזה נדמה כאשכול המלא ענבים כן היה מלא פרי צדקה ומשפט, וכמ"ש חז"ל אשכול איש שהכל בו, זה נאבד מן הארץ, ונגד בכורה אותה נפשי אמר **וישר באדם אין** שיהיה עכ"פ איש העושה עפ"י היושר במצות שבין אדם לחברו, ועל זה אמר באדם, ר"ל במצות הנוהגות מאדם לאדם ג"כ אין, כי בהפך **כולם לדמים יארבו,** לשפוך דם, **ואיש יצוד את אחיהו בחרם** ופח יקוש ללכוד אותו:


**Has perished.** He explains the corresponding phrase, “There is no cluster to eat”: **The pious man has perished from the land.** The pious person does more than he is strictly obligated to do, both in the commandments between man and God and those between one person and another. He resembles a cluster filled with grapes, for he is filled with the fruits of righteousness and justice. Thus our Sages interpreted *eshkol* as “a man in whom everything is found.” Such a person has perished from the land. Corresponding to “the first-ripe fig that my soul desires,” he says, **There is no upright person among men**—not even a person who at least acts uprightly in matters between one person and another. Therefore, he says “among men,” referring to commandments governing relations between people. Even such a person does not exist. On the contrary, **they all lie in wait for blood**, seeking to shed blood, **and each hunts his brother with a net**, setting a snare and trap to capture him.



### Verse 3

###### Malbim on Micah 7:3:1

**על הרע,** סדר הכתוב, **על הרע כפים השר שואל והשופט להיטיב בשלום,** ר"ל הגם שתראה שהשר והשופט שואל על הרע כפים, דהיינו ששואל וחוקר ודורש על הרע שנעשה בכפים, בל תחשוב ששואל ע"ז כדי לעשות משפט ולהעניש את עושה הרע, רק שואל כדי להיטיב את הרע בשילום, ר"ל ע"י שישלם לו שוחד וע"י התשלומין שיקבל מאת עושה רע ייטיב עם עושה הרע ולא יענישהו, והנה השר היה ממונה ליסר עושה עול, והשופט היה ממונה לעיין בדינו כפי המשפט, ושניהם השר והשופט קבלו תשלומין, והיטיבו עם עושה הרעה, ועוד היה עליהם הגדול שהוא הראש על כולם והמופלא שבסנהדרין וכשלא מצאו משפט אצל השר והשופט הלכו אל הגדול **והגדול דובר הות נפשו הוא (להיטיב בשלום)** ר"ל שהגדול רוצה ג"כ לקחת שוחד, לכן הוא דובר שהרע הזה הוא הות נפשו של עושה הרע. ר"ל שראוי להמית אותו ושתבא הוות ושבר על נפשו, אבל רק דובר כן, ור"ל השר והשופט שואל, והגדול דובר הוות נפשו הוא, וכ"ז להיטיב בשלום, בין שאלת ודרישת השר והשופט ובין מה שהגדול דובר ומחליט שהרע כפים ראוי להוות ושבר, כ"ז רק כדי שישלמו להם שוחד שע"י ייטיבו את הרע, ובזה **ויעבתוה,** שאז יסכימו השלשה שהם השר והשופט והגדול להיטיב את הרע, ויעשו עבות שאין עבות פחות משלשה כמ"ש חז"ל, ר"ל אז יעשו פסק דין בכל תוקף בחוט המשולש אשר לא ינתק לאשר הדבר בחוזק שאין מגיע עונש לעושה הרע:


**Concerning the evil.** The order of the verse is: **Concerning the evil committed by the hands, the prince inquires and the judge investigates, in order to favor the evildoer in exchange for payment.** That is, although you see the prince and the judge asking about the evil committed by someone’s hands—questioning, investigating, and examining the wrongdoing—do not think that they do so to administer justice and punish the evildoer. Rather, they investigate in order to favor him in exchange for payment. By paying them a bribe, the evildoer induces them to treat him favorably and refrain from punishing him. The prince was charged with disciplining wrongdoers, while the judge was charged with examining the case according to law; yet both the prince and the judge accepted payment and favored the evildoer. Above them stood the great man, the head of them all and the preeminent member of the Sanhedrin. When people could obtain no justice from the prince or the judge, they went to this great man. **And the great man declares the ruin desired by his soul—in order to favor him in exchange for payment.** The great man also desires a bribe. He therefore declares that this evil deed warrants the ruin of the evildoer’s soul—that he deserves death and that destruction and calamity should befall him—but he merely says this. Thus, the prince and judge investigate, while the great man declares that the evildoer deserves destruction; yet all of this is done only **to favor him in exchange for payment.** Both the investigation by the prince and judge and the great man’s declaration that the evil committed deserves ruin and destruction are merely intended to extract bribes from the offender, after which they will favor him. **And they weave it together.** The three—the prince, the judge, and the great man—then agree to favor the evildoer and form a threefold cord, for, as our Sages said, a cord consists of no fewer than three strands. That is, they issue a forceful and binding judgment, like a threefold cord that cannot be broken, firmly confirming that the evildoer deserves no punishment.

**טובם כחדק ישר ממסוכה,** גם האיש הטוב הנמצא ביניהם אינו טוב בעצמו, רק הוא דומה במשלו כחדק שהוא ישר נגד המסוכה, שהמסוכה הוא הגדר והסכך שעושים מקוצים וחדק, והחדק בעצמו הוא מלא עקמומית, וכשעושים ממנו מסוכה מעקמים אותו יותר ויסתבך ויתפתל יותר עד שהחדק הוא ישר בערכו, וכן הטוב שבהם הוא ג"כ נפתל ועקש, רק שהוא ישר בערך בני דורו שהם עקומים כמסוכה, **יום מצפיך פקודתך באה** ביום פורעניות המוכן למצפיך, ר"ל על האומות שמצפים לראות ברעתך, ביום המוכן לפורעניותם, הלא ביום ההוא באה פקודתך, פקדו את עוניך ותקבל עליהם עונש, באופן שיום המוכן לפורעניות האומות שכני א"י נפקדו גם ישראל על עונם, עד **שעתה תהיה מבוכתם,** שהעם כולו שאמר עליהם כולם לדמים יארובו וכו' יהיו נבוכים ויהיה מבוכה ביניהם:


**The best of them is like a thorn; the most upright is worse than a thorn hedge.** Even the good person found among them is not inherently good. He resembles a thorn that appears straight in comparison with a thorn hedge. A thorn hedge is a fence or covering made from thorns and briars. The thorn itself is full of twists, but when it is fashioned into a hedge, it is bent still further and becomes even more tangled and twisted, so that by comparison the individual thorn appears straight. Likewise, even the best among them is crooked and perverse; he is considered upright only in comparison with the members of his generation, who are as twisted as a thorn hedge. **The day of your watchmen—your visitation—has come.** On the day of calamity prepared for those who watch you—that is, the nations waiting to behold your misfortune—on the very day appointed for their punishment, your own visitation has also come: your sins have been remembered, and you will be punished for them. Thus, on the day prepared for the punishment of the nations neighboring the Land of Israel, Israel too was called to account for its sins, so that **now their confusion shall come.** The entire people, of whom he said, “They all lie in wait for blood,” etc., will become bewildered, and confusion will prevail among them.

**ואני,** אומר הנביא עם שפלות הדור הזה והשחתתם ורוע מצבם ומעמדם המדיני והביתי אין תקוה עתה. רק אני בה' אצפה שהמצפה הוא לזמן רחוק, שאצפה על הבטחת ה' שעוד ישוב יגאלנו באחרית הימים, **אוחילה** ר"ל ובציפוי הזה שאני מצפה על הגאולה העתידה באחרית הימים, יש שני ענינים, או שייחל וימתין עד בוא העת המיועד וקץ הימין, שאז מוכרח שתבא הגאולה בכל אופן, וע"ז יפול לשון יחול, שהמיחל ממתין על דבר שיבא בברור, כמו שממתין על הבקר שיבא בודאי בכלות הלילה, ועל זה אמר **אוחילה לאלהי ישעי** שהוא על עת הישועה, או שיצוייר שתבא הגאולה גם לפני זמן המיועד אם ישובו בתשובה ויבקשו פניו ויהיה עת רצון, כמ"ש בעת רצון עניתיך וביום ישועה עזרתיך (ישעיהו מ״ט:ח׳) וכמש"פ שם, ועל זה אמר **ישמעני אלהי** ר"ל שישמע תפלתי בעת רצון גם לפני זמן המיוחל:


**But as for me.** The prophet says: In view of the degradation of this generation, its corruption, and the evil state of its public and domestic life, there is no hope at present. **But as for me, I will look to Hashem.** Looking denotes expectation directed toward a distant time: I will look to Hashem’s promise that He will yet return and redeem us in the latter days. **I will wait.** That is, within this expectation of the future redemption in the latter days, there are two possibilities. One is to hope and wait until the arrival of the appointed time and the end of days, when the redemption must come under all circumstances. The term “waiting” applies to awaiting something certain to come, like waiting for morning, which will surely arrive when the night ends. Concerning this he says, **I will wait for the God of my salvation**—for the appointed time of salvation. Alternatively, the redemption may come even before its appointed time if they repent, seek His presence, and an acceptable time arrives, as it says, “In an acceptable time I answered you, and on a day of salvation I helped you” (Isaiah 49:8), as I explained there. Concerning this he says, **My God will hear me**—He will hear my prayer at an acceptable time, even before the time for which I wait.

###### Malbim on Micah 7:20:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/7:20:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Micah/r/7:20:1)


**תתן,** אחר שחשב כל המדות בא אל מדת אמת ומדת נוצר חסד לאלפים, ואמר שמדת אמת הוא שתקיים הבטחתך, **תתן האמת** וההבטחה שהבטחת **ליעקב** שהבטיחו והיה זרעך כעפר הארץ ופרצת ימה וקדמה, שר"ל אחרי הגלות שתדבק לעפר נפשם אז יקום לעזרה למו ויפרצו בנחלה בלי מצרים ותקיים הבטחה זו מצד מדת אמת שלך, וגם **תתן החסד** שהבטחת **לאברהם** (שאל אברהם לא נתחייב עדיין מצד הבטחה קודמת ונקרא חסד, ואל יעקב שכבר נתחייב לקיים השבועה שנשבע לאברהם ויצחק נקרא אמת) עד שתשמור **אשר נשבעת לאבותינו** הגם שהשבועה היא **מימי קדם** תצור חסד לאלפים:


**You will grant.** After enumerating all the attributes, he turns to the attribute of truth and the attribute of preserving kindness for thousands of generations. He says that the attribute of truth means that You will fulfill Your promise: **You will grant the truth**—the promise You made **to Jacob**, when You told him, “Your offspring shall be like the dust of the earth, and you shall spread out westward and eastward.” This means that after the exile, when their souls cling to the dust, He will arise to help them, and they will spread through an inheritance without boundaries. You will fulfill this promise through Your attribute of truth. You will also **grant the kindness** that You promised **to Abraham.** In Abraham’s case, God had not yet become obligated by any previous promise, and therefore it is called “kindness.” In Jacob’s case, however, He was already obligated to fulfill the oath He had sworn to Abraham and Isaac, and therefore it is called “truth.” Thus, You will preserve **what You swore to our forefathers**; although the oath dates **from days of old**, You preserve kindness for thousands of generations.
