###### Malbim on Joel 1:8:1

**אלי כבתולה,** המליץ במליצתו מצייר כאילו הגפן והתאנה הם המיללים את הדברים הנאמרים (בפסוק ה' ו' ז') שהגפן יאמר להשכורים שיבכו כי גוי עלה על ארצו של הגפן, ושם את גפנו לשמה כ"ז ידבר עץ הגפן והתאנה שנשחתו, ומצייר אותם **כבתולה חגורת שק על בעל נעוריה** והוא דימוי מלציי נפלא מאד, שהארבה אכל והשחית את הענפים של הגפן והתאנה ולא נשאר מהם רק הגזע, והמנהג לחבוש שק על הגזע שהוסר הקליפה ממנו, כדי לחתלו ולחממו, כדי שיצמח בדים ופאורות אחרים וזה הוא הציור של חגורת שק, ובפי החכמים יקרא האילן שלא נקצץ מימיו בשם בתולה, כמ"ש (בפ"ק דנדה) ג' בתולות הן, בתולת שקמה כ"ז שלא נקצצה מימיה, ועפ"ז יצייר הגפן שלא נקצץ מימיו עד עתה בשם בתולה, כי עד עתה היתה בתולה, ויקרא הענפים שקצצו ממנה בשם בעל, כי הם למעלה ממנה, והבתולה הזאת עתה חגורת שק על הבעל שנקצץ והלך ממנה. ומצוה לה שתיליל היללה הנאמר למעלה כי גוי עלה על ארצי, ר"ל על הארץ שהיא גדלה עליה, והשחית את גפנה ותאנתה:


**Lament like a virgin:** In his figurative language, the prophet portrays the vine and the fig tree themselves as wailing the words spoken in verses 5–7. The vine tells the drunkards to weep because a nation has invaded the vine’s land and laid its vine waste. All this is spoken by the vine and the fig tree that were destroyed. He depicts them **as a virgin clothed in sackcloth for the husband of her youth.** This is an exceptionally striking poetic image. The locusts devoured and destroyed the branches of the vine and the fig tree, leaving only their trunks. It is customary to wrap sackcloth around a trunk from which the bark has been removed, to bind and warm it so that it will grow new shoots and branches. This is the image of being “clothed in sackcloth.” In the terminology of the Sages, a tree that has never been cut down is called a “virgin,” as stated in the first chapter of Niddah: “There are three virgins,” one of which is “a virgin sycamore”—a sycamore that has never been cut down. Accordingly, he portrays the vine, which had never previously been cut down, as a virgin, for until now it had remained untouched. He calls the branches that were cut from it its “husband,” because they rise above it. This virgin now wears sackcloth in mourning for her husband, who has been cut down and taken from her. She is commanded to utter the lament stated above: “For a nation has invaded my land”—that is, the soil upon which she grows—and has destroyed its vine and its fig tree.

Regarding produce of the ground, he adds, **“the harvest of the field has perished,”** to include every kind. Regarding trees, he adds the apple tree, the most important of the remaining kinds of trees, and includes them all in the words, **“all the trees of the field have dried up.”** People were accustomed to rejoice with fruit from the trees on joyous occasions. Concerning this, he says, **“for joy has withered away from mankind.”**

**חגרו,** מצוה אל הכהנים **שיחגרו** שק לתפלה, **ושיספדו** על הצרה, וזה אומר דרך כלל הכהנים, ועתה מפרש שהוא מדבר אל שתי כתות מן הכהנים, שכת א' יחגרו שק ויתפללו, וכת אחרת ילילו ויספדו כמתיאש באין תקוה, כי היו כת כהנים צדיקים שהיו משרתי ה' ועובדים ה' בכונתם, והיו כת כהנים שהיו רק משרתי מזבח לעשות קרבנותיו לא משרתי ה' כפי הכונה הרצויה, ועל זה אמר **הילילו משרתי מזבח** שאלה שהם רק משרתי מזבח ילילו כמתיאש כי תפלתם אינה רצויה, **באו לינו בשקים משרתי אלהי** אבל הצדיקים שהם משרתי ה' בצדקתם, הם ילינו בשקים להתפלל ולעשות תשובה להשיב את הגזירה, ומפרש מדוע יתעוררו הכהנים ע"ז יותר מכל העם, **כי נמנע מבית אלהיכם מנחה ונסך.** שע"י הארבה נתקלקלו והתליעו החטים והושחתו היינות שהיו מביאים מהם מנחות ונסכים:


**Gird yourselves:** He commands the priests **to gird themselves** with sackcloth for prayer and **to lament** over the calamity. This is addressed to the priests in general. He then explains that he is speaking to two groups among them: one group should gird themselves with sackcloth and pray, while the other should wail and lament like those who despair without hope. There were righteous priests who were **ministers of Hashem,** serving Hashem with proper intention, while other priests were merely **ministers of the altar,** performing its sacrifices but not serving Hashem with the desired intention. Therefore, he says, **“Wail, O ministers of the altar”:** those who merely minister to the altar should wail in despair, for their prayers are not favorably received. **“Come, spend the night in sackcloth, O ministers of my God”:** the righteous, however, who truly serve Hashem, should spend the night in sackcloth, praying and repenting so as to avert the decree. He explains why the priests, more than the rest of the people, should be stirred to do this: **“For the meal offering and the libation have been withheld from the House of your God.”** Because of the locusts, the wheat used for the meal offerings had spoiled and become worm-infested, and the wine used for the libations had been ruined.

**אהה,** שיזעקו ויאמרו **אהה ליום** מפני שני טעמים, א] מצד **שקרוב היום** במהרה תבא הגזירה, ב] מפני כי **כשד משדי יבא** שאין עצה להמלט ממנו כאילו בא השוד ע"י ה' ובהשגחתו, אף שאין הטבע מחייבת את השוד בא מצד שדי ששודד את הטבע, ולפני זעם ה' מי יעמוד:


**Alas:** They should cry out and say, **“Alas for the day!”** for two reasons: 1) because **the day is near**—the decree will soon come; and 2) because **it will come like devastation from the Almighty,** from which there is no way to escape. It is as though the devastation comes from Hashem and through His Divine Providence. Although the natural order does not require this devastation, it comes from the Almighty, who overpowers the natural order—and who can withstand the wrath of Hashem?

**“Hashem answered and said to His people”:** His answer would be addressed directly to the people themselves, in their own merit. If He had acted for the sake of His great Name while they themselves were undeserving, it should have said, “Behold, I am giving you the grain,” since it would not be theirs by virtue of their deeds but merely a gift from Him. Since He would act for their sake, however, it says, **“Behold, I am sending you the grain,”** for it belongs to them by virtue of their deeds. Its arrival had merely been delayed, and Hashem would send it to them because it was rightfully theirs and they deserved it through their merit. Furthermore, if He had acted only for the sake of His great Name, He would not have performed a miracle for them beyond the natural order. He would have given them only grain, which grows naturally when sown, but not new wine and oil, since the trees had already been destroyed, as explained in chapter 1, and could not naturally produce fruit. He therefore promises to perform a miracle for them in their merit, so that **the new wine and the oil** would also flourish. Moreover, if He had acted only for the sake of His great Name, so that it would not be profaned, He would have provided for them only sparingly—the minimum necessary to prevent the profanation of His Name. But since He would act in their merit, He says, **“and you shall be satisfied with it”:** He would give them an abundance sufficient to satisfy them. Finally, if He had acted only for the sake of His great Name, He would not have been concerned for the honor of an undeserving people, but only for the honor of His Name. Since He will act in their merit, however, He will safeguard their honor: **“and I will no longer make you a disgrace among the nations.”**

**And the northern one:** Furthermore, if the deliverance had been granted only for the sake of His Name, He would not have driven the locust swarm far away, so that it would remain nearby and ready to return should they continue to sin. But now, **“I will remove it far from you”**—the locust swarm that came from the north. Finally, if He had acted for the sake of His Name, He would have sent the swarm against the nations to avenge the honor of His Name, which they had scorned, and so that Israel would learn a lesson, as it says, “I have cut off nations … I said: Surely you will fear Me; you will accept correction.” Here, however, since I have acted for your sake, **“I will drive it into a parched and desolate land.”** **Its vanguard:** He depicts the swarm as having spread across the entire land, from sea to sea, moving from west to east. When the decree ended, it divided into two parts. The part standing in the east, which he calls **“its vanguard,”** would be carried into **the eastern sea,** while **“its rear guard”** would be carried into **the western sea.** **“Its stench will rise”:** when a locust swarm departs, it ordinarily leaves foulness and stench behind—foulness from the eggs it deposits and stench from the many locusts that die. The land consequently becomes putrid, causing pestilence and disease through the corruption of the air. He promises that this foulness and stench will rise and disappear, **“for it has done great things.”** This means that the swarm has already completed its mission and caused immense devastation. It also means that it has already accomplished much by bringing the people to repentance, so there is no need for it to inflict any further suffering. Our Sages interpreted this passage as referring to the evil inclination. Their meaning is that it is not the serpent that kills, but sin that kills: the evil inclination caused the locust swarm to come. When “the northern one”—the evil inclination concealed within the heart and inciting a person to sin—is driven away, its consequence, the locust swarm that came because of sin, will also be driven away.

He therefore did not need to repay them for what the cutting locust consumed at the beginning of its invasion, but only for what the swarming locust, the hopping locust, and the destroying locust consumed after they had already repented. He would likewise repay them for what the cutting locust consumed at the end, after they had repented. During that later period, the cutting locust operated together with the other species, and He must therefore repay them for the losses they caused.

**And you shall eat abundantly and be satisfied:** Beyond repaying them by increasing the produce until it makes up for what was lost during the years of famine, He will place a blessing within their bodies, so that they will eat a little and it will become blessed within them. This is the meaning of **“you shall eat and be satisfied.”** This blessing is a supernatural miracle and wonder. Therefore, **“you shall praise the Name of Hashem your God,”** who has directed His particular Providence toward you and **“has dealt wondrously with you.”** Through this, the glory of His great Name will be magnified. **“And My people shall never be ashamed”:** Earlier, He said, “I will no longer make you a disgrace among the nations.” There is a distinction between disgrace and shame: disgrace is inflicted by others, whereas shame is what one feels within oneself. This means that they will no longer be ashamed of their evil deeds, for through repentance out of love, their sins will be transformed into merits. He also alludes to the law explained in the Laws of Fasts: if the people have begun fasting and rain falls, they do not complete the fast. They first eat and afterward recite Hallel, for Hallel can be recited only from a state of satisfaction. This applies when their prayers are answered in the middle of the fast. He thus depicts Hashem answering them during their fast, whereupon they eat to satisfaction so that they may praise Him through the recitation of Hallel, which is recited only after one has eaten.

**והיה אחרי כן,** מוסב על מה שאמר שיבא עת שידעו את ה' בידיעה ברורה שה' יהיה בקרבם שוכן בשכינה מתמדת שזה יהיה באחרית הימים, ינבא מה שיהיה בימים ההם, והנה מהרי"א הוכיח מזה שגם הנבואה הראשונה הוא על ד' מלכיות, וד' מיני ארבה הוא משל ומליצה אל ד' מלכיות שונות אשר יאכלום בכל פה, ולכן דמה את הארבה כעם רב וגוי עצום וכסוסים ופרשים ומרכבות וחיילות מזוינים לוחמים וכובשים שהכל יצדק כפי הנמשל, אולם פשטות כל הנבואה מורה שלא כפירושו, אם לא נאמר כי מדי נבא אל הארבה שהיה באמת בימים ההמה, שוטטה ברוחו פי שנים גם נבואת הד' מלכיות שדומה אליו במספר הארבעה, אולם למ"ש אין כל קושיא מסמיכת הנבואה הזאת שנמשך למ"ש וידעתם כי בקרב ישראל אני, שזו נבואה עתידה, ועל זה אמר שבאחרית הימים שתרבה ידיעת ה' למטה תתער גם אתערותא דלעילא וישפוך את רוחו עליהם, **אשפך את רוחי על כל בשר,** רוח ה' כולל כל המעלות הנפשיות כמ"ש ונחה עליו רוח ה' רוח חכמה ובינה רוח עצה וגבורה רוח דעת ויראת ה', שכל שפע רוחנית שתחול על האדם מאת ה', בין על כח הגוף בגבורה בין על כח המדמה בעצה והשערה אמתיית, בין על כח בשכל חכמה ובינה, בין השפע הנבואיית ברוח דעת ויראת ה' אשר למעלה מהשכל, יכונה בשם רוח ה', ואז ישפוך רוחו על כל בשר בשפע רב ועצום כשופך מים שהם מתפשטים בכל מקום ויכסו את הכל בשוה, אולם בכ"ז יהיה הבדל ביניהם לפי הכנת המקבלים, והוא **ונבאו בניכם ובנותיכם,** כי יש שלש מדרגות, א] הנבואה הגמורה, ב] החזון שהוא למטה מהנבואה שיראה מחזות בהקיץ ע"י שיחול השפע על כח דמיונו, ג] החלום הצודק שהוא למטה מן השפע, ומעורב בו תערובות תבן וסיגים יותר מן החזון, כמ"ש הנביא אשר אתו חלום יספר חלום וכו' מה לתבן את הבר (ירמיהו כ״ג:כ״ח), ובזה יאמר שהבנים והבנות הצעירים שהם נולדו בדור המשיח המוכן לרוח אלהי, הם ינבאו נבואות גמורות, **והזקנים** שהם נולדו בדור קדום רחוק מדור המשיח, לא יושפע עליהם רק **החלומות** הצודקים שהוא למטה מן החזון וכ"ש מהנבואה, **והבחורים** שהם נולדו ג"כ לפני בא המשיח ולידתם היה קרוב לזמנו יותר מן הזקנים, הם **חזיונות יראו** שהוא שפע ממוצע בין הנבואה והחלום:


**And it shall come to pass afterward:** This refers back to his statement that a time will come when they will know Hashem with clear knowledge, because Hashem will be in their midst and His Divine Presence will dwell among them continually. This will occur at the End of Days, and he now prophesies concerning what will happen at that time. Abarbanel deduced from this that the preceding prophecy also concerns the four kingdoms, and that the four species of locusts are a parable and poetic image of four different kingdoms that will devour Israel completely. This is why the locusts are compared to a numerous people and a mighty nation, to horses, horsemen, chariots, and armed forces that wage war and conquer—all of which fits the subject represented by the parable. The plain meaning of the entire prophecy, however, does not support his interpretation, unless we say that while the prophet was prophesying about the locust swarm that actually appeared in those days, his spirit perceived a second, parallel prophecy concerning the four kingdoms, which resemble the swarm in also being four in number. According to what I have explained, however, there is no difficulty in the placement of this prophecy, for it follows directly from the statement, “And you shall know that I am in the midst of Israel,” which is itself a prophecy concerning the future. He therefore says that at the End of Days, when knowledge of Hashem increases below, an awakening from above will also arise, and He will pour out His Spirit upon them. **“I will pour out My Spirit upon all flesh”:** The Spirit of Hashem encompasses every spiritual faculty, as it says, “The Spirit of Hashem shall rest upon him: a spirit of wisdom and understanding, a spirit of counsel and strength, a spirit of knowledge and fear of Hashem.” Every spiritual influence bestowed upon a person by Hashem is called the Spirit of Hashem—whether it affects the body’s power through strength; the imaginative faculty through counsel and true intuition; the intellect through wisdom and understanding; or, above the intellect, the prophetic faculty through a spirit of knowledge and fear of Hashem. At that time He will pour out His Spirit upon all flesh in immense abundance, like water that spreads everywhere and covers everything equally. Nevertheless, distinctions will remain according to the preparedness of those who receive it. Thus, **“your sons and your daughters shall prophesy.”** There are three levels: complete prophecy; vision, which is below prophecy and consists of beholding visions while awake when the spiritual influence rests upon the imaginative faculty; and a true dream, which is below vision and contains a greater admixture of chaff and impurities, as it says, “The prophet who has a dream, let him relate the dream … What has the chaff to do with the grain?” (Jeremiah 23:28). The young sons and daughters, who will have been born in the generation of the Messiah and will therefore be prepared to receive the Divine Spirit, will experience complete prophecy. **The elders,** who were born in an earlier generation remote from the generation of the Messiah, will receive only **true dreams,** which are below vision and certainly below prophecy. **The young men,** who were likewise born before the Messiah’s arrival but closer to his time than the elders, **will see visions,** an intermediate level of spiritual influence between prophecy and dreams.

This will be brought about by Hashem, **to bring them down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat.** He thus calls the Valley of Gog’s Multitude, where they will fall among the slain, “the Valley of Jehoshaphat.” He explains why it is given this name: **“And I will enter into judgment with them there,”** for there I will bring them to judgment **concerning My people and My inheritance, Israel,** because of the evils they committed against them. Although Hashem decreed exile upon Israel, the nations assisted in inflicting evil and exceeded the proper measure in two ways. First, **“they scattered them among the nations”:** because of the many expulsions and persecutions, Israel found no rest even in the lands of its enemies and was dispersed to every corner of the earth. Second, the nations took possession of Israel as though the people belonged to them and were property purchased with their money. This was expressed in **“they divided My land,”** as though they had acquired the land as an inheritance because they regarded Israel as their servants, and whatever a servant acquires belongs to his master.

**וגם,** והנה האומות שמסרתי אותם בידם כמו אשור ובבל הם רק הפריזו על המדה, אבל עקר הדבר שעשו להם רעה היה בגזרת ה', **אבל מה אתם לי** אתם **צור וצידון וכל גלילות פלשת?** שלא מסרתי את ישראל בידכם כלל מי צוה אתכם להרע להם? **הגמול אתם משלמים לי,** וכי גמלתי אתכם רעה שבעבורה אתם משלמים לי גמול רעה תחת רעה, מה הרעה אשר עשיתי לכם? ויש הבדל בין גומל גמול ובין משלם גמול, המשלם גמול הוא שקבל רעה מחברו על ידי איבה ושנאה שיש לו עליו והוא משלם כמדתו רעה תחת רעה, אבל הגומל גמול יצוייר גם שלא קבל רע ממנו רק שעושה לו רע מצד האיבה שיש לו עליו, ועל זה אמר **ואם גומלין אתם עלי** אחר שאני לא עשיתי עמכם רע עד שתשלמו לי גמול רעה תחת רעה, רק שאתם עושים רע מצד האיבה שיש לכם על ישראל הגם שלא הרעו לכם, **קל מהרה אשיב גמולכם בראשכם,** א"כ לא אמתין לכם עד העת האחרון ויום הדין העתיד כמו שאמתין ליתר אומות, רק אמהר להשיב גמולכם במהרה עוד בזה"ז, שבימי אלכסנדר מוקדון חרבו צור וצידון ופלשת והושמו תל עולם שממה:


**Moreover:** The nations into whose hands I delivered Israel, such as Assyria and Babylon, merely exceeded the proper measure, while the underlying harm they inflicted was ordained by Hashem. **But what are you to Me, Tyre, Sidon, and all the regions of Philistia?** I never delivered Israel into your hands at all; who commanded you to harm them? **Are you repaying Me for something?** Did I do you some harm for which you are now repaying Me evil for evil? What wrong have I done to you? There is a distinction between “recompensing” evil and “repaying” evil. One who repays evil has suffered harm from another because of that person’s hostility and hatred, and therefore repays him in kind—evil for evil. Recompensing evil, however, can apply even when one has suffered no harm from the other but harms him merely because of one’s own hostility toward him. Therefore, he says, **“And if you are recompensing Me,”** meaning: since I did you no harm for which you might repay Me evil for evil, you are acting solely because of your hostility toward Israel, even though Israel did you no harm. **“Swiftly and speedily I will return your recompense upon your own heads.”** I will therefore not wait until the final era and the future Day of Judgment, as I will with the other nations, but will swiftly repay you even in the present age. Indeed, in the days of Alexander the Great, Tyre, Sidon, and Philistia were destroyed and reduced to everlasting heaps of desolation.

Because they are the children of Israel—a name indicating that they transcend the natural order, as it says, “For you have striven with Divine beings”—He will be their stronghold, enabling them to prevail through the might of Hashem, which is greater than mere refuge.
