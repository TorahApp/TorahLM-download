וחזרו לבנותו י"ח שנה אח"ז בשנת שתים לדריוש, שנתקיים עד רצתה הארץ את שבתותיה: העיר ה' את רוח כורש מבואר ביוסיפון שעת פתר דניאל לבלשאצר מכתב המלך שיענש בלשאצר על שחלל כלי המקדש, ונהרג בו בלילה ודריוש מדאה קבל מלכותו, נודע זה לדריוש וכורש ונדרו לבנות המקדש, וגם י"ל שנודע לו מנבואת ישעיה שנבא עליו הוא יבנה עירי וגלותי ישלח, ודברי ה' אלה העירו עת רוחו, ויעבר קול בכל מלכותו וגם במכתב שהכריזו כן בקול, וגם נתן הפקודה במכתב כי הפקודה בלא מכתב יוכלו לשנותה, אבל כתב אשר נכתב בשם המלך, לא היה אפשר לבטל בדתי מדי ופרס [כמבואר באסתר, בדניאל סי' וא"ו], כי מה שבטל הבנין אח"ז לא היה בזה שינוי מפקודה זאת כמו שית' בסי' ד', וגם שע"י המכתב הזה יכלו להחל לבנות שנית בימי דריוש האחרון כמ"ש לקמן (סי' וא"ו א' ב'):


**“And in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia”**—Ezra [who wrote his own book and the Book of Chronicles] concluded at the end of Chronicles: “And he exiled the remnant to Babylon, and they became servants to him and his sons until the reign of the kingdom of Persia, to fulfill the word of Hashem spoken by Jeremiah, until the land had appeased its Sabbaths; throughout all the days of its desolation it rested, to fulfill seventy years. And in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, in order to complete the word of Hashem spoken by Jeremiah, Hashem stirred the spirit of Cyrus,” etc., through, “Whoever among you is of all His people—Hashem his God be with him, and let him go up.” It is evident that the Book of Ezra was written after the Book of Chronicles; therefore, Ezra began this book with the final two verses with which he concluded his previous book. He consequently said “And in the year,” with a conjunctive vav, because this is a continuation of the Book of Chronicles [this is also the opinion of Don Isaac Abarbanel]. The meaning is that Jeremiah’s prophecy explicitly stated that the decree of restoration would occur at the time of Babylon’s destruction, which was seventy years after the beginning of the Babylonian kingdom, as explained there [Jeremiah 25:12; 29:10]. However, because they were punished with seventy years of exile for having neglected the sabbatical years, the reckoning should have begun with the exile of Zedekiah, which occurred eighteen years after the beginning of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign, for it was from then that the land lay desolate and appeased its Sabbaths. The angel revealed this to Daniel in the third vision [Daniel 9:24], when he said, “Seventy weeks have been decreed upon your people,” as I explained there. Daniel therefore understood from the books that the reckoning had to be “for the completion of the desolations of Jerusalem,” as stated there [Daniel 9:2]. Both reckonings were fulfilled. To fulfill the word of Hashem spoken by Jeremiah, Hashem stirred the spirit of Cyrus in the first year of his reign, and Cyrus issued a decree permitting the people to return to Jerusalem and build the Temple. This decree was intended only to fulfill Jeremiah’s statement that, upon the completion of Babylon’s seventy years, “I will fulfill My good word concerning you, to return you to this place.” Jeremiah did not promise there that the Temple would then be built, for the proper time for that had not yet arrived. That time would come only eighteen years later, upon the completion of the seventy years from Jerusalem’s destruction, when atonement for the sin of neglecting the sabbatical years would be complete. However, had Cyrus not granted permission to build the Temple, the people would not have returned from exile. Therefore, Hashem stirred his spirit to grant permission to build the Temple; as a result, many returned from Babylon, and Jeremiah’s words were fulfilled. The building was subsequently halted until the second year of Darius, who again granted permission, thereby fulfilling the words, “until the land had appeased its Sabbaths,” whose reckoning began at the time of the destruction. This is what the end of Chronicles means: to fulfill the word of Hashem spoken by Jeremiah—which required them to return immediately at the beginning of Cyrus’s reign—and to fulfill “until the land had appeased its Sabbaths,” which was not completed until eighteen years later. Therefore, in the first year of Cyrus, Hashem stirred his spirit. The Book of Ezra thus continues and completes the Book of Chronicles: it relates that the exiles then went up and Jeremiah’s words were fulfilled; afterward, it relates how construction was halted and how they resumed building eighteen years later, in the second year of Darius, thereby fulfilling “until the land had appeased its Sabbaths.” “Hashem stirred the spirit of Cyrus”—it is explained in Josippon that when Daniel interpreted for Belshazzar the writing declaring that the king would be punished because Belshazzar had desecrated the vessels of the Temple, and Belshazzar was killed that very night and Darius the Mede received his kingdom, these events became known to Darius and Cyrus, and they vowed to rebuild the Temple. It may also be said that Cyrus learned of Isaiah’s prophecy concerning him: “He shall build My city and release My exiles.” These words of Hashem stirred his spirit. “And he caused a proclamation to pass throughout his entire kingdom, and also in writing”—he had it publicly proclaimed aloud, and he also issued the decree in writing, because a decree not committed to writing could be altered. But a document written in the king’s name could not be revoked under the laws of Media and Persia [as explained in Esther and in Daniel, chapter 6]. The later suspension of the building did not constitute a change to this decree, as will be explained in chapter 4. Moreover, because of this written document, they were able to begin building again in the days of the latter Darius, as stated below [Ezra 6:1–2].

**כל ממלכות**, הודיע שמאמין שיש אלהים בשמים מושל על כל, וקראו רק **אלהי השמים** כי חשב שהנהגת הארץ ימסור ביד מי שיתמנה להיות במקומו, ושהוא נבחר מה' להיות מנהיג תחתיו בארץ ומסר ממלכות הארץ בידו, והוא ג"כ **פקד עלי לבנות לו בית בירושלים** כי שם אוה למושב לו בין העם העובדים אותו:


**“All the kingdoms”**—He made known that he believed there is a God in heaven who rules over everything. He called Him only **“the God of heaven”** because he thought that God entrusted the governance of the earth to the one appointed to act in His place, and that he himself had been chosen by Hashem to govern on His behalf upon the earth, who had delivered the kingdoms of the earth into his hand. He had also **“charged me to build Him a house in Jerusalem,”** because there He desired to establish His dwelling among the people who worship Him.

**מי בכם**, אמר כן אל האומות מי אשר נמצא ביניכם מכל עמו, כי אחר שבנין ביתו א"א רק ע"י עמו א"כ צריכים אתם לשלוח אותם לשם, יהי ה' אלהיו עמו, ר"ל תלוה אליו עזר אלוה והתעוררות עליון, אם על העליה לירושלים שעז"א **ויעל לירושלים, אם על הבנין שעז"א ויבן את בית ה' אלהי ישראל**, ור"ל כי לעתיד כתיב כי ביתי בית תפלה יקרא לכל העמים, ולא יקרא בפרטות שהוא אלהי ישראל כי כל העמים יעבדוהו, אבל עתה לא הגיע הזמן לזה, ועז"א **הוא האלהים אשר בירושלים** לבד, כי הנהגת כל העולם חוץ מירושלים מסר ביד המערכה לדעתו, והנה אמר שמי שיתעורר מעצמו לעלות ולבנות ילך, אבל.


**“Whoever among you”**—He said this to the nations: Whoever is found among you from among all His people—for since the building of His Temple is possible only through His people, you must send them there. “May Hashem his God be with him,” meaning that Divine assistance and heavenly inspiration should accompany him: both for the ascent to Jerusalem, concerning which he said, **“Let him go up to Jerusalem,” and for the building, concerning which he said, “And let him build the house of Hashem, the God of Israel.”** His meaning was that concerning the future it is written, “For My house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples,” and He will not be called specifically the God of Israel, because all peoples will worship Him. But the time for this had not yet arrived. Therefore, he said, **“He is the God who is in Jerusalem”** alone, because, in his view, God had entrusted the governance of the entire world outside Jerusalem to the celestial order. He then said that whoever was moved of his own accord to go up and build should go; however—

**וכל הנשאר**, מי שלא יתעורר ללכת וישאר בחו"ל, שזה יהיה אם מפני שנפשו שפלה בלתי מתנשא לדבר הגדול הזה אם מפני שחסר לו ההוצאה, אם מפני שאין לו להביא נדבה לבנין המקדש, ע"ז צוה **שכל הנשאר מכל המקומות**, הוא מצוה לאנשי מקומו, א] **שינשאוהו בכסף ובזהב, וברכוש** שהוא שוה כסף, **ובבהמה** לרכוב עליה, ב] **שינשאוהו** שכולל שינשאו את רוחו לזה, ג] **עם הנדבה** והאומות ישלחו על ידו נדבה הקדש לבית אלהים:


**“And whoever remains”**—Whoever is not moved to go and remains outside the Land—whether because his spirit is lowly and does not rise to this great undertaking, because he lacks the expenses, or because he has nothing to contribute toward the building of the Temple—concerning him Cyrus commanded that **whoever remains, in whatever place he resides**, should be assisted by the people of his place: (1) **They should support him with silver and gold, with goods** having monetary value, **and with animals** upon which to ride; (2) **they should uplift him**, which also means that they should inspire his spirit to undertake this journey; and (3) **together with the voluntary offering**—the nations should send through him a consecrated donation for the House of God.

**ויקומו**, ספר שהיו שני ענינים, א] שהעם התנשאו מעצמם לדבר זה ובראשם היו **ראשי האבות ליהודה ובנימין** שהם התעוררו לזה תחילה והי' איתערותא דלתתא ויקומו, ומזה בא איתערותא דלעילא, כי **לכל**, ר"ל לכל העולים, העיר **האלהים את רוחו** בין **לעלות** בין **לבנות את בית ה'**, ובכ"ז גם **כל סביבותיהם** היינו נעמים שסביבותם **חזקו בידיהם** [חיזוק ידים רפות בא תמיד ביחוס הפעול, אבל פה לא היו צריכים חיזוק מצד רפיון ידים, רק שחזקום במתנות להיות חזקים יותר, וע"ז בא בקישור הב'], **לבד** רצה לומר שכ"ז נתנו לצורך העולים לצידה והוצאת הדרך, חוץ מה ששלחו על ידם נדבות לבהמ"ק:


**“Then they arose”**—Scripture relates that there were two aspects to this. First, the people elevated themselves toward this undertaking of their own accord, led by **the heads of the ancestral houses of Judah and Benjamin**, who were the first to be awakened to it. Thus there was an awakening from below—“then they arose”—and from this came an awakening from above, for **everyone**—that is, everyone who went up—was one **whose spirit God had stirred**, both **to go up** and **to build the House of Hashem**. Nevertheless, **all those around them**—that is, the neighboring peoples—also **strengthened their hands**. [“Strengthening weak hands” is always expressed with the hands as the direct object. Here, however, they did not require strengthening because their hands were weak; rather, the people strengthened them through gifts, enabling them to be even stronger. Therefore, the phrase is constructed with the preposition “in.”] **Besides** means that everything mentioned thus far was given for the needs of those going up—their provisions and traveling expenses—apart from the donations that were sent through them for the Temple.

###### Malbim on Ezra 1:7:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/1:7:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/1:7:2)


**והמלך כורש הוציא** וכו' **ויוציאם**, כי כאשר היו בבית אלילים וא"א לשמש בהם עוד בבהמ"ק, ספר שכורש הוציא אותם תחלה מבית ע"א ויתנם אל הגזבר, וזה היה כעין ביטול, אחרי שיצאו מרשות ע"א לרשות המלך, ומרשות המלך הוציאם על ידי הגזבר שלו ויספרם לששבצר:


**“And King Cyrus brought out,”** etc., **“and he brought them out”**—Because the vessels had been kept in a temple of idols, and it would otherwise have been impossible to use them again in the Temple, Scripture relates that Cyrus first brought them out of the idolatrous temple and delivered them to the treasurer. This constituted a kind of nullification of their idolatrous status, since they passed from the possession of the idol to the possession of the king. The king then had his treasurer bring them out from his own possession and count them out to Sheshbazzar.

**ואלה בני המדינה**, פרשה זאת הוכפלה בנחמיה [סי' ז'], בשנויים רבים, קצת מהם בא שם המספר פחות מבכאן כמו בני ארח שבע מאות, ושם חשבם שש מאות?, בני זתוא תשע מאות ושם שמונה מאות?, וברובם בא שם המספר נוסף כמו בני עזגד אלף ושם אלפים, בני עדין ד' מאות ושם ו' מאות, וכדומה, וכפי הנראה כאן מדבר מן החשובי' שעלו מבבל עצמה, כי בגלות יכניה הגלה את השרים וטובי העם ואותם הגלה לעיר המלוכה והיו תמיד בכבוד, אבל גלות צדקיהו הגלה אל המדינה כי היו שפלים בעיניו, וכמ"ש [ירמיה כ"ד] כתאנים הטובות וכו' והתאנים הרעות וכו', ופה אמר אשר הגלה נ"נ לבבל, ושם אמר סתם אשר הגלה נ"נ, כי פה דבר מאותם שגלו בגלות יכניה שהם גלו לבבל עצמה לעיר המלוכה, ושם דבר מגלות צדקיהו שהגלה אל ערי המדינה. החשובים שדבר מהם בכאן היה עיקר מהלכם לירושלים ומשם התפשטו ביהודה, והפחותים היה עיקר מהלכם ליהודה לגור שם, לכן אמר פה לירושלים ויהודה בלא למ"ד היחוס ושם אמר לירושלים וליהודה שניהם בלמ"ד, וזה כמ"ש בספרי התו"ה [פ' אחרי בפסוק ואיל לעולה] שכ"מ שלא כפל אות היחוס הראשון עקר, וכשכפלו שניהם שוים, בכאן אמר אשר באו עם זרובבל וכו', ושם הוסיף עזריה רעמיה נחמני, שהם הגיעו אח"כ עם חבורות אנשים, ולכן חשב שם יותר מבכאן, והקצת שחשב בכאן יותר הם אותם ששבו מא"י לבבל אחר שנשבת הבנין ונחסר מנינם בחשבון השני שהיה מאוחר [וע"ע בפ' ס"ד]:


**“Now these are the people of the province”**—This passage is repeated in Nehemiah [chapter 7], with many variations. In some cases, the number given there is smaller than the number here: for example, the descendants of Arah number seven hundred here and six hundred there; the descendants of Zattu number nine hundred here and eight hundred there. In most cases, however, the number there is greater: the descendants of Azgad number one thousand here and two thousand there; the descendants of Adin number four hundred here and six hundred there, and so forth. It appears that the present passage speaks of the prominent people who went up from Babylon itself. When Jehoiachin was exiled, Nebuchadnezzar exiled the officials and the leading members of the nation. He brought them to the capital, where they were always treated with honor. Those exiled with Zedekiah, however, were sent throughout the provinces, because they were lowly in his eyes, as described [in Jeremiah 24] by the good figs and the bad figs. Here it says, “whom Nebuchadnezzar had exiled to Babylon,” whereas there it merely says, “whom Nebuchadnezzar had exiled.” This is because the present passage speaks of those exiled with Jehoiachin, who were brought to Babylon itself, the capital city, while the passage there speaks of those exiled with Zedekiah, who were sent to the provincial cities. The prominent people discussed here had Jerusalem as their principal destination, and from there they spread throughout Judah. The less prominent people had Judah as their principal destination, intending to settle there. Therefore, here it says “to Jerusalem and Judah,” without repeating the preposition “to” before Judah, whereas there it says “to Jerusalem and to Judah,” using the preposition for both. This accords with what I wrote in my commentary HaTorah VeHaMitzvah [on the portion of Aharei Mot, concerning the verse “and a ram for a burnt offering”]: wherever the preposition is not repeated, the first element is primary; when it is repeated, both elements are equal. Here it says that they “came with Zerubbabel,” etc., whereas there it adds Azariah, Raamiah, and Nahamani, who arrived later with additional groups of people. Therefore, the number counted there is greater than the number here. In the few cases where the number counted here is greater, the difference consists of those who returned from the Land of Israel to Babylon after construction was halted; their number was consequently absent from the second count, which was made later. [See further below, verse 64.]

**“The descendants of Solomon’s servants”**—According to one opinion in Yevamot, which holds that the child of a non-Jew or a slave and a Jewish woman is a mamzer, they were mamzerim, because Solomon’s servants married Jewish women and had children by them. They therefore belong with those listed afterward whose parentage was uncertain—the shetuki and the asufi—as stated in Kiddushin: “Ten genealogical classes went up from Babylon.” According to the opinion that the child is legitimate, it may be said that they were included among the emancipated slaves. As Tosafot explain in Yevamot [16b], because their owners had despaired of recovering them, they did not require documents of emancipation. They were thus included among the converts and freedmen counted among the ten genealogical classes. Since they are counted together with the Temple servants, it appears that they descended from the seven Canaanite nations whom Solomon subjected to forced labor, as stated in II Chronicles [8:7]. **“The descendants of Hassophereth”**—The initial heh is not the definite article but an integral part of the name, as I have demonstrated similarly in I Chronicles 2:52 and elsewhere.

**בני הקוץ בני ברזילי**, ר"ל שמשפחת הקוץ היו כהנים ונזכרו בדה"י [א' כ"ד י"ט] שהיו בית השביעי במעמד המשמרות, ועתה נולד בקצת בניו ספק, והיה הסיבה לזה מפני שקוץ לקח אשה מבנות ברזילי חשובים בימי דוד ושלמה, והבנים שנולדו מן האשה הזאת נקראו לכבוד על שם ברזילי ג"כ, וי"ל שחביה הוא הבן שנולד מן האשה הזאת ובניו היה להם שני שמות שנקראו בשם בני הקוץ וגם בשם בני ברזילי, ומפרש שזה היה מפני **אשר לקח**[ר"ל הקוץ לקח] **מבנות ברזילי הגלעדי אשה ויקרא** הוא חביה שנולד מן האשה הזאת נקרא **על שמם**, שנקרא על שם אביו ועל שם אבי אמו, והבנים נקראו בני הקוץ ובני ברזילי, וכ"ז שהיה ספר היחוס בידם לא היה בזה חשש, אבל**עתה בקשו כתבם המתיחשים ולא נמצאו** כתבי היחוס שלהם [כי היה לכל משפחה ספר יחוס כולל לכל המשפחה ויחידים היו להם ג"כ ע"ז כתבים בפ"ע איש איש יחוסו הפרטי ולא נמצא לא כתבם הכולל [ועז"א כתבם בלשון יחיד] ולא יחוסיהם הפרטיים שעז"א ולא נמצאו בל"ר] ועי"כ נפל ספק בענינם כי יש לחוש שאינם כהנים כלל רק ישראלים מבני ברזילי, כי היתה גם משפחה מישראל שנקראת בשם בני ברזילי, ולכן **ויגאלו מן הכהונה**, בפרט כי בספק כזה נכנס ספק אחר שהם חללים, כי עי"כ גם הם בעצמם לא נשמרו מנשים הפסולות לכהונה, וזה נכלל במ"ש **ויגואלו**, שכבר נגאלו מן הכהונה כי לקחו נשים פסולות, ובזה תמצא חללי שחשב בין עשרה יוחסין:


**“The descendants of Hakkoz, the descendants of Barzillai”**—That is, the family of Hakkoz consisted of priests, and they are mentioned in Chronicles [I Chronicles 24:19] as the seventh house in the order of the priestly divisions. Now, however, doubt arose concerning some of his descendants. The reason was that Hakkoz had taken a wife from the daughters of Barzillai, who were prominent in the days of David and Solomon, and the sons born from this woman were also honorably called by the name of Barzillai. It may be that Habaiah was the son born from this woman, and his descendants had two names: they were called both the descendants of Hakkoz and the descendants of Barzillai. Scripture explains that this was because **he took**—that is, Hakkoz took—**a wife from the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and he was called**—that is, Habaiah, who was born from this woman, was called—**by their name.** He was named both after his father and after his maternal grandfather, and his descendants were therefore called both the descendants of Hakkoz and the descendants of Barzillai. As long as their genealogical register remained in their possession, this created no difficulty. But **they sought their genealogical record, but it could not be found.** Their genealogical documents were no longer available. [Each family possessed a general genealogical register covering the entire family, while individuals also possessed separate documents recording their own particular lineage. Neither their collective family record—which is why “their record” is expressed in the singular—nor their individual genealogical records—which is why “they were not found” is expressed in the plural—could be found.] Consequently, doubt arose concerning their status, for there was reason to suspect that they were not priests at all but Israelites descended from Barzillai, since there was also an Israelite family known as the descendants of Barzillai. Therefore, **they were disqualified from the priesthood.** In particular, this uncertainty created an additional concern that they were halalim—persons disqualified from the priesthood through improper priestly unions—because, owing to their uncertain status, they themselves had not refrained from marrying women forbidden to priests. This too is included in the words **“they were disqualified,”** for they had already become defiled with respect to the priesthood by marrying disqualified women. This accounts for the halalim counted among the ten genealogical classes.

**“The entire congregation together”**—The individual figures fall short of the total by thirteen thousand; see Rashi. Apparently, the individual enumeration did not include everyone who came, but only those who first arrived from Babylon. Even in Nehemiah, which also includes those who came from the land of the Chaldeans [as I explained on verse 1], not everyone was counted individually, because additional people arrived every day who are not mentioned in the enumeration here. Those who joined families from which people had already arrived were added to the individual family totals in Nehemiah. New families, however, or individuals who came from cities not previously represented, were not mentioned individually at all. Nevertheless, they were included in the general count, so that after all the groups had assembled, their number was forty-two thousand, etc. Here, the descendants of Magbish number one hundred and fifty-six, whereas Nehemiah does not mention them. It must therefore be that Nehemiah included them within one of the families whose number is greater there. For example, the descendants of Azgad number one thousand more there. It may be that all the people of the city of Magbish belonged to the family of Azgad: here they were counted according to their city, while there they were counted according to their family. This may also explain why the descendants of Arah and the descendants of Zattu are each fewer by one hundred in Nehemiah than in the first count. It may be that one hundred descendants of Arah and one hundred descendants of Zattu lived in the city of Senaah and were counted there among the people of their city. This would explain why Nehemiah adds three hundred to the descendants of Senaah, without requiring the strained explanation that some of them returned to Babylon.

Sin offerings and guilt offerings, however—such as the festival goats—were not offered upon a great public altar. Likewise, on Yom Kippur they offered neither the goats nor the bulls, as Tosafot explain. Individual obligatory offerings were certainly not offered, nor were meal offerings, since meal offerings were not permitted upon an altar outside the Temple. The verses can therefore be understood literally: they offered burnt offerings alone.

**“And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam wrote”**—At that time, they hired counselors against them to frustrate the construction, as we shall explain. Although there are various opinions concerning the Persian kings and their names, this Artaxerxes must have been Cyrus, as Rashi explains, since construction was halted during Cyrus’s reign. We must therefore say that “Artaxerxes” was an honorific title used for the Persian kings. According to the opinion that Artaxerxes was the name of a different king, we must say that construction was not halted until after Cyrus’s death, and that this Artaxerxes was a king who reigned after Cyrus. In any event, we must say that construction was suspended for fourteen years—the two weeks mentioned in Daniel [9:25], according to my interpretation. Before explaining the following passage, I shall present an introduction, for several matters are extremely perplexing. First, Cyrus himself had issued the decree authorizing the Temple’s construction and had said that Hashem had charged him to build Him a House. How, then, could he have reversed his decree and halted the construction only a short time later? We know that under the laws of Persia and Media, any document written in the king’s name could not be revoked by the king under any circumstances. This is evident from the account of Daniel [Daniel 6] and from that of Ahasuerus, who could not revoke the written decree even though it had been issued in error, as we explained in our commentary on the Book of Esther. Second, they wrote that the Jews were building and fortifying the walls of Jerusalem. This was entirely false, for the Jews were building only the Temple. The wall was not built until the days of Artaxerxes, who granted special permission for it. Moreover, how could the construction of the Temple enable a rebellion when they had no fortified city in which to take refuge? The Temple was being built from wood and stone and could easily have been burned if they rebelled against the king’s command, as will be explained in chapter 6. How, then, did the king heed these lies, and how did they dare to speak falsely? These questions illuminate the meaning of the verses. In truth, an elaborate deception was carried out. Through monetary bribes, the adversaries of Judah hired counselors, and their deceit succeeded. They knew that it was impossible to revoke the decree authorizing the Temple’s construction, because under the laws of Persia and Media, a document written in the king’s name could not be rescinded. They also knew that they could not accuse the Jews of rebellion merely for building the House of God, since Jerusalem’s wall remained breached. Yet they were afraid to write falsely to the king that the Jews had violated his command and, instead of building the Temple, were rebuilding the city wall, lest the king discover their falsehood and hold them liable with their lives. They therefore devised the following plan. Because the Persian Empire was vast and extensive, and the various peoples under its rule each spoke their own language, the law permitted anyone wishing to write to the king to write in his own language and script, as it is written, “that every man should rule in his own house and speak the language of his own people.” In each province, two royal officials were appointed to receive letters written in the language of that province, translate them into the Persian language and script, and send them to the king. Likewise, the king’s reply would arrive in Persian language and script to these two translators. They would translate it into the language of the province and deliver the translated response to the original writers. The officials appointed over the province of Syria were Rehum the counselor, who translated from one language into another, and Shimshai the scribe, who converted the writing into Persian script. The language and script used in Syria were Aramaic. Therefore, the three men who composed the accusation—**Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of their associates**, meaning their companions and assistants—**wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The text of the document**—that is, the document that had to be translated, with the translation made to correspond precisely to the original—**was written in Aramaic and translated in Aramaic**; that is, it was in Aramaic script and the Aramaic language. They delivered this letter, prepared for the king, to Rehum the counselor and Shimshai the scribe so that they could translate it into the Persian language and script.

**“Let it be known to the king”**—Here begins the body of the petition. **“The Jews who came up from you”**—whom you released and sent away. [This proves that construction was halted during the reign of Cyrus. If this occurred after his reign, one must interpret the phrase as meaning “from your province.”] **“Have come to Jerusalem, the rebellious and wicked city; they are building, and they have completed the walls”**—Here lay the translators’ deception and falsification, for this wording admits two interpretations. It may be understood as attaching “the rebellious and wicked city” to what precedes: they came to Jerusalem, which is a rebellious and sinful city. The word “building” would then begin a separate statement, meaning that they were constructing a building there—namely, the Temple—and had laid the foundations of the Temple’s walls. That is, they were preparing to build its walls, for **“they had already joined together its foundations.”** According to this reading, they wrote nothing false: they truly were building the House of Hashem there in accordance with Cyrus’s command. Alternatively, the word “building” may be joined to “the rebellious and wicked city,” meaning that they came to Jerusalem and were rebuilding the rebellious and wicked city. The translators rendered it in this second sense, as though the Jews were rebuilding the city itself. If the king later discovered that this was false, they could not be punished, because the letter’s wording also permitted the first interpretation, which is how we understand it in truth. Nor could the original writers be punished, for they could defend themselves by claiming that they had intended the first interpretation—that the Jews were building the Temple there.

**“Then, as soon as the interpretation of the copy,”** etc.—The king’s decree did not mention halting the construction of the Temple, but only the rebuilding of the city. However, his words, “Stop these men, and let this city not be rebuilt,” could be interpreted as stating two separate commands: first, “Stop these men” from building the Temple—for since they were not actually rebuilding the city, there was no occasion to stop them from doing so—and second, “Let this city not be rebuilt,” an additional command forbidding the city’s reconstruction. This interpretation would have been correct had the letter sent to the king been an accusation concerning the building of the Temple, for the king’s reply would then have meant that they were to stop the Jews from building the Temple. In truth, however, the translators had transformed the accusation into one concerning the rebuilding of the city. Accordingly, the king’s words, “Stop these men,” meant to stop them from rebuilding the city, and “Let this city not be rebuilt” explained what they were to stop them from doing. Construction of the Temple remained permitted under the king’s original decree, which could not be revoked. But because Rehum and Shimshai the scribe were the translators, they now claimed that the accusation had concerned the building of the Temple. They interpreted **“the interpretation of the copy”**—that is, their rendering of the translated copy of the king’s letter, which had likewise arrived in Persian and which they translated and interpreted—as meaning that the king intended to halt construction of the Temple. Since the interpretation of the letter was in their hands, they hurried to Jerusalem, to the Jews, and stopped them by force and military power.

### Verse 14

###### Malbim on Ezra 6:14:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/6:14:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/6:14:1)


**ושבי יהודיא, ושכללו מטעם אלה ישראל** וכו' ר"ל שהיה השגחה מעוטפת בטבע שהיה מטעם אלהי ישראל ענין השגחיי, אבל לא נס נגלה רק מטעם המלכים שנתן ה' כזאת בלבם, כמ"ש ראויים היו ישראל לעשות להם נס בימי עזרא כדרך שנעשה להם בימי יב"נ אלא שגרם החטא, ומ"ש **כורש ודריוש וארתחששתא** לדעת חז"ל בר"ה הוא שם אחד, וכן פירש"י שארתחששתא הוא דריוש ונקרא כן ע"ש המלכות, וכן מ"ש [למעלה ד' ז'] ובימי ארתחששתא פירש"י שהוא כורש, שכ"ה דעתך חז"ל, אולם הרז"ה בפ"ק דר"ה כתב, זה העולה בידנו לפי מדרש חכמים, אבל הפי' הנכון לפי הפשט מ"ש מטעם כורש ודריוש וארתחששתא שלשה מלכים היו, כורש המתחיל, ודריוש המשלים בנין הבית וסמוך ליה ארתחששתא בונה חומות ירושלים, וכבר נפלו שיטות ודעות שונות במנין מלכי פרס ושמותם, ואין בזה דבר ברור:


**“And the Jewish elders—and they completed it by the command of the God of Israel,”** etc.—That is, Divine Providence was clothed in natural events: it was **by the command of the God of Israel**, and thus providential, but it was not an open miracle. Rather, it occurred through the command of the kings, for Hashem placed this intention in their hearts. As our Sages said, Israel was worthy that a miracle be performed for them in the days of Ezra as it had been in the days of Joshua son of Nun, but sin prevented it. Regarding the words **“Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes,”** according to our Sages in Rosh Hashanah, these are different names of one person. Rashi likewise explains that Artaxerxes is Darius and was called by that name as a royal title. Similarly, on the verse above [4:7], “And in the days of Artaxerxes,” Rashi explains that this refers to Cyrus, for such is the opinion of our Sages. However, Rabbi Zerahiah HaLevi writes in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah: “This is the conclusion that emerges according to the interpretation of the Sages; but the correct explanation according to the plain meaning is that the statement ‘by the command of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes’ refers to three kings: Cyrus, who began the process; Darius, who completed the building of the Temple; and immediately afterward Artaxerxes, who built the walls of Jerusalem.” Many different systems and opinions have been proposed concerning the number and names of the Persian kings, and nothing in this matter is certain.

**ויעשו חנוכת הבית הזה בחדוה**, כבר פי' הקדמונים מ"ש הבית הזה, כי אם היו זוכים אז היה מגיע זמן הגאולה העתידה, ותיכף אחר בנין זה היה יורד ביהמ"ק מן השמים כמקדש שראה יחזקאל לעתיד לבא והיו מתחילים החנוכה השנית שנאמרה ביחזקאל, ולע"ע עשו חנוכת הבית הזה, שמלת דנא מורה שמצפה על חנוכת בית אחר, והנה חנוכת יחזקאל תתחיל בכ"ג אדר, כמבואר שם, והם המשיכו חנוכה שלהם עד כ"ג אדר שהם כ"א יום, ואם היו זוכים היה תיכף חנוכת הבית האחרון שיבנה בידי שמים, ויען שבימים האלה חל פורים שהם ימי משתה ושמחה, אמר שהיה עיקר החדוה על החנוכה שהיתה גדולה משמחת הפורים:


**“And they celebrated the dedication of this House with joy”**—The earlier commentators have already explained the expression **“this House.”** Had they been worthy at that time, the appointed time of the future redemption would have arrived, and immediately after the construction of this Temple, the Temple from heaven—the future Sanctuary seen by Ezekiel—would have descended, and they would have begun the second dedication described in Ezekiel. For the time being, however, they celebrated the dedication of **this** House, the word “this” indicating the expectation of the dedication of another House. Ezekiel’s dedication is to begin on the twenty-third of Adar, as explained there. They therefore extended their dedication until the twenty-third of Adar, a period of twenty-one days. Had they been worthy, the dedication of the final Temple, to be built by Heaven, would have begun immediately afterward. Since Purim, a time of feasting and rejoicing, occurred during these days, Scripture emphasizes that their principal joy concerned the dedication, which exceeded even the joy of Purim.

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/7:11:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/7:11:1)


**וזה פרשגן הנשתון**, ר"ל שהמלך נתן לו הפקודה בכתב ולשון פרסי, והועתק ללשון ארמי שמדברים בה במדינת סוריא, ע"פ שרי המלך הממונים ע"ז שהעידו שההעתקה משתוה עם הכתב, שזה פי' מלת נשתון כנ"ל, **ספר דברי מצות**, מפרש מדוע נקרא סופר, לא מפני מלאכת ספרות לפרנסתו, רק שהיה סופר ומונה דברי מצות ה', שספר אותיות התורה ועשה סייג למסורה, וכן גדר גדרים בהעתקת יתר כתבי הקדש לנקותם מכל סיג, וגם ספר דברי תורה שבעל פה המסורה שזה נכלל במלת חקים כנ"ל: **על ישראל**, נראה שנמשך למעלה **אשר נתן המלך לעזרא על ישראל** בעבור ישראל ולצרכם:


**“And this is a copy of the document”**—That is, the king gave him the decree in the Persian script and language, and it was translated into Aramaic, the language spoken in the province of Syria, by the royal officials appointed for this purpose, who attested that the translation corresponded precisely to the original document. This is the meaning of the word *nishtavan*, as explained above. **“A scribe of the words of the commandments”**—Scripture explains why he was called a scribe. It was not because he practiced scribal work as his occupation, but because he counted and recorded the words of Hashem’s commandments. He counted the letters of the Torah and established safeguards for the Masoretic text. He likewise established safeguards in the transmission of the other books of Scripture, purifying them of every corruption. He also recorded the transmitted teachings of the Oral Torah, which are included in the term “statutes,” as explained above. **“Concerning Israel”**—This phrase apparently refers back to what precedes: **“which the king granted to Ezra concerning Israel”**—that is, for the sake of Israel and for their needs.

This is equivalent to “etc.” Above [4:17], the word ***shelem*** is used with the same meaning as “and so forth.”

### Verse 3

###### Malbim on Ezra 10:3:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/10:3:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/10:3:1)


**ועתה נכרת לאלהינו בעצת ה' ובעצת החרדים למצותיו להוציא כל נשים והנולד מהם**, ובאשר ידעו רבים יקומו נגדם ולא ירצו להוציא נשותיהם, ועזרא לא היה לו כח להעניש רק על דין תורה, והוא היה צריך להפחידם עתה בעונשין יותר חמורים, שע"ז יש כח להכלל אם יקבלו הדבר בשבועה, שאז הכלל יש להם בזה דין מלך שיכול להעניש בעונשים יותר מן הדין, כמ"ש במעשה דפלגש בגבעה ובכ"מ, ועז"א שיכרתו כולם ברית ושהם כולם עמו להסכים ע"ז באלה ובשבועה ואז **כתורה יעשה** להעניש כל העובר בכח כלל ישראל, כמו שעשו אז עונש חרוץ בבני יבש גלעד שעברו על שבועת הקהל:


**“And now, let us make a covenant with our God, according to the counsel of Hashem and of those who tremble at His commandments, to send away all the women and those born from them.”** They knew that many would oppose them and refuse to send away their wives. Ezra possessed authority to punish only according to Torah law, whereas he now needed to frighten them with penalties more severe than those prescribed by law. The community has the power to impose such penalties if its members accept the matter by oath, for the community then possesses the authority of a king, who may impose punishments beyond the strict law, as seen in the incident of the concubine at Gibeah and elsewhere. Therefore, he proposed that they all enter into a covenant and unite with him in accepting the matter through an oath and imprecation. Then **“let it be done according to the law”**—they could punish anyone who violated it by virtue of the authority of the collective body of Israel, just as a severe punishment was imposed upon the people of Jabesh-gilead for violating the congregation’s oath.

### Verse 17

###### Malbim on Ezra 10:17:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/10:17:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Malbim%20on%20Ezra/r/10:17:1)


**ויכלו בכל** [ומפרש]**באנשים** [הב' נמשך לשתים, ולכן בא מלת בכל בחול"ם] כמ"ש הראב"ע, **ההושיבו**, אשר הושיבו. **ואשמים איל צאן**, פירשו חז"ל בכריתות דף י"א שבעלו שפחה חרופה לכן היו מחוייבים איל אשם כדין, ובזה יצדק מ"ש ואשמים, כי בש"ח אינו חייב רק אם בעל כדרכו וגמר ביאתו, וע"כ אם אמרו לו שנים בעלת ש"ח והוא אומר לא בעלתי אינו חייב, שיל"פ לא גמרתי ביאתי כמ"ש בכריתות דף י"ב, וא"כ א"א לחייבו איל אשם ע"י עדים, רק הם עצמם הודו שהם אשמים, ושעי"כ חייבים להביא איל צאן. ובאשר היו יכולים לשחררה, אך שהם היו כהנים ואסורה להם משום זונה, וע"ז נתנו ידם בת"כ להוציא, כדין כהן שנשא אשה בעבירה נודר ועובד ורש"י כתב שהוראת שעה היה, והוא תמוה שהוא נגד הגמ' הנ"ל, ונראה שרש"י לשטתו שכתב בגיטין דף ל"ו דבבית שני לא נהגו יובלות וא"כ למאי דקיי"ל דש"ח היא המאורסה לעבד עברי לא היה זה אפשר בבית שני דאין עבד עברי נוהג אלא בזמן שהיובל נוהג, וע"כ שרב חסדא שאמר ששפחה חרופה בעלו, אמר כן לדעת אחרים בכריתות [י"א] דש"ח היא המאורסת לעבד כנעני, ואינה כהלכה לר"ע ור"י דס"ל דש"ח היא המאורסת לעבד עברי, אמנם התוס' בגיטין [שם] ובערכין [דף ל"א] כתבו שהיובל היה נוהג בבית שני והיה ע"ע נוהג, ולדידיה אתיא דרב חסדא כהלכה:


**“And they finished with all”**—[Scripture explains:] **“with the men”**—the preposition *bet* applies to both words, and therefore the word *bakol* is vocalized with a *holam*, as Ibn Ezra explains. **“Who had married”**—that is, who had caused foreign women to dwell with them. **“And being guilty, they offered a ram from the flock”**—Our Sages explained in Keritot 11a that they had relations with a designated maidservant and were therefore legally obligated to bring a ram as a guilt offering. This explains the words **“and being guilty,”** because one is liable for relations with a designated maidservant only if he had relations in the ordinary manner and completed the act. Therefore, if two witnesses said to him, “You had relations with a designated maidservant,” and he replied, “I did not,” he is not liable, since his response may mean, “I did not complete the act,” as explained in Keritot 12a. Consequently, witnesses alone could not obligate him to bring a ram as a guilt offering; rather, the men themselves admitted that they were guilty and thus obligated to bring a ram from the flock. Although they could have freed these women, the men were priests, and the women would have remained forbidden to them as women who had engaged in prohibited relations. They therefore pledged, under a conditional vow, to send them away, following the law governing a priest who married a woman forbidden to him: he makes a vow to divorce her and may then perform the Temple service. Rashi writes that this was a temporary ruling, but this is difficult, because it contradicts the Gemara cited above. It appears that Rashi follows his own position in Gittin 36a that the Jubilee was not observed during the Second Temple period. Since the accepted law identifies the designated maidservant as one betrothed to a Hebrew slave, such a case could not have occurred during the Second Temple period, because Hebrew slavery applies only when the Jubilee is observed. Rav Hisda’s statement that they had relations with designated maidservants must therefore follow the opinion of Aherim in Keritot 11a, which identifies her as a maidservant betrothed to a Canaanite slave, an opinion that does not accord with the law according to Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Ishmael, who maintain that she is betrothed to a Hebrew slave. Tosafot in Gittin and Arakhin [31b], however, write that the Jubilee was observed during the Second Temple period and that Hebrew slavery therefore applied. According to their view, Rav Hisda’s explanation accords with the accepted law.
