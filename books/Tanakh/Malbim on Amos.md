לה', (ומ"ש שנבא בימי ירבעם בן יואש בארתי בתחלת הושע, ששנתים לפני הרעש היה עוזיה נכנע תחת מלכי ישראל ונקרא המלכות ע"ש מלכות ישראל ולכן הזכיר את ירבעם עיי"ש):


**The words of Amos, who was among the herdsmen.** Abarbanel here objects to the position of the Guide (II:32), who established as a fundamental principle that prophecy can rest only upon a person who is prepared for it, whether through natural preparation according to the constitution of his body and brain and the power of his imagination, or through the study of wisdom and the perfection of his moral qualities. Abarbanel says that this opinion is based upon the words of the philosopher, according to whom prophecy is a natural phenomenon that comes to a person like other natural forms, which do not rest upon their subjects except after the appropriate preparations have been made. But this is not the view of our Torah, for prophecy is not a natural phenomenon, nor is preparation necessary for it; rather, prophecy is necessarily miraculous. For every faculty of apprehension has a fixed and limited relationship to the thing apprehended by it, whereas the human soul has no relationship or proportion to the Divine order that would enable it to receive that order naturally, except by way of a miracle. Accordingly, natural preparations arising from one's constitution, as well as the study of wisdom, are not conditions for prophecy. Indeed, Amos, though he was among the sheep herders—for the owner of sheep is called a *noked*—was "among the *nokdim*," meaning that he served the sheep owners. He had not studied wisdom, nor did he possess honor or wealth, and nevertheless he prophesied. In my opinion, however, there is a distinction between a prophet who prophesies for his own sake and perfection, and a prophet who is sent as a messenger for the sake of the community. For God to grant prophecy to a person who is not prepared for it is like creating something from nothing, and God will not do this except for a great necessity. For all the miracles that God performed involved the creation of something from something through the transformation of forms, as I have explained elsewhere. Therefore, God will not choose to bestow the spirit of prophecy upon a person unless he is prepared for it; He then adds His blessing to the preparation already present within him, with greater strength and greater power, in order to perfect him. As our Sages said, "The Holy One, blessed be He, gives wisdom only to one who already possesses wisdom." But when God needs to send a prophet to a generation and no person can be found who is naturally prepared for this from birth, from the womb, from conception, and through his upbringing, He will choose the person whom He brings near to Himself because of his righteousness, and the miracle will be greater. Amos's prophecy was of this kind. He did not prophesy for his own sake, but rather for the sake of the community. Although three other prophets also prophesied during that period, God desired that two prophets prophesy specifically to the Ten Tribes—Hosea and Amos—and that two prophesy to Judah—Micah and Isaiah. He desired that the former two be from among the Ten Tribes: Hosea was from the tribe of Reuben, and Amos was from Tekoa, which is in the territory of Asher. At that time, He found no one among them who was more prepared for prophecy relative to the rest of the people. Since it was necessary to send him on a mission concerning the community as a whole, He added blessing and an influx of holiness to the small degree of preparation that He found within him and poured out His spirit upon him. As for Abarbanel's objection that prophecy is not found among the nations of the world, nor among Israel after the destruction of the Temple, even though it is impossible that no person prepared for prophecy should be found, this is no objection. From Maimonides' statement that only one who is prepared for prophecy can prophesy, we cannot infer that everyone who is prepared for prophecy must necessarily prophesy. For the resting of prophecy even upon one who is prepared for it depends upon the Divine will. Even one who is prepared for prophecy is not prepared for it in the manner of things that necessarily occur by nature. The prophecy of a prepared person is itself a miracle and a wonder; it is merely not as great a miracle as the prophecy of one who is unprepared. Similarly, the miracle in which God sends such blessing upon the seed of the earth that one year's produce yields grain sufficient for three years is not as great a miracle as Elisha's miracle, in which the small amount of oil in the jar filled all the vessels. Now, concerning the words, **"which he saw concerning Israel,"** Abarbanel asks: Did he not also prophesy concerning Judah and several other nations? And why does Scripture mention the earthquake? And why does the prophecy begin, **"And he said: Hashem will roar from Zion,"** and afterward say, **"Thus says Hashem"**? This does not appear to follow the usual style of prophetic expression. In my opinion, the words **"And he said: Hashem will roar from Zion"** continue the description of Amos. That is, the meaning is: This is Amos, who saw concerning Israel and said that **"Hashem will roar from Zion."** For Amos was not prepared for prophecy, nor was he established in the eyes of the people as a prophet. This was so both because of who he was—a man among the herdsmen, a shepherd of sheep and a dresser of sycamore trees—and because of his place of origin, since he was from Tekoa, where there were no disciples of the prophets and no prophet who could ordain him and testify concerning him that he was a true prophet. Nevertheless, he became established as a true prophet because, two years before the earthquake, he prophesied and said in his prophecy that **"Hashem will roar from Zion,"** referring to the earthquake that would come from Zion. Likewise, at that time he prophesied concerning the cessation of rainfall that would occur then, as it is written, **"and the pastures of the shepherds shall mourn."** He prophesied this two years before the earthquake, and his prophecy came to pass two years after he had uttered it. Through this, all Israel knew that he was a trustworthy prophet of Hashem. (As for the statement that he prophesied in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, I explained at the beginning of Hosea that two years before the earthquake, Uzziah was subordinate to the kings of Israel, and the kingdom was therefore called by the name of the kingdom of Israel. This is why Jeroboam is mentioned; see there.)

**ויאמר** כבר פירשתי ששיעור הכתוב **אשר חזה ויאמר שנתים לפני הרעש,** אז נבא על הרעש, **שה' ישאג מציון,** כי הרעש התחיל מציון ושם היה שאגה גדולה, **ומירושלים** העיר **יתן קולו** שהוא קטן משאגה, כי הרעש התפשט מציון לירושלים ומשם לכל ערי א"י ונתן קולו קול עוז, שהוא קול הרעש אשר הרגיז ארץ ממקומה ועמודיה יתפלצון, זה נבואה אחת שנבא קודם בואה. נבואה ב' הודיע כי יהיה שנת בצורת עד כי **יאבלו נאות הרועים** כי לא יהיה מרעה לבהמות, **ויבש ראש הכרמל** ששם מלא תבואה לחה תמיד. כ"ז נבא שתי שנים קודם ונתקיימה נבואתו והוחזק לנביא נאמן:


**And he said.** I have already explained that the meaning of the verse is: **"which he saw and said two years before the earthquake."** At that time, he prophesied concerning the earthquake: **"Hashem will roar from Zion,"** for the earthquake began in Zion, and there was a great roaring there; **"and from Jerusalem He will give forth His voice,"** which is less powerful than a roar, for the earthquake spread from Zion to Jerusalem and from there to all the cities of the Land of Israel. He gave forth His mighty voice—the sound of the earthquake that shook the earth from its place and caused its pillars to tremble. This was one prophecy that he uttered before its fulfillment. A second prophecy announced that there would be a year of drought, so severe that **"the pastures of the shepherds shall mourn,"** for there would be no pasture for the animals, **"and the summit of Carmel shall wither,"** though it is always filled with fresh produce. All this he prophesied two years beforehand, and when his prophecy was fulfilled, he became established as a trustworthy prophet.

**כה אמר ה',** אחר שספר תארי הנביא ואיך הוחזק לנביא נאמן, מתחיל גוף הספר, וטרם יתחיל נבואותיו על יהודה ועשרת השבטים הקדים נבואת פורעניות על שכני א"י אשר הרעו לישראל, והודיע כי יענישם ה' בעבור זה, שהגם שישראל לקו בעונם בכל זאת לא ינקו כל הנוגעים בם לרעה כמ"ש וגם את הגוי אשר יעבדו דן אנכי, והנה ארם היה שכן לא"י בצד מזרח ופלשתים במערב וצור בצפון ואדום ועמון ומואב בדרום ויודיע ענשם זאח"ז, והתחיל מארם שהם הריעו לישראל תחלה מן ימי אלישע עד ימי ירבעם בן יואש כמבואר בספר מלכים, ואמר **על שלשה פשעי דמשק ועל ארבעה** הכי **לא אשיבנו** את גמולו? אחר שחטאו שלשה פשעים וגם הפשע הרביעי הלא בהכרח שאענישם ואשוב גמולם להם, אולם מה הן השלשה פשעים פי' הרד"ק שלש פעמים שהרעו לישראל תחלה, והרי"א פי' שר"ל שלשה פשעים הידועים שהם ע"ז וג"ע וש"ד שהיה ביניהם, ואמר שהגם שלא השבתי גמולם על שלשת הפשעים הכי עתה שפשעו גם פשע הרביעי הכי לא אשיבנו? והפשע הרביעי הוא **על דושם בחרוצות הברזל את הגלעד,** שהיו מעבירים על גופם חריצות ברזל כמו שדשים את התבואה ליסרם באכזריות חמה ושטף אף:


**Thus says Hashem.** After describing the characteristics of the prophet and how he became established as a trustworthy prophet, the main body of the book begins. Before beginning his prophecies concerning Judah and the Ten Tribes, he first introduces prophecies of punishment against the neighbors of the Land of Israel who had harmed Israel, and announces that God would punish them for this. For although Israel was punished for its sins, nevertheless, all those who harmed them would not go unpunished, as it is written, "And also the nation whom they shall serve, I will judge." Now Aram was a neighbor of the Land of Israel to the east, the Philistines to the west, Tyre to the north, and Edom, Ammon, and Moab to the south, and he announces their punishments one after another. He begins with Aram, because they were the first to harm Israel, from the days of Elisha until the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, as explained in the Book of Kings. He said, **"For three transgressions of Damascus, and for four, shall I not requite him?"** After they committed three transgressions, and then the fourth transgression as well, must I not necessarily punish them and repay them for their deeds? But what are these three transgressions? Radak explains that they refer to the three times that they had previously harmed Israel. Abarbanel explains that they refer to the three well-known transgressions—idolatry, sexual immorality, and bloodshed—which existed among them. Thus, He says: Although I did not repay them for the three transgressions, now that they have committed a fourth transgression as well, **shall I not requite them?** And the fourth transgression was **"because they threshed Gilead with threshing sledges of iron."** They passed iron implements over their bodies, just as grain is threshed, torturing them with cruel fury and an outpouring of wrath.

### Verse 1

###### Malbim on Amos 2:1:1

**על שלשה פשעי מואב** פי' מהרי"א שהתחיל נבואתו על דמשק שנלחמו בגלעד באכזריות חמה, ואח"כ בעזה וצור שעזרו לאדום, ואח"כ באדום עצמו שהרעו לישראל, ואחר זה נבא שיעניש את האומות שהיו סבה לכל זאת, שבני עמון היו הסבה לרעה שעשו דמשק, כי הם התחילו ללחום בגלעד ולקחת את גבולה ועי"כ ערב לב דמשק לבא אחריהם להשמיד ולהחרים, והסבה לרעת בני אדום שנגררו אחריהם פלשת וצור, היה מה **ששרפו עצמות מלך אדום לשיד** כי אדום היה בברית עם ישראל, ובימי יהורם ויהושפט הלך אדום עמהם להלחם עם מואב, ואז תפס מלך מואב את בן הבכור של מלך אדום אשר ימלוך תחתיו (ולכן קראו פה מלך אדום) ויעלהו עולה על החומה וישרפהו לשיד, ושם כתיב ויהי קצף על ישראל, שפי' שאז קצף אדום על ישראל על שלא הצילוהו מיד מלך מואב, ומאז התחיל אדום ללחום עם ישראל וכמ"ש (במלכים ב ח׳:ט״ז) שבימי יהורם בן יהושפט (שמלך בשנת חמש ליורם בן אחאב) פשע אדום מתחת יהודה, ומאז התחיל להרע לישראל ושמר לו איבת נצח, ומואב היה הסבה לזה ולכן נענש, ועז"א (ירמיהו מ״ח:ל״ה) במפלת מואב והשבתי למואב מעלה במה ומקטיר לאלהיו, שזה מדה כנגד מדה:


**For three transgressions of Moab.** Abarbanel explains that he began his prophecy with Damascus, who fought against Gilead with fierce cruelty, and afterward with Gaza and Tyre, who aided Edom, and afterward with Edom itself, who harmed Israel. After this, he prophesied that He would punish the nations that had been the cause of all this. The children of Ammon were the cause of the evil committed by Damascus, for they were the first to wage war against Gilead and seize its territory, and thereby Damascus was emboldened to follow after them to destroy and annihilate. And the cause of the evil committed by the children of Edom, whom Philistia and Tyre followed, was that **they burned the bones of the king of Edom into lime.** For Edom had been in a covenant with Israel, and in the days of Jehoram and Jehoshaphat, Edom went with them to fight against Moab. At that time, the king of Moab captured the firstborn son of the king of Edom, who was destined to reign after him (and therefore he is called here the king of Edom), offered him as a burnt offering upon the wall, and burned him into lime. There it is written, "And there was great wrath against Israel," meaning that Edom then became enraged against Israel for not saving him from the hand of the king of Moab. From that time onward, Edom began to wage war against Israel, as it is written (II Kings 8:16) that in the days of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat (who became king in the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab), Edom rebelled from under the rule of Judah. From then onward, Edom began to harm Israel and maintained an everlasting enmity against them. Moab was the cause of this and was therefore punished. Concerning this it says (Jeremiah 48:35), in the prophecy of Moab's downfall, "And I will put an end in Moab to him who offers upon the high place and burns incense to his god," this being measure for measure.

**Will a lion roar?** As a second introduction, when a lion roars in the forest, it certainly has prey and does not roar for nothing. This serves as an introduction to what he will say in verse 12: the king of Assyria, who roars against them, will seize his prey, devour men, and no one will deliver them from his hand. It is already explained in accounts of natural history that when a lion is young, while it is in the stage called a *kefir*, it does not go out in search of its prey, but sits in its lair in ambush to devour animals that pass by it. Therefore, it says, **"Does a young lion give forth its voice from its den?"** But when it grows old and is called an *aryeh*, it goes far from its den to seize prey. Therefore, it says, **"Will a lion roar in the forest?"** The metaphor is that both when Assyria roared against them in their own land, and when it captured one of them in its own den—when Hoshea son of Elah went out to him and he imprisoned him, so that he was caught by him as one is caught by a young lion, for he had then entered the territory of Assyria—in both cases, there was no one who could deliver from his hand.

**And I will strike the winter house.** The rebels destroyed the king's house, both his winter house and his summer house, and **the houses of ivory shall perish,** which belonged to the king and the princes, and thereby **many houses shall come to an end,** whether through the turmoil among themselves, in which they destroyed many houses, as it is also written (Isaiah 9:20), "And the people shall be as fuel for the fire, etc.; Manasseh against Ephraim, and Ephraim against Manasseh," or because, as a result of this, they informed against their king to the king of Assyria, who imprisoned him when he went out to him. This was the snare that led to the trap, for through this snare he fell into his net, and as a result the king of Assyria afterward came up, besieged Samaria, captured it, and exiled Israel. Thus, the sin of idolatry that was in their hands was the primary snare by which the bird—Ephraim and the tribes of Israel—was caught.

**Come to Beth-el.** Now he begins to explain the fourth introduction, concerning what he said, "If a shofar is sounded in a city, will the people not tremble?" He relates how Hashem commanded the prophets to sound the shofar, declare their transgression to them, and inform them of the punishment destined to come upon them, yet they did not tremble. The prophets said to them in the name of Hashem, **"Come to Beth-el and transgress,"** etc., **"and I also gave you cleanness of teeth."** Just as you increase your transgressions, so will I increase your punishment. The word *"proclaim"* in the following verse applies to the entire passage, and its meaning is: **"Proclaim: Come to Beth-el and transgress."** You shall proclaim, announce, and command the people to come to Beth-el and transgress against Hashem, **and to come to Gilgal,** and there **increase transgression** even more, for in Gilgal they sinned more than in Beth-el (as it is written in Hosea 9:15, "All their wickedness is in Gilgal," and there, 12:12, "In Gilgal they sacrificed bulls"). **"And bring your sacrifices every morning."** Proclaim that they should bring sacrifices to idolatry every morning, **and bring the tithes every three days.**

More than this, even within a single city, **one portion was rained upon** if the owner of the field had committed fewer transgressions or had repented, **and the portion upon which it did not rain withered.** For although, if rain fell upon one portion and rainwater collected there in cisterns, they could have taken water from there to irrigate the neighboring portion upon which rain had not fallen, it would not be so, for the rain would fall in a precisely measured amount sufficient only for that particular portion, and no water would remain to irrigate the neighboring portion, so that the second portion would wither.

**לכן,** אחר שפי' מ"ש אם יתקע שופר בעיר ועם לא יחרדו, וכן מ"ש אם תהיה רעה בעיר וה' לא עשה, כי ברר להם שכל הרעות האלה אינם מקריים רק השגחיים ע"י עונותיהם, באר מ"ש כי לא יעשה ה' דבר כי אם גלה סודו אל עבדיו הנביאים, שה' אינו עושה את הרע בהחלט, כי תכלית מה שגוזר את הרעה הוא כדי שישובו בתשובה ע"י תוכחת הנביאים ועי"כ תובטל הגזרה ולא יביא את הרע, וז"ש **לכן כה אעשה לך ישראל** ר"ל מה שכה אעשה לך כל הרעות הנאמרות אינו כדי להביא הרע, רק שתכלית גזרת הפורעניות הוא כדי **שעקב כי זאת אעשה לך** שע"י שתדע כי אעשה לך הרעה הזאת עי"כ **הכון לקראת אלהיך ישראל,** תשים אל לבך להכין את עצמך לקראתו ולפייסו בתשובה ומעשים טובים, ולא תמתין עד יבא עליך ויעשה לך הרעה החרוצה מלפניו, רק צא לקראתו קודם שיבא אליך וזאת יעשה לך, ופייס אותו ושובה אליו כדי שלא יעשה הרעה כי באמת לא יעשה דבר כי אם גלה סודו אל עבדיו הנביאים, וגם רמז עוד בדרך הדרוש כונה שניה. שלא לבד שאין חפץ ה' להביא עליך רעה כי בהפך חפצו רק להטיב ולהשפיע טוב וחסד, ובמה שאתה מכריחו להרע עמך ע"י מעשיך הרעים, אתה מכריחו נגד רצונו עד שגם ע"ז תקבל עונש, מה שסבבת שיעשה רע נגד חפצו להיטיב, כענין מ"ש שכר עבירה עבירה שהעונש שמקבלין על העבירה זה עצמו עבירה וגם ע"ז יקבלו עונש, וז"ש **זאת אעשה לך עקב כי זאת אעשה לך,** שעל זה בעצמו שזאת אעשה לך ע"ז עצמו תענש ואעשה לך רע על מה שהכרחת אותי לעשות רע, ולכן הכון לקראת אלהיך המשגיח עליך בפרטות:


**Therefore.** After explaining what was meant by, "If a shofar is sounded in a city, will the people not tremble?" and likewise by, "Can there be calamity in a city that Hashem has not caused?" for He made clear to them that all these calamities were not matters of chance but acts of Divine Providence brought about by their sins, he now explains what was meant by, "For Hashem God does nothing unless He has revealed His secret to His servants the prophets." Hashem does not bring about evil as an absolute end in itself, for the purpose of His decreeing calamity is that they should repent through the rebukes of the prophets, and thereby the decree will be annulled and He will not bring the calamity. This is the meaning of **"Therefore, thus will I do to you, O Israel,"** meaning that what I will thus do to you—all the calamities that have been mentioned—is not for the purpose of bringing evil; rather, the purpose of the decree of punishment is that **"because I will do this to you,"** through your knowing that I will bring this calamity upon you, you will thereby **"prepare to meet your God, O Israel."** You will take it to heart to prepare yourself to meet Him and appease Him through repentance and good deeds. Do not wait until He comes upon you and brings upon you the calamity decreed by Him; rather, go out to meet Him before He comes to you and does this to you, and appease Him and return to Him so that He will not bring the calamity, for in truth, "He does nothing unless He has revealed His secret to His servants the prophets." He also alludes, by way of homiletical interpretation, to a second meaning: not only does Hashem have no desire to bring calamity upon you, but, on the contrary, His desire is only to do good and bestow goodness and kindness. By compelling Him to deal harshly with you through your evil deeds, you compel Him to act against His will, to the extent that you will receive punishment even for this—for having caused Him to do evil contrary to His desire to do good. This is similar to the saying, "The reward of a transgression is a transgression": the punishment received for the transgression is itself a transgression, and for this too they will receive punishment. This is the meaning of **"This I will do to you because I will do this to you":** for the very fact that I must do this to you, you will be punished, and I will bring evil upon you for having compelled Me to do evil. Therefore, **"prepare to meet your God,"** who exercises particular Providence over you.

**כי הנה יוצר הרים.** מביא ראיה על השגחתו, א] מתחלת הבריאה שבתחלה היה העולם מים במים ויסוד המים כסה כל כדור הארץ, ובארנו בפי' מעשה בראשית שתחלה היה יסוד העפר בלול ביסוד המים כמ"ש וחשך על פני תהום, ויסוד הרוח היה בלול ביסוד המים למעלה וע"כ התפשטו המים וכסו כל יסוד העפר, ע"י כח ההתפשטות אשר ליסוד האויר כמ"ש ורוח אלהים מרחפת על פני המים, וע"י האור שברא הקב"ה הפריש יסוד הרוח מיסוד המים שיהיו לבדו, ועי"כ נצטמצמו המים ונראו ראשי ההרים ונתגלה היבשה, וכמ"ש יקוו המים, וז"ש **כי הנה יוצר הרים ובורא רוח,** שנפרש יסוד העפר לבדו ויסוד הרוח לבדו, וזה מצד השגחתו הכוללת שתהיה הארץ מוכנת לישוב הבע"ח והאדם, וכן השגחתו מתמדת על האדם בפרטות, כי **הוא מגיד לאדם מה שחו** שלא לבד שידע דבור האדם ומעשיו שעושה בכונה, כי גם השיח שהוא התנועות או הדבורים הנפלטים ע"י רגשת הנפש שלא בכונה שהאדם עצמו אינו יודע מהם, הוא מגיד לו מה ששח ברגשת נפשו, וכמו שבהשגחתו תכן את הטבע והבריאה ולשבת יצרה, כן ברצותו יכול להרסה ולהחריבה שאז **עושה שחר עיפה** יחשיך השחר ויבטל האור שברא בראשית הבריאה שעל ידו תכן את הרוח (כמו שבארתי בפי' בראשית) וזה הפך של בורא רוח, **ודורך על במתי ארץ** להשקיע ההרים בתהומות מים כמו שהיה תחלה תהו ובהו וזה הפך של יוצר הרים, **כי ה' צבאות שמו** שהוא מושל על כל צבאות הבריאה וכחותיה:


**For behold, He forms mountains.** He brings proof of His Providence: First, from the beginning of creation, when initially the world was water within water, and the element of water covered the entire globe. We explained in our commentary on the account of Creation that initially the element of earth was mingled with the element of water, as it is written, "And darkness was upon the face of the deep," while the element of air was mingled with the element of water above. Therefore, the waters spread out and covered the entire element of earth through the expansive power possessed by the element of air, as it is written, "And the spirit of God hovered over the face of the waters." Through the light that the Holy One, blessed be He, created, He separated the element of air from the element of water so that it would exist independently, and as a result the waters contracted, the tops of the mountains became visible, and the dry land was revealed, as it is written, "Let the waters be gathered together." This is the meaning of **"For behold, He forms mountains and creates the wind,"** for the element of earth was separated to exist by itself and the element of air by itself. This was through His general Providence, so that the earth would be prepared for habitation by animals and man. Likewise, His Providence continually watches over man individually, for **"He declares to man what his thought is."** Not only does He know a person's speech and deeds that are performed intentionally, but even his *siach*—the movements or words that escape through an emotional stirring of the soul without intention, of which the person himself is unaware—He reveals to him what he uttered through the stirring of his soul. And just as through His Providence He established nature and creation and formed the earth to be inhabited, so, when He wills, He can destroy and lay it waste. Then **"He makes the dawn into darkness,"** darkening the dawn and nullifying the light that He created at the beginning of creation, through which He established the air (as I explained in my commentary on Genesis); this is the opposite of **"creates the wind."** And **"He treads upon the high places of the earth,"** sinking the mountains into the watery depths, as it was originally when the earth was formless and void; this is the opposite of **"forms mountains."** **For Hashem of Hosts is His name,** for He rules over all the hosts of creation and all its powers.

**נפלה,** כשיפול האדם נפילה בלתי מסוכנת יקום בעצמו מנפילתו, וכשיפול נפילה מסוכנת עד שאינו יכול לקום בעצמו אז ימהרו קרוביו ומיודעיו להקימו, אולם **בתולת ישראל נפלה** נפילה מסוכנת עד **שלא תוסיף קום,** שא"א שתקום בעצמה, ולפ"ז הלא מהראוי שימהרו קרוביה ובניה להקימה מנפילתה. ובכ"ז **נטשה על אדמתה ואין מקימה** לא נמצא מי שיקים אותה, הגם שהיא עדיין על אדמתה אין לה מקים. והנמשל כי בימים הראשונים גם אם נפלו התחזקו אח"כ וקמו בעצמם ויתאזרו חיל. וגם בעת שלא היה לאל ידם לקום בעצמם ראה ה' בענים והקים אותם כמו שהיה בימי ירבעים בן יואש שראה ה' את עני ישראל ורחם עליהם. ועתה אין לה כח בעצמה וגם ה' נטש אותה ואין לה מקים:


**She has fallen.** When a person suffers a fall that is not dangerous, he rises from the fall by himself; and when he suffers a dangerous fall to the point that he cannot rise by himself, his relatives and acquaintances will hasten to raise him up. But **the virgin of Israel has fallen** with a dangerous fall, to the point that **she shall rise no more,** for she cannot rise by herself. Accordingly, it would be proper for her relatives and children to hasten to raise her from her fall. Nevertheless, **she is forsaken upon her land, with none to raise her up;** no one can be found to raise her, even though she is still upon her own land, she has no one to raise her. The metaphor is that in earlier times, even when they fell, they afterward strengthened themselves, rose by their own power, and girded themselves with strength. And even when they lacked the power to rise by themselves, Hashem saw their affliction and raised them up, as occurred in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, when Hashem saw the affliction of Israel and had compassion upon them. But now she has no strength of her own, and Hashem has also forsaken her, and she has no one to raise her up.

Justice moves upon the earth to preserve civilized life, so that people may live lives that are sweet and pleasant, protecting society from oppression and robbery and enabling them to live in peace and tranquility; but they **turn justice into wormwood.** And righteousness takes wing toward heaven, through which the soul attains happiness, since it is a daughter of God, spiritual and Divine; but they **cast righteousness down to the earth,** for they regard it as an earthly, material thing of no importance whatsoever, since they do not believe in the immortality or holiness of the soul.

And just as they overturned the order of His governance, so will He overturn the order of the governance of the world to destroy them from the face of the earth.

(16–17) **Therefore, thus says Hashem.** Since you do not return to Hashem, **there shall be lamentation in all the streets, for I will pass through your midst.** This serves as an introduction to what is said afterward, "Woe to those who desire the day of Hashem." It had been received among them from the prophets that there is a day appointed for Hashem, when Hashem will reveal Himself in His glory to punish the nations that harmed Israel, called "the great and awesome day of Hashem," and that at that time Israel will be saved with an everlasting salvation. Joel had already prophesied (chapter 3), "The sun shall be turned into darkness and the moon into blood before the coming of the great and awesome day of Hashem." Israel desired and awaited the coming of that day, when Hashem would pass through their midst, for then He would bring all the nations down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat and judge them for the evil they had done to Israel. The prophet told them that they were mistaken in this matter, for the day of Hashem that will come for Israel's benefit will be when Israel is perfect, righteous, and worthy of salvation; but the day of Hashem that will come now, when Hashem passes through their midst, will be for them a day of darkness and gloom, for He will pass through their midst to destroy and exile them (in the manner of, "And Hashem will pass through to strike Egypt"). This is the meaning of what he says: now, **when I pass through your midst, there shall be lamentation in all the streets.** The *rehov* is the broad place before the houses where the people gather, while the *hutzot* are the narrow places behind the houses, where only the inhabitants of the alley and courtyard are found, namely the women and children. He thus depicts everyone gathering in the broad street to lament, while the women and children standing **in the narrow streets shall answer** the lamentation with **"Alas! Alas!" And they shall call the farmer to mourning,** for it was customary first to lament and afterward to observe mourning, as it is written, "And there they lamented with a very great and grievous lamentation, and he observed mourning for his father seven days." During the days of mourning, people cease from work, and therefore the farmers who worked the land did not participate in the people's mourning, so that they would not be kept from their work in the fields. But to this mourning they will call even the farmer, for in any case the land will lie desolate and there will be no work for them in the field. **And they shall call those skilled in lamentation to wailing;** for the sake of the lamentation they will call those who know how to raise a bitter cry with expressions of "Alas!" and "Woe!" to intensify the bitterness of the lamentation. And **in all the vineyards there shall be lamentation,** whereas until now they had celebrated in the vineyards, rejoicing and dancing in circles. All this will occur **when I pass through your midst.**

### Verse 1

###### Malbim on Amos 6:1:1

**Woe to those who are at ease in Zion.** This entire passage continues from what is said in verse 7, **"Therefore, now they shall go into exile at the head of the exiles,"** meaning that Israel went into exile first, and afterward all the neighboring nations went into exile, so that Israel was the first of all the exiles. This was so both in the exile of the Ten Tribes by the king of Assyria—for although the king of Assyria also exiled the other nations, as it is written, "Is not Calno like Carchemish? Is not Hamath like Arpad? Is not Samaria like Damascus?" (Isaiah 10:9), it is clear from this that he did not exile Calno, Hamath the Great, and the Philistines until after he had first exiled the Ten Tribes, so that at the time the Ten Tribes went into exile, the countries of Calno, Hamath, and Eden were still dwelling upon their land. (Likewise, we find concerning Nebuchadnezzar that he did not exile the neighboring nations of Edom, Ammon, Moab, and the Philistines until after he had exiled Judah, so that Israel was always the first of the exiles.) The prophet wonders why this was so, for according to the natural course of events, he should first have conquered and exiled the smaller and weaker kingdoms and only afterward conquered Samaria or Zion, which were mighty and fortified kingdoms. This is the meaning of **"Woe,"** I call out: are not **those who are at ease in Zion and those who trust in the mountain of Samaria** secure? There is a distinction between being at ease and trusting: one who is at ease has no war at all and no external threat to disturb him, whereas one who trusts relies upon his strength even though he is at war and is not at ease. Now the inhabitants of Zion had no war at all at that time, for the king of Assyria did not come against Judah until the fourteenth year of Hezekiah, while the exile of Samaria occurred in the sixth year of Hezekiah. Therefore, he called them "those who are at ease." **השמחים ללא דבר,** הנה (במלכים ב י״ד:כ״ה) אמר על ירבעם בן יואש שהוא השיב את גבול ישראל מלבא חמת עד ים הערבה כדבר ה' אשר דבר ביד עבדו יונה בן אמתי אשר מגת החפר כי ראה ה' את עני ישראל מורה מאד וכו' ולא דבר ה' למחות את שם ישראל מתחת השמים ויושיעם ביד ירבעם בן יואש, ור"ל שמה שהצליח ירבעם בעת ההיא להשיב את גבול ישראל, הגם שידע ה' שהם עתידים לגלות מפני עונותיהם, הוא מפני שאז לא נחרץ עדיין גז"ד, שהגז"ד על הגלות לא נחרץ עד ימי עמוס שאז נחרצה הגזרה שיגלו, וכמ"ש עמ"ש (ישעיהו ז׳:ח׳) ובעוד ששים וחמש שנה יחת אפרים מעם שהוא מן העת שנבא עמוס שישראל גלה יגלה מעל אדמתו שאז נחרץ גזר דינם שיגלה אחר ס"ה שנה, ואחר שבימי ירבעם בן יואש עדיין לא נגזרה הגזרה, שעז"א ולא דבר ה' למחות את שם ישראל, דהינו שלא דבר הגזר דין עדיין אז, וראה שהם מעונים מאד ואם יניחם ביד הטבע והמקרה יכלו מעצמם ולא היה עדיין זמן לזה, לכן הוכרח להושיעם ביד ירבעם בן יואש, והדור ההוא ששמעו נבואה זו מפי יונה בן אמתי שהגיד להם שמה שהושיעם הוא מפני שה' לא דבר לכלותם, **שמחו על לא דבר,** היינו על שלא יצא דבר וגזרה עליהם, ועפ"ז חשבו אחר שמה שהתגברו על האויב והושב גבול ישראל מלבא חמת עד ים הערבה לא היה ע"י שה' דבר, רק ע"י שה' לא דבר, א"כ התשועה לא היתה בדרך נס מאת ה' כי לא הושיעם בפועל ע"י דבור שדבר לטובתם, רק היה ע"י שלא דבר רע עליהם, ועי"כ נושעו מעצמם ע"י כח ירבעם במלחמה, ועי"ז חשבו שנצחו בחזקתם וגבורתם, ואמרו **הלא בחזקנו לקחנו לנו קרנים** שמה שנצחנו היה ע"י חזקנו וגבורתנו, ולכן יעשה ה' עמהם מדה כנגד מדה:


**Who rejoice over no word.** Behold, concerning Jeroboam son of Joash, it is written (II Kings 14:25) that "he restored the border of Israel from the entrance of Hamath to the Sea of the Arabah, according to the word of Hashem, the God of Israel, which He spoke through His servant Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet who was from Gath-hepher. For Hashem saw the affliction of Israel, that it was very bitter, etc., and Hashem had not spoken of blotting out the name of Israel from under heaven, and He saved them by the hand of Jeroboam son of Joash." This means that Jeroboam's success at that time in restoring the border of Israel, even though Hashem knew that they were destined to go into exile because of their iniquities, was because the final decree had not yet been issued. The final decree of exile was not issued until the days of Amos, when it was decreed that they would go into exile. As I explained concerning the verse (Isaiah 7:8), "And within sixty-five years Ephraim shall be shattered from being a people," this period begins from the time when Amos prophesied, "Israel shall surely go into exile from its land," for at that time their judgment was finally decreed that they would go into exile after sixty-five years. Since in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash the decree had not yet been issued, concerning which it says, "And Hashem had not spoken of blotting out the name of Israel," meaning that He had not yet spoken the final decree at that time, and since He saw that they were greatly afflicted and that, if He left them to nature and chance, they would perish by themselves even though the time for this had not yet arrived, He was therefore compelled to save them through Jeroboam son of Joash. That generation, which heard this prophecy from Jonah son of Amittai, who told them that they had been saved because Hashem had not spoken of destroying them, **rejoiced over "no word,"** meaning over the fact that no word or decree had been issued against them. Accordingly, they reasoned that since their victory over the enemy and the restoration of Israel's border from the entrance of Hamath to the Sea of the Arabah had come about not because Hashem had spoken, but because Hashem had not spoken, their salvation had therefore not occurred miraculously through Hashem. For He had not actively saved them through a word spoken for their benefit; rather, it had come about because He had not spoken evil against them, and thus they had saved themselves through Jeroboam's military power. Consequently, they thought that they had triumphed through their own strength and might, and they said, **"Have we not by our own strength taken horns for ourselves?"** meaning that their victory had been achieved through their own strength and might. Therefore, Hashem will deal with them measure for measure.

**כה הראני ה',** כבר אמר למעלה (סי' ד') שה' העניש אותם בחמשה מיני עונשים כדי שישובו בתשובה והם לא שבו אל ה', ושלשה עונשים הראשונים היו ע"י רעב שהביא עליהם ושנים האחרונים היו ע"י דבר וחרב, ואחר שבכל אלה לא שבו היה מדת הדין מתוחה כנגדן לכלותם, כמש"ש לכן זאת אעשה לך ישראל וכו' וכמו שפרשתי שם, וספר פה איך הראהו ה' את העונשים יוצאים מכח אל הפועל, ונצטיירו בציורים שונים במחזה הנבואה, ותחלה הראהו ה' עונשי הרעב, שחשב למעלה שהיה תחלה מניעת הגשם בעוד שלשה חדשים לקציר ואח"כ היה שדפון וירקון ולבסוף בא הגזם שהוא מכת הארבה שהיה המכה האחרונה בגזרת הרעב. הראהו ה' **והנה יוצר גובי,** שהוא הארבה, **בתחלת עלות הלקש,** הלקש הוא המטר המאוחר הנקרא מלקוש, והתבואה הגדלה על ידי המלקוש נקראת לקש, וראה שכאשר התחיל הלקש לעלות ולגדל יצר ה' את הארבה להשחית את התבואה הגדלה, וספר שהלקש בא **אחר גזי המלך,** אחר העת שגוזזים את צאן המלך שהוא באמצע הקיץ, אז גדל הלקש, כי נתאחרה התבואה בעת ההיא מפני עצירת גשמים, ואז בא הארבה:


**Thus Hashem showed me.** It was already stated above (chapter 4) that Hashem punished them with five kinds of punishments in order that they should repent, yet they did not return to Hashem. The first three punishments were through famine, which He brought upon them, and the final two were through pestilence and the sword. And since through all these they did not repent, the attribute of justice was poised against them to destroy them, as it is written there, "Therefore, thus will I do to you, O Israel," etc., and as I explained there. Here he relates how Hashem showed him the punishments going forth from potential into actuality, depicted in various images in the prophetic vision. First, Hashem showed him the punishments of famine, which he mentioned above: first there was the withholding of rain three months before the harvest, afterward there was blight and mildew, and finally came the cutting locust, which was the plague of locusts and the final blow in the decree of famine. Hashem showed him, **and behold, He was forming locusts,** **at the beginning of the growth of the latter crop.** The *lekesh* is the late rain called the *malkosh*, and the crop that grows through the *malkosh* is called *lekesh*. He saw that when the *lekesh* began to sprout and grow, Hashem formed the locusts to destroy the growing crop. And he relates that the *lekesh* came **after the king's mowings,** after the time when the king's sheep are shorn, which is in the middle of summer. Then the *lekesh* grew, for the crop had been delayed at that time because of the withholding of rain, and then the locusts came.

First, a wall built with a plumb line, meaning a wall constructed according to precise measure and balance. This is an image of the entirety of creation that Hashem created, which is the wall encompassing all existence, built according to the attribute of justice and the plumb line of judgment. For the scales and balances of justice are extended over all existence: everything was created according to justice, and everything is ordered by the attribute of justice and general Providence, through which Hashem oversees all existence with justice and judgment. Hashem stands upon this wall, overseeing it through general Providence according to justice. Second, he saw that **there was a plumb line in His hand.** Although the building had already been completed, He nevertheless holds the plumb line in His hand to measure anew. This means that, beyond the general Providence established from the six days of Creation, He exercises at every moment individual Providence to punish sinners through special Providence, according to the particular judgment appropriate to the time and to those being judged.



### Verse 8

###### Malbim on Amos 7:8:1

**ויאמר ה' אלי מה אתה רואה עמוס,** באשר ראה שני דברים, חומת אנך, והאנך אשר ביד ה', שאל אותו מה אתה רואה, ר"ל מה הדבר אשר הוא חידוש בעיניו במחזה הזאת, ואומר אנך ר"ל החומה של אנך אינה לפלא בעיני כי ידעתי שהכל ערוך במדה ובמשקל ובמדת הדין, רק אני רואה ומתפלא על האנך אשר בידך שאתה אוחז אחר גמר הבנין, שזה מורה שאתה רוצה להעניש איזה אומה במשפט הפרטי ובהשגחה המיוחדת אשר אין לה קשר עם המשפט הכללי המיוסד בכלל המציאות זה אני רואה ומדקדק עליו, **ויאמר ה' הנני שם אנך בקרב עמי ישראל,** ר"ל שלא אניח אותם שישפטו על פי קו הדין הכללי שבו לא ישקיף על הפרט, לשפוט כל איש ואיש ביחוד רק דן את כל העולם בכלל, וכן מצד הדין הכללי יעבור לפעמים על העון והעונש אם אין הזמן הכללי מסכים אל הרעה, כי בצד הדין הכללי תבא הרעה לפי הסדרים הטבעיים והערך המערכיי הקצוב בהנהגה הכללית וימתין על העת המוכשר לזה כפי סדר העולם וכפי המערכת, אבל אני שם אנך חדש ומדה מיוחדת המודדת כפי ההשגחה הפרטיית, וזה יבדל מן האנך הכללי של חומת אנך בשני דברים, א] **שאני שם אנך בקרב עמי ישראל** לשפוט כל איש ואיש ביחוד ע"י ההשגחה האישיית ומשפט הפרטי, ב] **שלא אוסיף עוד עבור לו,** שאף שלא יוכשר הזמן אל העונש לפי סדר העולם הטבעי לא אעבור את העונש לזמן המוכשר לזה, כי אמדוד להם כפעלם ע"פ ההשגחה הפרטיית:


**And Hashem said to me, "What do you see, Amos?"** Since he saw two things—the wall built with a plumb line and the plumb line in Hashem's hand—He asked him, "What do you see?" That is, what in this vision appears novel and remarkable in your eyes? **And I said, "A plumb line."** That is, the wall built with a plumb line is not remarkable in my eyes, for I know that everything is ordered by measure and weight and according to the attribute of justice. Rather, what I see and find remarkable is the plumb line in Your hand, which You are holding after the building has already been completed. This indicates that You intend to punish a certain nation according to particular judgment and special Providence, which has no connection with the general judgment established over existence as a whole. This is what I see and carefully observe. **And Hashem said, "Behold, I am placing a plumb line in the midst of My people Israel."** That is, I will not leave them to be judged according to the general rule of justice, which does not look to the individual to judge each person separately, but rather judges the entire world collectively. Likewise, under general justice, sin and punishment may sometimes be passed over when the general circumstances are not suited to calamity, for according to general justice, calamity comes in accordance with the natural order and the fixed arrangement of the general governance of the world, and waits for the time suited to it according to the order of the world and the celestial configuration. But I am placing a new plumb line, a special measure that measures according to individual Providence. This differs from the general plumb line of the wall built with a plumb line in two respects: First, **"I am placing a plumb line in the midst of My people Israel,"** to judge each individual separately through individual Providence and particular judgment. Second, **"I will no longer pass over him,"** meaning that even if the time is not suited to punishment according to the natural order of the world, I will not postpone the punishment until a time more suited to it, but will measure out to them according to their deeds through individual Providence.

**And the songs of the temple shall become wailings on that day.** The songs that they sang in the temple of the idols will be transformed into wailing, for it was because of them that the calamity came. And the fact that they were placed in a cage, which is a vessel specifically used for placing dead birds, comes to allude to the fact that **many shall be the corpses,** as numerous as the carcasses of captured birds, so that they will lie **in every place.** Not all of them will remain in the cage, but they will be **cast out** from the cage, for he saw the cage empty, without birds. That is, their corpses will lie strewn through the streets. **Silence.** This word has no clear explanation, and it appears that the prophet had one more thing to reveal but feared to reveal it because of the danger, and therefore said, **"Silence,"** meaning that one must remain silent and not reveal the matter. What the prophet concealed here was later explained (Isaiah, chapter 7) in the words, "And within sixty-five years Ephraim shall be shattered from being a people." This means within sixty-five years from the time when Amos prophesied that "Israel shall surely go into exile from its land," for from that time until the exile of the Ten Tribes was sixty-five years. Yet nowhere in the words of Amos do we find that he explicitly specified the time, for he was commanded not to reveal it openly but only by allusion, and it was transmitted by tradition to the disciples of the prophets. He therefore said, **"Silence,"** meaning that one must remain silent, and he chose this word because its numerical value is sixty-five, thereby alluding that within sixty-five years, corresponding to the numerical value of *has*, this event would occur. The reason he did not explicitly reveal the time is that when a prophet foretells a future calamity without specifying a time, this does not greatly trouble the people, for they know that adverse prophecies can sometimes be changed or their fulfillment delayed when the people's conduct changes for the better, as it is written, "The vision that he sees is for many days hence" (Ezekiel 12). But if he specifies a definite time, this indicates that the time is near and that the event will not change under any circumstances. Hashem therefore did not wish him to say this openly, and he said the word *has*, "Silence," to indicate that there was a secret here about which he remained silent, while also alluding to the time through the numerical value of *has*. This was transmitted by tradition to the disciples of the prophets, and Isaiah, who lived in the days of Ahaz, when the time was already approaching, explained the matter: the word *has* means that within sixty-five years Ephraim would be shattered from being a people, and there would be many corpses everywhere.

I explained that Hoshea son of Elah sought to abolish the worship of the calves and removed the guards who had prevented Israel from going up on pilgrimage, and he cut off the horns of the altar. This caused them to inform against Hoshea before the king of Assyria, who imprisoned him and besieged Samaria, thereby bringing about their exile, as I have explained in various places in my commentary on Hosea. He depicts this as having occurred through the Providence of Hashem, as though **Hashem were standing upon the altar** at Beth-el, **and He said, "Strike the capital."** This refers to the lintel of the altar-house, upon which there was a kind of ornamental knob. Through this, **the thresholds shall shake,** and the altar-house will be destroyed. This is itself what was meant by "and the horns of the altar shall be cut off." **And cut them upon the head of them all;** through this, they were all cut apart and divided into different factions, and this occurred **at the beginning,** meaning at the beginning of their downfall. For **their end**, the ultimate result of the turmoil, will be that I shall kill them by the sword through the king of Assyria. **None who flees among them shall escape,** for they will not be saved through flight; and even one who **escapes** the enemy's sword **shall not be delivered,** for the sword will later overtake him, as he goes on to explain.

Likewise, through Hashem, **the whole of it rises like the Nile,** and the enemies cover it, as explained there above. All this occurred through Hashem's Providence.



### Verse 6

###### Malbim on Amos 9:6:1

**הבונה בשמים מעלותיו,** הוא עפמ"ש נוטה שמים כיריעה המקרה במים עליותיו וכו' יסד ארץ על מכוניה, והתבאר אצלי בפי' מעשה בראשית, כי אחר שתחלה היה העולם כולו מים במים ברצות ה' שתתגלה היבשה נטה שמים כיריעה, היינו מה ששם בטבע המים שיעלו באדים אל הרקיע שהוא עגול הנשימה, ושם נפרדו המים אשר היו מעורבים עם האויר ונשארו בעיגול הנשימה, כמ"ש המקרה במים עליותיו, שהוא החק ששם אל המים שיעלו עד שם ושם ישארו צרורים בעבים, ועי"ז נתגלה היבשה וההרים עלו מתוך התהום, שעז"א יסד ארץ על מכוניה, וז"ש **שבנה בשמים מעלותיו** וששם הוא עליית האדים והמים, ועי"כ **אגודתו על ארץ יסדה** נאגדה היבשה, ושם לים גבול בל יצא מן האגד ולחוץ, והוא בעצמו אשר עצר בעד המים בל יכסו היבשה, הוא **הקורא למי הים וישפכם על פני הארץ** ויחזור העולם שיהיה מים במים, כמו שעשה בדור המבול שירד הגשם מן השמים ויכסו המים את היבשה, בענין שהעושו יגיש חרבו להשיב את הבריאה אל רחם התהו:


**Who builds His upper chambers in the heavens.** This is based on what is written: "Who stretches out the heavens like a curtain; who lays the beams of His upper chambers in the waters, etc.; who founded the earth upon its foundations." As I explained in my commentary on the account of Creation, at first the entire world was water within water. When Hashem willed that the dry land should appear, He stretched out the heavens like a curtain, meaning that He established in the nature of water that it should rise as vapor to the firmament, which is the sphere of the atmosphere. There, the waters that had been mixed with the air were separated and remained within the sphere of the atmosphere, as it is written, "Who lays the beams of His upper chambers in the waters." This refers to the law He established for the waters, that they should rise to that point and remain there bound within the clouds. Through this, the dry land was revealed and the mountains rose out of the deep, concerning which it is written, "He founded the earth upon its foundations." This is the meaning of **"Who builds His upper chambers in the heavens,"** for there the vapors and waters ascend, and through this **"He founded His gathering upon the earth,"** for the dry land was gathered together, and He set a boundary for the sea so that it would not go beyond that gathering. And He Himself, who restrained the waters so that they would not cover the dry land, is **"He who calls for the waters of the sea and pours them out upon the face of the earth,"** so that the world returns to a state of water within water, as He did in the generation of the Flood, when rain descended from the heavens and the waters covered the dry land. Thus, He who made it can bring His sword near to return creation to the womb of chaos.

**הלוא כבני כושיים,** אחר שנבא פורעניות, סיים את ספרו בדברי תנחומין, אומר **אתם** מיוחדים **לי כבני כושיים** שהם מצויינים ונכרים תמיד ומיוחדים לאבותיהם ע"י שחרות עורם, שהגם שיגלו ויתערבו בין עמים אחרים בל יתערבו עמהם, כן אתם הגם שתגלו ותתערבו בין העמים תמיד תהיו נכרים כי אתם לי. כי אתם **בני ישראל,** וכל רואיהם יכירום כי הם בני אל חי, ומביא ראיה לזה **הלא את ישראל העליתי מארץ מצרים** שהגם שהייתם במצרים ימים רבים גולים ומשועבדים, בכ"ז לא התערבתם עם המצריים והעליתי את ישראל מופרד וניכר בשמו וביחוסו, אבל הכי **פלשתים** (העליתי) **מכפתור וארם מקיר** בתמיה? פלשתים שנכבשו בימים קדמונים ע"י הכפתורים כמ"ש והעוים היושבים בחצרים עד עזה כפתורים היוצאים מכפתור השמידום וישבו תחתם, ואח"כ חזרו הגולים לארץ פלשתים כמ"ש כי שודד ה' את פלשתים שארית אי כפתור, בכ"ז הכי העליתי את פלשתים, או כשאעלה את ארם מגלותם לקיר (שלשם הגלה אותם מלך אשור) וכי אעלה את ארם, הלא בגלותם יתערבו עם העמים ויתבטלו בתוכם ולא יהיו עוד פלשתים וארמים רק כפתורים ואנשי קיר, כי כבר חדלו מהיותם הגוי אשר היו מקודם שגלו, רק ישראל נשארו ישראל ולא התחתנו ולא התערבו עמהם, כמ"ש ונודע בגוים זרעם וכו', ובאר הדבר.


**Are you not like the children of the Cushites to Me?** After prophesying calamity, he concludes his book with words of consolation. He says: **You** are set apart **for Me like the children of the Cushites,** who are always distinctive and recognizable, and remain identified with their fathers because of the blackness of their skin. Although they may go into exile and mingle among other peoples, they do not become indistinguishable from them. So too, although you will go into exile and mingle among the nations, you will always remain recognizable, for you belong to Me. For you are **the children of Israel,** and all who see them will recognize them as children of the living God. He brings proof of this: **Did I not bring Israel up from the land of Egypt?** Although you lived in Egypt for many years as exiles and slaves, you nevertheless did not become assimilated among the Egyptians, and I brought Israel out as a separate and recognizable people, retaining its name and lineage. But did I likewise **bring the Philistines from Caphtor and Aram from Kir?** This is a rhetorical question. The Philistines had been conquered in ancient times by the Caphtorites, as it is written, "And the Avvim, who dwelt in villages as far as Gaza—the Caphtorites who came forth from Caphtor destroyed them and dwelt in their place." Later, the exiles returned to the land of the Philistines, as it is written, "For Hashem will destroy the Philistines, the remnant of the island of Caphtor." Yet can it be said that I brought up the Philistines? Or when I bring Aram back from its exile in Kir, to which the king of Assyria exiled them, can it truly be said that I bring up Aram? Surely, in their exile they will intermingle with the other peoples and disappear among them, and they will no longer remain Philistines and Arameans, but will become Caphtorites and people of Kir, for they will have ceased to be the nations they were before their exile. Only Israel remained Israel and did not intermarry or assimilate among them, as it is written, "And their offspring shall be known among the nations, etc." And he explains the matter.

**For behold, I command.** He explains the workings of Divine Providence in this matter: **He will shake them among all the nations,** so that they will wander, be scattered, and dispersed among all the peoples. This is what our Sages meant by the expression, "The Holy One, blessed be He, acted charitably toward Israel by scattering them among the nations," for through this they will not disappear in any one place, nor will any single nation be able to destroy them, since they will wander from nation to nation. Second, they will be like grain **shaken in a sieve.** Wheat and barley are placed in a sieve, and the sieve is shaken to cleanse them of the refuse. The fine waste falls through the holes of the sieve, but **the kernel,** meaning the full grains in which flour is contained, **shall not fall to the ground,** but will remain in the sieve. So too, through dispersion and exile, the wicked among them will fall away and become assimilated among the nations, whereas the righteous, who are bound in the bond of Torah, the commandments, and the unity of Israel, will not fall to the ground but will remain steadfast in their faith. He now explains who constitutes the refuse that will fall to the ground and pass out of the sieve.

Afterward, it was no longer a permanent house but only a temporary booth. This was during the Second Temple period, when the monarchy of the house of David had ceased and the Hasmoneans and Herod ruled. Nevertheless, there were still leaders descended from the house of David, and this was comparable to a temporary booth. Afterward, even the booth of David fell, for in this present exile the leaders too ceased to exist, and no trace of leadership remained among the descendants of the house of David. Then Hashem will raise up the booth of David, meaning that a man descended from the house of David will arise and lead the people in the manner of the former leaders. It has already been explained (Ezekiel 34:23–24) that the sun will rise for the house of David in three stages, as it is written there: **"He shall feed them, and he shall be their shepherd; and I, Hashem, shall be their God, and My servant David shall be a prince among them."** At first, the man from the house of David **shall feed them,** meaning that he will lead them and compel them to practice justice and righteousness. Through this, the booth of David will be raised up—that is, leadership from his descendants will be restored—but the booth will still remain in ruins. Afterward, however, Israel will accept the kingdom of Heaven and willingly accept his leadership, and then **he shall be their shepherd** by their own consent. Concerning this it says, **"I will close up its breaches."** Through the repairing of the breaches of the generation, **"I will raise up its ruins,"** and the booth will become whole rather than ruined, for they will willingly accept him as their shepherd. Afterward, the kingdom of Heaven will be revealed, as it is written, **"And I, Hashem, shall be their God,"** and then, **"My servant David shall be a prince among them."** He will be the Messiah. At that time it will no longer be a temporary booth but a permanent structure, and therefore it says, **"And I will build it as in the days of old,"** for the kingdom will once again be permanently established as it was formerly.
