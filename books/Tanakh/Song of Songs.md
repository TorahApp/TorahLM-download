# Song of Songs

שיר השירים

Link: [https://torahapp.org/share/book/Song%20of%20Songs](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Song%20of%20Songs)

שיח פיוטי בין נאהבים שנתפרש במסורת חז"ל כאלגוריה ליחסי עם ישראל עם אלוקיו.

Poetic conversations of two lovers, traditionally read as a metaphor for God and Israel.

###### Song of Songs 1:6

Do not gaze upon me, because I am black, because the sun has scorched me: my mother’s children were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards; but my own vineyard I have not kept.

Do not look at me [scornfully] because I am black; for the sun has scorched me [and I can return to my former beauty]. The sons of my mother [(among whom I was reared and who ascended with me from Egypt as the "mixed multitude”)] have taunted me [(with their idolatries)], making me a keeper of their vineyards [i.e., gods]; my own vineyards I did not keep.


###### Song of Songs 1:7

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Song%20of%20Songs/r/1:7](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Song%20of%20Songs/r/1:7)


הַגִּ֣ידָה לִּ֗י שֶׁ֤אָֽהֲבָה֙ נַפְשִׁ֔י אֵיכָ֣ה תִרְעֶ֔ה אֵיכָ֖ה תַּרְבִּ֣יץ בַּֽצׇּהֳרָ֑יִם שַׁלָּמָ֤ה אֶֽהְיֶה֙ כְּעֹ֣טְיָ֔ה עַ֖ל עֶדְרֵ֥י חֲבֵרֶֽיךָ׃


**The Metsudah Five Megillot, Lakewood, N.J., 2001:**
Tell me, you whom my soul loves, where do you graze your flock where do you rest your flock at noon; for why should I flit about around the flocks of your companions?


**The Koren Jerusalem Bible:**
Tell me, O thou whom my soul loves, where thou feedest, where thou makest thy flock to rest at noon: for why should I be like one who cloaks himself by the flocks of thy companions?


**The Rashi Ketuvim by Rabbi Shraga Silverstein:**
[(The Congregation of Israel says to G-d):] Tell me, [You] whom my soul loves, how will You graze [Your flock among these wolves, the nations of the exile]? How will You make [them] lie down in the afternoon [of the exile? (the afternoon being a particularly difficult time for sheep)]. For why shall I be enwrapped [to the lip like a mourner at the plight of my sheep] among the flocks of Your neighbors [i.e., among the hordes of the nations, who rely on other gods]?
