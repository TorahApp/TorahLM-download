Link: [https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations)

## Chapter 1


### Verse 1

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:1:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:1:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:1:1)


**אֵיכָה יָשְׁבָה בָדָד.** יִרְמְיָה כָתַב סֵפֶר קִינוֹת, הִיא הַמְּגִלָּה אֲשֶׁר שָׂרַף יְהוֹיָקִים עַל הָאֵשׁ אֲשֶׁר עַל הָאָח, וְהָיוּ בָהּ שָׁלשׁ אָלֶ"ף בֵּיתוֹ"ת: "אֵיכָה יָשְׁבָה", "אֵיכָה יָעִיב", "אֵיכָה יוּעַם". שׁוּב הוֹסִיף עָלָיו "אֲנִי הַגֶּבֶר" שֶׁהוּא שָׁלשׁ אָלֶ"ף בֵּיתוֹ"ת, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר, "וְעוֹד נוֹסַף עֲלֵיהֶם דְבָרִים רַבִּים כָּהֵמָּה", שָׁלשׁ כְּנֶגֶד שָׁלשׁ:


**How can it be that she sits in isolation.** Yirmiyahu wrote the book of Lamentations. ¹ This is the scroll that Yehoyakim burned on the hearth.² It contained three³ alphabetical acrostics:⁴ “Oh how can it be that she sits [in isolation],”⁵ “How can it be [that God] has cast a gloom,”⁶ [and] “How dim can it be [that the golden glow] has paled.”⁷ He added to it, “I am the man...”,⁸ which contains three sets of alphabetical acrostics, as it is stated, “and many more words were added similar to those [of the original scroll],”⁹ three corresponding to three.¹⁰

footnotes:
¹ Because Chapter 3 of Eichah begins with Yirmiyahu writing, “I am the man,” one might mistakenly think that is the beginning of Yirmiyahu’s writing, therefore Rashi states that he wrote the entire Book of Lamentations [=Eichah]. (Sifsei Chachomim)
² See Yirmiyahu 36:23.
³ The three acrostics respond to the three tragedies that occurred: the physical exile of the Bnei Yisroel, the departure of the Shchinah and the loss of Eretz Yisroel. (Yalkut Me’am Lo’ez)
⁴ The alphabetical acrostic symbolizes the Bnei Yisroel’s transgression of the Torah which was written with the letters of the Aleph Beis. (Sifsei Chachomim)
⁵ Eichah 1:1.
⁶ Ibid., 2:1.
⁷ Ibid., 4:1.
⁸ Ibid., 3:1.
⁹ Yirmiyahu 36:32.
¹⁰ He also added Chapter 5. See Eichah Rabbah, Pesichta 28.


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:1:2

**בָדָד.** גַּלְמוּד מִיּוֹשְׁבֶיהָ:


**In isolation.** Devoid of her inhabitants.


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:1:3

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:1:3](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:1:3)


**רַבָּתִי עָם.** יוּ"ד יְתֵרָה, כְּמוֹ "רַבַּת עָם", שֶׁהָיְתָה עַמָּהּ רַב. יֵשׁ מִדְרְשֵׁי אַגָּדָה הַרְבֵּה, וַאֲנִי בָאתִי לְפָרֵשׁ לְשׁוֹן הַמִּקְרָא כְּמַשְׁמָעוֹ:


**Teeming with people.** The *yud* of רַבָּתִי is superfluous;¹¹ is not superfluous because the prophet is speaking in [first person singular] God’s Name, saying, “How can it be that the city that ‘I’ made great and that ‘I’ made important, etc.” [it means the same] as, רַבָּתִי עָם, that her people were many.¹² There are many Aggadic midrashim, but I have come to explain the Scripture according to its literal meaning.

footnotes:
¹¹ Palgei Mayim explains that the yud
¹² Alternatively, ‘the city whose people were so wise’ yet they were unable to prevent its destruction. (Eichah Rabbah); or, ‘the city with great people.’ (Ibn Ezra)


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:1:4

**הָיְתָה כְּאַלְמָנָה.** וְלֹא אַלְמָנָה מַמָּשׁ, אֶלָּא כְאִשָּׁה שֶׁהָלַךְ בַּעְלָהּ לִמְדִינַת הַיָּם וְדַעְתּוֹ לַחֲזוֹר אֶצְלָהּ:


**Has become like a widow.** But not really a widow; rather, like a woman whose husband went abroad and he intends to return to her.¹³

footnotes:
¹³ See Maseches Sanhedrin 104a.



### Verse 2

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:2:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:2:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:2:1)


**בָּכוֹ תִבְכֶּה.** שְׁתֵּי בְכִיּוֹת עַל שְׁתֵּי חֻרְבָּנִין:


**She weeps bitterly.** Weeping twice over the two destructions [of the Beis Hamikdosh].¹⁴

footnotes:
¹⁴ Rashi is explaining the reason for the double verb בכו תבכה. Alternatively, Yerusholayim weeps, and God, as it were, also weeps. (Yalkut Me’am Lo’ez)


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:2:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:2:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:2:2)


**בַּלַּיְלָה.** שֶׁהַמִּקְדָּשׁ נִשְׂרַף בַּלַּיְלָה, דְּאָמַר מַר "לְעֵת עֶרֶב הִצִּיתוּ בוֹ אֶת הָאוּר". דָּבָר אַחֵר: "בַּלַיְלָה", לַיְלָה שֶׁל בְּכִיַּת מְרַגְּלִים בְּתִשְׁעָה בְאָב גָּרְמָה לָהֶם. דָּבָר אַחֵר: "בַּלַּיְלָה", שֶׁכָּל הַבּוֹכֶה בַלַּיְלָה, הַשּׁוֹמֵעַ קוֹלוֹ בּוֹכֶה עִמּוֹ:


**In the night.** For the Beis Hamikdosh was burned at night, as the master said, “at the time of evening they set it on fire.”¹⁵ Another explanation of “in the night,” [is that] the night of the weeping of the spies on the ninth of Av caused it to happen to them.¹⁶ Another explanation of “in the night,” is that whoever weeps at night, the one who hears his voice weeps along with him.¹⁷

footnotes:
¹⁵ Maseches Ta’anis 29a.
¹⁶ Ibid.
¹⁷ Maseches Sanhedrin 104b.

**Her tear on her cheek.** Because she cries constantly.¹⁸

**And excessive toil.** With which the people of Kasdim burdened her.²⁰

**הִיא יָשְׁבָה בַגּוֹיִם.** וּבַמָּקוֹם שֶׁגָּלְתָה וְיָשְׁבָה שָׁם, לֹא מָצְאָה מָנוֹחַ:


**[Even] when she dwelt among the nations.** And in the place where she was exiled and settled, she found no respite.²¹

footnotes:

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:3:5

**בֵּין הַמְּצָרִים.** שֶׁיֵּשׁ גֹּבַהּ מִכַּאן וּמִכַּאן וְאֵין מָקוֹם לָנוּס:


**Between the barriers.** It is elevated on either side and there is no place to flee.


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:3:6

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:3:6](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:3:6)


**הַמְּצָרִים.** גְּבוּלִים שֶׁל שָׂדֶה וָכָרֶם. וּמִדְרַשׁ אַגָּדָה, בֵּין שִׁבְעָה עָשָׂר בְּתַמּוּז לְתִשְׁעָה בְאָב:


**The barriers.** The boundaries of fields and vineyards.²² The Midrash Aggadah [explains that it refers to the boundaries of time], i.e., between the seventeenth of Tammuz and the ninth of Av.

footnotes:
²² From where there is no way to escape. (Palgei Mayim)

### Verse 6

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:6:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:6:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:6:1)


**כְּאַיָּלִים לֹא מָצְאוּ מִרְעֶה.** כְּאַיָּלִים אֲשֶׁר לֹא מָצְאוּ מִרְעֶה, שֶׁאֵין לָהֶם כֹּחַ לִבְרֹחַ, שֶׁהֻחְלַשׁ כֹּחָם בְּרָעָב:


**Like harts who could find no pasture.** Like harts who did not find pasture and have no strength to flee,²⁶ for their strength has been weakened by hunger.

footnotes:
²⁶ Alternatively, her nobles acted like harts who abandon their young when there is no pasture. Similarly, Bnei Yisroel’s leaders did not chastise their brethren, but rather abandoned them. (Palgei Mayim)


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:6:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:6:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:6:2)


**לִפְנֵי רוֹדֶף.** כָּל "רֹדֵף" שֶׁבַּמִּקְרָא חָסֵר, וְזֶה מָלֵא, שֶׁנִּרְדְּפוּ רְדִיפָה שְׁלֵמָה עַל כֵּן יָסַד הַפַּיָּט, "מָלֵא רֻדַּפְתִּי וְחָסְרָה שְׁנַת גְּאוּלַי". "שְׁנַת גְּאֻלַי בָּאָה" חָסֵר כְּתִיב:


**Before the pursuer.** Every רֹדֵף in Scripture is defective,²⁷’. but this one רוֹדֵף is full,²⁸. for they were totally pursued. Therefore the liturgic author composed, “I have been totally pursued, but the year of my redemption has not come.”²⁹ [Also,] “and the year of my redemption has come,”³⁰ is spelled defectively גְּאֻלַי.³¹. In our editions it is spelled full [גאולי]. However, the prophecy in Yeshayahuu 59:20 states, “A redeemer [גואל—spelled full] will come to Tzion,” to indicate a “total redemption.” (Eichah Rabbah 1:33)

footnotes:
²⁷ I.e., it is spelled without a ‘vav
²⁸ I.e., it is spelled with a vav
²⁹ Selichos for the second day of Aseres Yemei Teshuvah.
³⁰ Yeshayahu 63:4.
³¹ Without a vav

**The days of her poverty.** The days of her destruction, which brought her to poverty.


###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:7:3

**And lamenting.** This וּמְרוּדֶיהָ is an expression of pain³² as in, “and I will wail וְיָרַדְתִּי upon the mountains,”³³ [and as in,] “I lament אָרִיד as I speak and I moan.”³⁴

³² Alternatively, ‘and her rebellion [מרד] against God.’
³³ Shoftim 11:37.
³⁴ Tehilim 55:3.

**שָׂחֲקוּ עַל מִשְׁבַּתֶּהָ.** שָׂמְחוּ עַל שְׁבִיתַת מְשׂוֹשָׂהּ, חַגָּהּ, חָדְשָׁהּ וְשַׁבַּתָּהּ וּמִדְרַשׁ אַגָּדָה דוֹרְשׁוֹ בְלָשׁוֹן אַחֵר, שֶׁהָיוּ שׁוֹבְתִין בַּגּוֹלָה בְשַׁבָּתוֹת וְיָמִים טוֹבִים וְשׁוֹמְטִים בִשְׁבִיעִית, וְהָיוּ הָעַכּוּ"ם מְשַׂחֲקִים עֲלֵיהֶם וְאוֹמְרִים "שׁוֹטִים, בְּאַרְצְכֶם לֹא שְׁמַטְתֶּם, וְעַכְשָׁיו בַּגּוֹלָה תִשְׁמְטוּ? בְּאַרְצְכֶם לֹא שְׁמַרְתֶּם, וְעַכְשָׁיו בַּגּוֹלָה תִשְׁמְרוּן?":


**Laughing derisively when she was curbed.** They rejoiced over the cessation of her celebrating at her festivals, New Moons, and Sabbaths. But the Midrash Aggadah interprets it as a different expression, that they rested in exile on the Sabbath and festivals, and observed the seventh [i.e., Sabbatical] year. The gentiles ridiculed them and said, “Fools! In your land you did not observe [Sabbath and festivals,] and now in exile you keep it? In your land you did not observe [the Sabbatical year,] and now in exile you keep it?”

### Verse 13

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:13:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:13:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Rashi%20on%20Lamentations/r/1:13:1)


**וַיִּרְדֶּנָּה.** וַיִּרֶד אוֹתָהּ, וַיְשַׁבֵּר אוֹתָהּ עַל יְדֵי רִדּוּי וְיִסּוּרִין, לָכֵן הַנּוּ"ן דְּגוּשָׁה, לְפָתְרוֹ בְּלָשׁוֹן יְחִידִית, כְּמוֹ יַעֲשֶׂנָּה, יְכַרְסְמֶנָּה, יִרְעֶנָּה, שֶׁהָעֶצֶם לְשׁוֹן נְקֵבָה, כְּמָה דְאַתְּ אָמַר "הָעֲצָמוֹת הַיְבֵשׁוֹת". שִׁבֵּר כָּל אַחַת וְאֶחָת. דָּבָר אַחֵר: "וַיִּרְדֶּנָּה" כְּמוֹ "וַיִּרְדֵּהוּ אֶל כַּפָּיו". הֵרִיק אֶת הַמּוֹחַ, גּוֹרֵר וְרוֹדֶה הַמּוֹחַ מִתּוֹכוֹ:


**Crushing it.** It [the fire] crushed it and broke it through punishment and suffering. Therefore, the ‘*nun*’ is punctuated with a ‘*dagesh*’, so that it is interpreted in the feminine singular, as in יַעֲשֶׂנָּה [=he will do it], יְכַרְסְמֶנָּה [=he will gnaw at it], יִרְעֶנָּה [=will graze upon it]; for עֶצֶם [=bone] is in the feminine gender, as [Scripture] states, “the dry bones [=הָעֲצָמוֹת הַיְבֵשׁוֹת.”⁴⁶ It broke each one. Another explanation, וַיִּרְדֶּנָּה as in, “and he separated it into his hands.”⁴⁷ He emptied the marrow, [i.e.,] he scraped and separated the marrow from within it.⁴⁸

footnotes:
⁴⁶ Yechezkeil 37:4.
⁴⁷ Shoftim 14:9.
⁴⁸ Alternatively, ‘and sent it [i.e., fire] down.’



### Verse 14

###### Rashi on Lamentations 1:14:1

**נִשְׂקַד עֹל פְּשָׁעַי בְּיָדוֹ.** אֵין לְתֵבָה זוּ דִמְיוֹן בַּמִּקְרָא וּבְלָשׁוֹן אֲרַמִּי שֶׁל פְּסִיקְתָּא קוֹרִין לְדָרְבָן מַסְקָדָא, מַלְמַד הַבָּקָר. וְאוֹמֵר אֲנִי, "נִשְׂקַד" פיינטרינ"ט בְּלַעַ"ז. נְקֻדִּים, מְנֻמָּרִים וּמְסֻמָּנִים הָיוּ פְשָׁעַי בְּיָדוֹ שֶׁל הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא לְזִכָּרוֹן. לֹא נִשְׁכַּח מִנְיָנָם וְתַשְׁלוּמֵיהֶם:

Similarly, every word whose radical ends with a *hei*, e.g., to turn פָּנָה, to go astray זָנָה, to weep בָּכָה, are vowelized in that manner when the *hei* is missing, when it speaks of itself it is vowelized with a *chirik*, e.g., “And Pharaoh turned.”¹³ But when it speaks of others it is vowelized with a small *patach* [=*segol*], e.g., “and he turned [the foxes] tail to tail,”¹⁴ [and] “and Yehudah went into exile from his land,”¹⁵. [and] “and the king of Bavel exiled Yisroel into Assyria.”¹⁶

###### Rashi on Lamentations 3:38:1

**מִפִּי עֶלְיוֹן וְגוֹ'.** וְאִם בָּאתִי לוֹמַר: "לֹא מִיָּדוֹ בָאָה אֵלַי הָרָעָה הַזֹּאת, מִקְרֶה הִיא שֶׁהָיָה לִי", אֵין זֹאת, כִּי אִם בֵּין רָעוֹת וּבֵין טוֹבוֹת, מִי זֹאת אָמַר וַתֶּהִי, אִם ה' לֹא צִוָּה, וּמִפִּיו לֹא תֵצֵא הֵן רָעָה הֵן טוֹבָה, אֲבָל מַה יֵּשׁ לְהִתְאוֹנֵן אָדָם חָי? "גֶּבֶר עַל חֲטָאָיו". כָּל אִישׁ וְאִישׁ יִתְאוֹנֵן עַל חֲטָאָיו, כִּי הֵם הַמְּבִיאִים עָלָיו הָרָעָה. ("מִפִּי עֶלְיוֹן לֹא תֵצֵא". אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן: מִיּוֹם שֶׁאָמַר הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא "רְאֵה נָתַתִּי לְפָנֶיךָ הַיּוֹם אֶת הַחַיִּים וְאֶת הַטּוֹב וְגוֹ'", לֹא יָצָא רָעָה וְטוֹבָה מִפִּיו, אֶלָּא הָרָעָה בָאָה מֵאֵלֶיהָ לְעוֹשֶׂה רַע וְהַטּוֹב לְעוֹשֶׂה טוֹב.) לְפִיכָךְ, "מַה יִּתְאוֹנֵן"? לָמָּה יִתְרַעֵם הָאָדָם אִם לֹא עַל חֲטָאָיו:

But what should a living man bemoan? “A man for his sins.” Every man should bemoan his sins, because they are what bring the evil upon him. (“They did not emanate from the Almighty,” Rabbi Yochonon said, “From the day that the Holy One, Blessed Is He, said, ‘See, I have placed before you today the life and the good, etc.,’³⁰ neither evil nor good has come from His command.” Rather the evil comes by itself to the one who commits evil, and the good to the one who does good.) Therefore, what should one bemoan? Why should a man be angry, if not about his sins?

###### Rashi on Lamentations 3:41:1

**טְחוֹן נָשָׂאוּ.** כְּשֶׁהָיוּ הָאוֹיְבִים מוֹלִיכִין אוֹתָם בְּקוֹלָרִין, הָיוּ נוֹתְנִים עַל כְּתֵפֵיהֶם רֵחַיִם וּמַשָׂאוֹת כְּדֵי לְיַגְּעָם. וְכֵן, "בָּעֵץ כָּשָׁלוּ", כָּשַׁל כֹּחָם. וּלְשׁוֹן כִּשְׁלוֹן נוֹפֵל בְּתַשּׁוּת כֹּחַ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר בְּעֶזְרָא "וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוּדָה כָּשַׁל כֹּחַ הַסַּבָּל". וְכֵן "הִכְשִׁיל כֹּחִי":
