**“And it came to pass in the days when the judges judged”**—Scripture explains the cause that moved Elimelech to leave the Land of Israel for the Diaspora. It was because this occurred “in the days when the judges judged,” regarding which it is said (Judges 17 and 18): “In those days there was no king in Israel; every man did what was right in his own eyes.” Nor was there at that time a single judge appointed over all Israel, for this was an interval between one judge and another, when there were many judges, and anyone who desired would assume authority for himself. The fear of the judge was not upon the people, as our Sages said: “Were it not for the fear of government, a man would swallow his fellow alive.” And the Sages expressed this figuratively: “Woe to the generation that judged its judges.” A second factor was that “there was a famine in the land.” In times of famine, crowds of poor people gather against the wealthy and compel them to give bread and grain, and if not, they seize it by force—especially when there is no king to impose fear upon them. Therefore, “a man from Bethlehem in Judah went to sojourn in the fields of Moab.” Since he was a wealthy man, he feared that the poor would descend upon him and plunder all that he possessed. Nevertheless, he did not go in order to settle permanently, but only “to sojourn in the fields of Moab” until the anger should pass. Nor did he go to dwell in a city or town, but only “in the fields of Moab,” like one temporarily residing in the countryside. Likewise, although it was a time of suffering and distress, not everyone left the land—only one man departed, while the other wealthy people did not join him in leaving the Land of Israel for the Diaspora. Even Elimelech’s household did not leave willingly, for they did not wish to abandon the desirable land; rather, only he, his wife, and his two sons went, because he was the principal actor in this matter, while they were compelled to follow him. Thus the Midrash says: “He was primary, and his wife secondary.”* The Midrash says: Elimelech was the leading man of the generation. He said: “Now all Israel will come daily to my doorway [seeking support],” and so he fled. It also says there that there were two famines: a famine of Torah and a famine of bread...

**ושם האיש**. באר שהיה איש הנודע בשם לא לבד הוא כי גם אשתו ובניו היו אנשי שם, והוא טעם אל יציאתו שלכן היה ירא מההמון שיבקשו ממנו שבר רעבון בתיהם, וגם הוא טעם אל העונש שהשיגהו, שלפי שהיה איש גדול הפיל לבן של ישראל בצאתו כמ"ש במד' וגם שהי' חלול השם: אפרתים מבית לחם יהודה, בית לחם היתה נקראת תחלה בשם אפרת ואח"כ נתן לה שם בית לחם כמ"ש ואקברה שם בדרך אפרת היא בית לחם, וגם היא משפחה מגדולי היחס של שבט יהודה שנקראו אפרתים [ע"ש אשת כלב שנקראת אפרת והיתה גדולה וחשובה] וע"כ כפל שהיה מן המיוחסין והגדולים שבבית לחם יהודה, ויבואו ר"ל והגם שתחלה יצאו רק לגור בשדי מואב לפי שעה, אחר שבאו שדי מואב היו שם, ר"ל אז הסכימו בדעתם להשאר שם בקביעות:


**“And the name of the man”**—Scripture explains that he was a man well known by name; not only he himself, but also his wife and sons were people of distinction. This further explains why he left, for he feared the masses, who would seek from him provisions to sustain their households during the famine. It also explains the punishment that befell him, since, being a great man, his departure discouraged the hearts of Israel, as stated in the Midrash, and also constituted a desecration of God’s name. **“Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah”**—Bethlehem was originally called Ephrath, and only afterward was given the name Bethlehem, as it says: “And I buried her there on the road to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem” (Genesis 48:7). It was also a family of especially distinguished lineage within the tribe of Judah, called Ephrathites [after Caleb’s wife, who was called Ephrath and was great and distinguished]. Therefore, Scripture repeats that he was among the well-born and prominent families of Bethlehem in Judah. **“And they came”**—Although at first they left only “to sojourn in the fields of Moab” temporarily, once they arrived in the fields of Moab, “they remained there”—that is, they then resolved in their minds to stay there permanently.

**וימת**, וע"י שהסכים להשאר בח"ל נענש תיכף וימת, ובאר מדוע נענש הוא ולא נעמי כי הוא היה איש נעמי ומושל עליה, ועקר החטא מתיחס אליו, וחז"ל אמרו שתחלה הענישו ה' בממונו כי אין בעל הרחמים פוגע בנפשות תחלה, וזה מרמז במ"ש איש נעמי, כי עשיר גדול כאלימלך הוא בעצמו נודע בשם ע"י עשרו והיו אומרים שמת אלימלך העשיר וכ"ש אם היה בא"י שם היו אומרים שמת אלימלך הפרנס וגדול הדור, אבל אחר שאבד כל עשרו והיה בארץ נכריה לא נודע בשם רק שהיה איש נעמי שהיא נודעה שם יותר ממנו והוא נודע רק במה שהוא איש נעמי. ותשאר האשה, ר"ל ובכ"ז לא לקחו מוסר לשוב לארץ ישראל רק נשארו שם, ובמדרש אומר ותשאר האשה נעשית שיורי מנחות, ר"ל שהלא שאר ונותר לא יצדק רק אם הרוב נלקח ונשאר המעוט, ואיך יצדק שרק אחד מת ושלשה נשארו, ועז"א כשיורי מנחות שהגם שלא יקחו רק הקומץ קורא כל המנחה בשם שירים, אחר שהקומץ הוא העיקר והמנחה טפלה להקומץ, וכן כאן הוא היה עיקר הבית ולכן נקראו כלם בשם שירים:


**“And Elimelech died”**—Because he resolved to remain permanently outside the Land of Israel, he was punished immediately and died. Scripture explains why he was punished while Naomi was not: he was “the husband of Naomi,” meaning that he ruled over her, and thus the primary sin was attributed to him. Our Sages said that God first punished him through his wealth, for the Merciful One does not strike at a person’s life first. This is hinted at by the phrase **“the husband of Naomi”**, for a wealthy man such as Elimelech would ordinarily have been known by name because of his riches, and people would have said, “Elimelech the wealthy man has died.” All the more so had he remained in the Land of Israel, where they would have said, “Elimelech, the provider and leader of the generation, has died.” But after he lost all his wealth and was in a foreign land, he was no longer known by his own name, but only as “the husband of Naomi,” who was more recognized there than he was; he was identified only through being Naomi’s husband. **“And the woman remained”**—Even so, they did not take the lesson to heart and return to the Land of Israel, but remained there. The Midrash says concerning **“and the woman remained”**: “She became like the remainder of a meal offering.” The terms “remainder” and “leftover” properly apply only when most has been taken and a minority remains. How, then, can it be said here when only one person died and three remained? The answer is: like the remainder of a meal offering, where even though only the handful (*kometz*) is taken, the entire offering is called the “remainder,” because the handful is the essential part and the meal offering is secondary to it. So too here: Elimelech was the essence of the household, and therefore all the others were called the “remainder.”

**וישאו**. והנה הבנים הוסיפו לחטוא במה שנשאו נשים מואביות שלא גיירו אותן כמ"ש במדרש ועז"א שם האחת ערפה, כי בגירותן היו משנים את שם הקודם והם נשארו בשמם [והגם שרות לא שנו שמה גם אחר שנתגיירה עז"א בברכות (דף ט') מאי רות שיצא ממנה דוד שריוהו להקב"ה בשירות ותשבחות שהקושיא מאי רות היינו למה לא שנו שם גיותה] וגם זה מבואר מלשון וישאו, שעל לקיחה בקדושין כדת בא לשון לקיחה ולשון וישאו בא לרוב בנשואין של נשים נכריות בכל ספר עזרא ונחמיה ועל אשה שהיתה טפלה כמ"ש ברחבעם (דה"ב י"א) נשים שמונה עשרה נשא ופילגשים ששים, שרק שתים היו עקריים שאמר עליהם ויקח, וכדומה, ומבאר שהיה שם האחת ערפה ושם השנית רות מב' טעמים: א] שכליון האח הצעיר הקדים ויקח את ערפה והתחיל בעברה שזה מורה על מעוט המוסר של האחים שהצעיר נשא לפני הבכור ושהאח הגדול לא מיחה בידו, ב] שאם היה מחלון הנושא ראשונה היה קצת למוד זכות על שניהם, על מחלון שהכיר בצדקת רות כמו שהוכיח סופה על תחלתה, ועל כליון שחשב שגם הוא ימצא אשה טובה כמו אחיו, אבל האמת היה בהפך שכליון הקדים לישא את ערפה המרשעת אחיו השני היה לו להכיר איך נכשל האח הצעיר באשה רעה והוסיף פשע, ועוד חטאו במה שישבו שם כעשר שנים שכבר מבואר שנתיאשו לגמרי מלחזור לא"י:


**“And they took wives”**—The sons further compounded the sin by marrying Moabite women without converting them, as stated in the Midrash. Therefore it says, **“the name of the one was Orpah”**, for when converts entered Judaism, they would generally change their former names, yet these women retained their original names. [Although Ruth did not change her name even after conversion, the Talmud asks in Berakhot (9b): “Why was she called Ruth?” and answers: “Because David descended from her, who satisfied the Holy One, blessed be He, with songs and praises.” The very question, “Why Ruth?” implies: why was her gentile name not changed?] This is also indicated by the wording **“and they took wives”** (*vayisu*). The term *lekichah* (“taking”) is generally used for marriages contracted through proper kiddushin according to Jewish law, whereas the expression *vayisu* is commonly used for marriages to foreign women throughout Ezra and Nehemiah, or regarding women of secondary status. Thus it says concerning Rehoboam (II Chronicles 11): “He took eighteen wives and sixty concubines,” where only two principal wives are described with the term *“he took”* (*vayikach*), and similarly elsewhere. Scripture specifies **“the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the second was Ruth”** for two reasons: First, because Kilion, the younger brother, preceded Mahlon and married Orpah first. This demonstrates the brothers’ lack of proper conduct, since the younger brother married before the elder, and the elder brother did not protest. Second, had Mahlon married first, there might have been some basis to judge them favorably: Mahlon could be credited with recognizing Ruth’s righteousness, as her later deeds proved her earlier character, and Kilion might have reasoned that he too would find a worthy wife like his brother. But the truth was the opposite: Kilion first married the wicked Orpah, and Mahlon, the elder brother, should have learned from the younger brother’s failure in choosing an evil woman, yet instead added sin upon sin. They further sinned by remaining there for about ten years, for this demonstrates that they had completely abandoned hope of ever returning to the Land of Israel.

**וימותו**. לכן נענשו גם המה ומחלון מת תחלה קודם כליון, כי הוא היה לו למחות ביד אחיו הצעיר ומת בעד עונו ובעד שלא מיחה באחיו, וחז"ל אמרו שאמר מלת גם שתחלה ירד ממונם לטמיון ואח"כ מתו כי אין בעה"ר פוגע בנפשות תחלה. ותשאר האשה, כי היא לא השתתפה בחטאם והיה דעתה תמיד לחזור לא"י:


**“And they also died”**—Therefore they too were punished, and Mahlon died before Kilion, because he ought to have protested against his younger brother. He died both for his own sin and for failing to rebuke his brother. Our Sages said that the word **“also”** (*gam*) indicates that their wealth first perished, and only afterward did they die, for the Merciful One does not strike at a person’s life first. **“And the woman remained”**—For she did not participate in their sin, and her intention had always been to return to the Land of Israel.

**ותקם היא וכלותיה**. הנה כלם הסכימו לצאת מן המקום הזה כי חשבו שמזל המקום גורם, ולפ"ז בהיציאה ממה שממנו היו כולם שוים, אבל בהליכה אל מה שאליו רק נעמי לבדה גמרה בדעתה לשוב משדי מואב. שהשיבה מציין השב אל מקום שהיה שם כבר, ע"ז לא הסכימה רק נעמי, ועז"א ותשב משדי מואב, שהיא גמרה כן בדעתה בעודה בשדי מואב אבל הם לא הסכימו לזה בעודם בשדי מואב רק אח"כ כמו שיתבאר. כי שמעה בשדה מואב, ר"ל כי היא לא שבה מפני מזל הרע של המקום ומפני יראת מות שאז הי' די אם תצא ממקום זה למקום אחר במואב, רק היא שבה מפני כי שמעה בשדה מואב כי פקד ה' את עמו לתת להם לחם, ואחר שהיא לא יצאה רק מפני הרעב ורק לגור עד יפסק הרעב ולכן כששמעה שלא ישאו עוד חרפת רעב שבה לא"י, ובמדרש ממי שמעה מרוכלים המחזירים בעיירות, ומה שמעה כי פקד ה' את עמו, כתוב א' אומר כי לא יטוש ה' את עמו ונחלתו לא יעזוב וכתוב א' אומר כי לא יטוש ה' את עמו בעבור שמו הגדול, כיצד בזמן שישראל זכאים הוא עושה בעבור עמו ונחלתו ובזמן שאין ישראל זכאים הוא עושה בעבור שמו הגדול, ר"ל אחר שאמר הלשון ששמעה כי פקד ה' את עמו זה א"א ששמעה כן מאנשי מואב שהגם שהם קרובים לא"י והיו יודעים את הנעשה שם לא רגיל על לשונם לומר הלשון כי פקד ה' את עמו שהם לא האמינו בה' ולא בקשר שי"ל עם ישראל שהם עמו וע"כ אומר ששמעה מרוכלין המחזירים בעיירות והביאו דברים מא"י למכור בשדה מואב וזה סימן שפסק שם הרעב, ומפרש עוד שבעבור מה שפסק הרעב לבד לא היתה שבה עדיין שאחר שהרעב בא בסבת רוע המעשים כמ"ש ויהי בימי שפוט השופטים ויהי רעב ופי' במד' שבא הרעב בעבור שלא היה ביניהם משפט ושפטו את שופטיהם ושופטיהם היו צריכים להשפט, א"כ כל עוד שלא הטיבו מעשיהם איננה בטוחה שלא ישוב הרעב שנית רק שהיא שמעה שה' פקד את עמו ר"ל שלא עשה זה בעבור שמו רק בעבור העם שהיטיבו מעשיהם והיו ראוים לפקידה ע"י שהם עמו ונחלתו ועי"כ פקדם לתת להם לחם שיהיה נתינה קבועה וקיימת:


**“And she arose, she and her daughters-in-law”**—All of them agreed to leave that place, for they thought that the misfortune was caused by the fate of the place. In this respect, they were equal in departing from where they had been. However, regarding the destination to which they were going, only Naomi had firmly resolved to return to the fields of Moab. The term **“return”** signifies returning to a place where one had previously been, and only Naomi had agreed to this. Therefore it says, **“and she returned from the fields of Moab”**—she had already resolved upon this while still in the fields of Moab, whereas her daughters-in-law had not yet agreed to this while there, as will later be explained. **“For she had heard in the fields of Moab”**—that is, Naomi did not return because of the ill fortune of the place or out of fear of death, for in that case it would have been enough merely to leave that place for another location in Moab. Rather, she returned because **“she had heard in the fields of Moab that Hashem had remembered His people to give them bread.”** Since she had left only because of the famine and intended merely to sojourn until it passed, once she heard that the disgrace of famine had ended, she returned to the Land of Israel. The Midrash asks: From whom did she hear this? From traveling merchants who went from town to town. And what did she hear? **“That Hashem had remembered His people.”** One verse says, “For Hashem will not forsake His people, nor abandon His inheritance” (Psalms 94:14), while another says, “For Hashem will not forsake His people for the sake of His great name” (I Samuel 12:22). How are these reconciled? When Israel is worthy, He acts for the sake of His people and His inheritance; when Israel is not worthy, He acts for the sake of His great name. Malbim explains: Since Scripture says that Naomi heard, **“Hashem had remembered His people,”** this could not have been something she heard from Moabites. Although they lived near the Land of Israel and knew what was happening there, it was not their manner of speech to say, **“Hashem had remembered His people,”** for they did not believe in Hashem nor in His special relationship with Israel as His people. Therefore, the Midrash says that she heard this from traveling merchants who passed through towns and brought goods from the Land of Israel to sell in the fields of Moab—this itself being a sign that the famine there had ended. Furthermore, Naomi would not yet have returned merely because the famine had ceased, for the famine had originally come as a consequence of wicked behavior, as Scripture says: **“And it came to pass in the days when the judges judged… and there was a famine,”** which the Midrash explains as resulting from the lack of justice among them—they judged their judges, and their judges themselves needed judging. Therefore, as long as the people had not improved their conduct, Naomi could not be certain that the famine would not return. Rather, she heard that **“Hashem had remembered His people”**—meaning that He acted not merely for the sake of His name, but for the sake of the people themselves, because they had improved their deeds and were worthy of Divine remembrance as His people and inheritance. Therefore, He had remembered them **“to give them bread”**—a lasting and enduring provision.

**ותצא**, מפרש דבריו הקודמים שמצד היציאה מן המקום יצאה ושתי כלותיה עמה, שבזה השוו כלם בדעה אחת תיכף שצריך לצאת ממקום הזה ששם הורע מזלם, אבל במה שהיא הסכימה תיכף לשוב אל ארץ יהודה לא השוו תיכף כי הם היה דעתם לגור בשדה מואב, רק ותלכנה בדרך אחר שהחזיקו בדרך ובאו עד סוף ארץ מואב שהוא הדרך ההולך רק אל ארץ יהודה אז הסכימו גם הם לשוב אל ארץ יהודה:


**“And she departed”**—This explains the earlier statement: with respect to leaving the place, Naomi departed and her two daughters-in-law went with her, for in this they immediately shared the same opinion—that it was necessary to leave that place where their fortune had turned against them. However, regarding Naomi’s immediate resolve to return to the land of Judah, they did not at first share her intention, for their plan had been merely to settle elsewhere in the fields of Moab. Only later, **“they went on the road”**—after they had committed themselves to the journey and reached the edge of the land of Moab, the road leading only toward the land of Judah—did they also agree to return to the land of Judah.

**ותאמר נעמי**. נעמי חשבה תחלה שמה שהולכים אתה בדרך של ארץ יהודה כוונתן רק ללוותה שלא תלך יחידי ודעתן ללותה עד ארץ יהודה בתורת לויה ואח"ז ישובו משם לארץ מואב. עז"א להם לכנה היינו שילכו לדרכן שהיא בארץ מואב כי אינה צריכה ללויה, ובאשר בלכתן להתישב בארץ מואב יש להם ברירה להתישב בעיר אחת מערי מואב בפ"ע או לשוב אל בית אמם, אמרה עצתה שובנה אשה לבית אמה, שכמו שדבקתם בי כי הייתי לכן כאם טוב יותר שתשובו לאמכן האמתית, עתה פרשה דבריה נגד מ"ש לכנה היינו שלא יתלבטו בדרך לצרכה כדי ללוותה, עז"א יעש ה' עמכם חסד ר"ל כבר עשיתם עמדי חסד די והותר בין עם המתים ובעודם חיים שהיו טובות לבעליהם ובין עמדי, וחז"ל פי' עם המתים שנתנו להם תכריכין ועמדי שמחלו להן כתובתן, שמחוקי מדינת מואב היה צורך הקבורה מעזבון המת וגם היה צריך לתת להם כתובתן והם ויתרו כ"ז משלהם שזה אינו מצד הדין רק מצד חסד וכבר עשיתם חסד די והותר עד שבודאי כן יעשה ה' עמכם חסד כי לא יקפח שכר הראוי לכם לפי חסדכם, עז"א יעש ה' ר"ל זה יעשה בודאי מצד דרכו לשלם לאיש כמעשהו:


**“And Naomi said”**—At first, Naomi thought that their intention in accompanying her on the road to the land of Judah was merely to escort her, so that she would not travel alone, and that they intended only to accompany her to the land of Judah as an act of escort, after which they would return to the land of Moab. Therefore she said to them, **“Go”**—meaning, go on your own way, which is in the land of Moab, for she had no need of an escort. Since, upon returning to settle in the land of Moab, they had the choice either to settle independently in one of the cities of Moab or to return to their mother’s house, she advised them: **“Return, each of you, to her mother’s house”**—just as you attached yourselves to me, for I was like a mother to you, it is better that you now return to your true mother. She then explains her words concerning **“Go”**—that they should not trouble themselves on the journey for her sake in accompanying her. Thus she said: **“May Hashem deal kindly with you”**—meaning, you have already shown me kindness more than sufficiently, both **“with the dead”** while they were alive, in being good wives to your husbands, and **“with me.”** Our Sages explained: **“with the dead”**—that they provided them with burial shrouds; **“and with me”**—that they waived their marriage settlements. According to the customs of Moab, burial expenses would normally come from the deceased’s estate, and they were also entitled to receive their marriage settlements, yet they relinquished all of this from what was rightfully theirs. This was not required by law, but was an act of kindness. You have already shown kindness more than sufficiently, such that surely Hashem will likewise deal kindly with you, for He will not withhold the reward due to you according to your acts of kindness. Therefore she said: **“May Hashem deal”**—meaning, this He will certainly do, according to His way of repaying each person in accordance with his deeds.

**יתן**, וחוץ ממה שיעש עמכם חסד המגיע לכם יתן לכם מתנה [אף שאינו גמול מצד מעשה קודמת] ומצאן מנוחה אשה בית אישה, וזה מקביל נגד מ"ש שובנה אשה לבית אמה, כי האלמנות שאין רצונם להנשא עוד יעשו להם בית בפ"ע כאשה שלטת ברשות עצמה, אבל האלמנה שדעתה להנשא תשוב אל בית אביה ואצל עכו"ם שאין להם יחוס אב תשוב לבית אמה עד תהיינה לאיש, ע"ז ברכה אותם שימצאו מנוחה אשה בית אישה שלא ידעו עוד שכול ואלמון רק ישבו במנוחות שאננות וזה יהיה מתנת ה': ותשק להן, נשיקה של פרידה:


**“May He grant”**—Beyond the kindness that Hashem will show you as your due reward, **“may He grant”** refers to a gift bestowed upon you [even though it is not repayment for a prior deed]. **“That you may find rest, each woman in the house of her husband”** corresponds to what Naomi had said: **“Return, each of you, to her mother’s house.”** For widows who do not wish to remarry establish a household of their own, as women independent in their own domain. But a widow who intends to remarry returns to her father’s house; and among gentiles, who do not trace lineage through the father, she returns to her mother’s house until she marries again. Therefore Naomi blessed them that they should **“find rest, each woman in the house of her husband”**—that they should no longer know bereavement or widowhood, but instead dwell in peaceful security. This would be Hashem’s gift. **“And she kissed them”**—a kiss of parting.

**ותאמר נעמי**. ע"ז אמרה נעמי להן עצתה שובנה בנותי למה תלכנה עמי. ר"ל הלא בני עמי לא יתחתנו עמכם, ואם תקוו שאני אתחתן עמכם אחר שכבר הייתן כלותי, העוד לי בנים במעי, וכבר התבאר אצלי (התו"ה תזריע סימן ד') ששם בנים בדיוק יבא רק על בנים שנולדו לא על העוברים במעי אמם, ופה אמרה העוד לי בנים במעי שזה סותר כלל זה אך בכאן דברה בדרך מליצה שתחלה התחילה וכי יש לי בנים גדולים שראוים לנשואין תכף עד כי והיו לכם לאנשים ר"ל שיהיו תיכף לאנשים אך בדרך הלצה אמרה וכי יש לי בנים גדולים כאלה וטמנתים במעי שלכן לא תראו אותם כי הם טמונים ונחבאים במעי ואני אוציאם ממעי ויהיו לכם לאנשים תכף, וזה בדרך מליצה כי בתחלת הסברא לא יעלה על הדעת שירצו להמתין עד שתנשא ותוליד בנים ויגדלו הבנים שאז יהיו הם זקנות ובלתי ראוים לבנים הצעירים מהם שנים רבות:


**“And Naomi said”**—In response, Naomi advised them: **“Return, my daughters; why should you go with me?”** That is, the people of Israel will not intermarry with you. And if you are hoping that I will somehow arrange marriages for you, since you were already my daughters-in-law, **“Do I still have sons in my womb?”** It has already been explained elsewhere (Torat HaKohanim, Tazria, §4) that the term *banim* (“sons” or “children”) in its precise sense refers only to children already born, not to embryos in their mother’s womb. Yet here Naomi says, **“Do I still have sons in my womb?”**—which appears to contradict that rule. Malbim explains that Naomi is speaking figuratively. She first means to say: Do I have grown sons already fit for marriage immediately, such that **“they could become husbands to you”**—that is, suitable men for you right away? But in a playful manner of speech she says: Do I perhaps have such grown sons hidden away in my womb, concealed so that you cannot see them, whom I could suddenly bring forth and who would immediately become husbands for you? This is a figurative expression, for at the outset it would not even occur to anyone that they intended to wait for Naomi to remarry, bear sons, and then wait for those sons to grow up—by which time they themselves would be elderly and unsuitable for much younger husbands.

**שובנה**, עתה הוסיפה הצד השני, גם אם יעלה על הדעת שרצונכם להמתין עד שאנשא לאיש ואוליד בנים והבנים יגדלו, בזה יש שני ריעותות, א' מצד כי זקנתי מהיות לאיש, ב' מצד הנולדים שתצרכו להמתין עד אשר יגדלו, ובמדרש אמר אלו הייתי הלילה לאיש וגם הייתי ילדתי בנים, הא אלו הייתי הלילה לאיש ילדתי בנים, חז"ל פי' הכתובים עפמ"ש שאשה ששהתה בלא בעל עשר שנים שוב אינה יולדת, והקשו הלא בת רב חסדא שהיתה אלמנה עשר שנים וילדה אח"כ, והשיבו שהיתה דעתה להנשא ואז תוכל להוליד אף ששהתה בלא בעל עשר שנים, והנה פה כתיב וימת אלימלך וגו' וישבו שם כעשר שנים, נמצא שהתה נעמי אלמנה אחרי מות בעלה עשר שנים, ובזה א"א עוד שתלד בנים רק בא' משני אופנים, א] אם כל ימי אלמנותה לא התיאשה מלהנשא והיה דעתה להנשא שבזה תוכל לילד גם אחר עשר שנים, או גם אם לא היה דעתה להנשא יש אופן שתלד בנים אם תנשא בלילה הזאת דוקא, שלילה זו היה הלילה האחרון מן העשר שנים, וז"ש שובנה בנותי לכן כי זקנתי מהיות לאיש, ר"ל אחרי ששהתה אלמנה עשר שנים א"א שתנשא לאיש להוליד לו בנים, אמנם אמרה כי אמרתי יש לי תקוה אם בכל משך העשר שנים שישבתי אלמנה אמרתי תמיד שיש לי תקוה להנשא ולא התיאשתי מן הנשואין. שבאופן זה תוכל להוליד גם אחר עשר שנים, והצד השני הוא גם הייתי הלילה לאיש, אם אנשא לאיש בלילה הזאת שהוא הלילה האחרון שבתוך עשר שנים ועז"א במדרש אלו הייתי הלילה לאיש ילדתי בנים, ר"ל דוקא בלילה זאת, אז אשאלכם הלהן תשברנה וגו':


**“Return”**—Naomi now added a second argument. Even if one were to suppose that you wish to wait until I remarry, bear sons, and those sons grow up, there are still two obstacles: first, on my side, **“for I am too old to have a husband”**; and second, on the side of the children to be born, for you would need to wait until they matured. The Midrash says: **“Even if I were to have a husband tonight, and even if I were to bear sons”**—indeed, if I were to have a husband tonight, I could bear sons. The Sages interpret these verses in light of the principle that a woman who has remained without a husband for ten years can no longer bear children. They ask: But did not the daughter of Rav Chisda remain widowed for ten years and afterward bear children? They answer: Since she intended to remarry, she was still able to bear children even after ten years without a husband. Here Scripture says: **“And Elimelech died…”** and afterward, **“they lived there about ten years.”** Thus Naomi had remained widowed for ten years after her husband’s death. Under such circumstances, it would no longer be possible for her to bear children except in one of two ways: first, if throughout all her widowhood she had never despaired of remarriage and always intended to remarry, in which case she could still bear children even after ten years; second, even if she had not intended to remarry, there was still one possibility—that she marry precisely on this very night, since this was the final night of the ten-year period. Thus she said: **“Return, my daughters, go, for I am too old to have a husband”**—meaning that after remaining widowed for ten years, she could no longer marry in order to bear children. Yet she added: **“For I said, I have hope”**—that is, if throughout those ten years of widowhood I had always said to myself that I still had hope of remarriage and had not despaired of it, then I could still bear children. The second possibility is: **“Even if I were to have a husband tonight”**—if I were to marry tonight, which is the final night within the ten-year period. Thus the Midrash says: **“If I were to have a husband tonight, I could bear sons”**—meaning, specifically tonight. Even then, Naomi asks: **“Would you wait for them…?”**

**הלהן תשברנה**, [גדר השבר הוא המקוה על דבר ברור שהסברא נותנת שימלא תקותו בעתו, כמו שברו על ה' אלהיו, וכן אם המצא השבר בעתו, ביום אשר שברו אויבי היהודים לשלוט בהם, אליך ישברון ואתה נותן להם את אכלם בעתו] ר"ל הלא יש ספק אם יגדלו כלל כי יוכל להיות שימותו בקטנותם, אולם גם אם יגדלו הלהן תעגנה לבלתי היות לאיש, אל בנותי במדרש מפרש מלת אל, מלשון אלי כבתולה (יואל א׳:ח׳) אללי לי (מיכה ז׳:א׳) ר"ל אוי ואבוי לי יותר מכם. כי יצאה בי יד ה', יד ה' המורה על המכה לא נמצא בלשון יציאה רק בלשון הויה, תהי ידך בי, היתה בי יד ה', ולשון יציאה הבא פה מורה שכבר כלה בה כל מכותיו עד שא"א עוד שיכה אותה שנית, ועז"א במדרש יצאה בי, בי ובבני ובבעלי, ר"ל שאחר שהכה את כלם לא נשאר עוד דבר שיכה שנית, אבל בכם היתה יד ה' ולא יצאה:


**“Would you wait for them?”**—The term *sheber* (“hope” or “expectation”) refers to one who confidently expects something that reason suggests will come to pass in due time, as in: **“his hope is in Hashem his God”** (Psalms 146:5), **“if the hoped-for grain comes in its season,”** **“on the day when the enemies of the Jews hoped to rule over them”** (Esther 9:1), and **“They all look to You in hope, and You give them their food in its season”** (Psalms 104:27). Naomi’s meaning is: there is uncertainty whether such sons would even grow up at all, for they might die in childhood. But even if they did grow up, **“would you remain bound, refraining from marrying another husband for their sake?”** **“No, my daughters”**—the Midrash interprets the word **“no”** (*al*) here as related to expressions of lamentation, such as **“Lament like a virgin”** (Joel 1:8) and **“Woe is me!”** (Micah 7:1), meaning: woe and grief are greater for me than for you. **“For the hand of Hashem has gone out against me”**—the expression **“the hand of Hashem”**, which signifies Divine affliction, is ordinarily expressed with terms of being or resting upon someone, such as **“Let Your hand be upon me”** or **“the hand of Hashem was upon me.”** The wording here, **“has gone out”**, indicates that God’s blows against Naomi had already been exhausted, such that no further strike remained. Therefore the Midrash says: **“It has gone out against me”**—against me, my sons, and my husband. That is, since all had already been struck, nothing remained to be struck again. But regarding you, **“the hand of Hashem was upon you”**—it had not yet **“gone out.”**

**אל תפגעי בי**, [במדרש אמרה לה לא תחטא עלי לא תסבון פגעך מני, ר"ל כי פגיעה משמש על ג' דברים: הריגה ויפגע בו וימת, פגישה ופגע ביריחו, תחנונים ופגעו לי בעפרון, ולדעת המדרש תחנונים ופגישה ענינם א' שמבקש ומצפה שחברו יצא לקראתו ברצון לבבו למלא בקשתו, ולז"א שתפסה לשון פגיעה אם מענין הריגה שע"י שתעזוב אותה תמות מיתת הנפש, ועז"א לא תחטא עלי, אם מענין פגישה ותחנונים, ר"ל אל תחשוב שלבבי יפגעך בבקשה זאת ויהיה לבבי כלבבך ותסבון ר"ל תקח פגעך ממני]. **לעזבך לשוב מאחריך**, ר"ל לא שאעזוב אותך כי איני רוצה להפרד ממך ואף לא לשוב מאחריך שאף אם אצטרך לעזבך לא אשוב מאחריך לארץ מואב כי בכל זה אלך לארץ יהודה ואתגייר שם ע"י אחרים [וכ"ה במדרש לשוב מאחריך מ"מ דעתי להתגייר אלא מוטב על ידך ולא ע"י אחרים], **כי אל אשר תלכי אלך,** ר"ל בל תחשוב כי תכלית הליכתי משונה מן תכלית הליכתך שאת הולכת לשם לבעבור דתך שתוכל לשמור מצות התלויות בארץ והתורה והמצוה ואני הולך רק בעבור השגת איזה תועלת, כי תכלית הליכתי הוא עצמו התכלית שאת הולכת אליו, וכן אל תחשבי שאני מקוה איזו הצלחה זמניית שאנשא לאיש עשיר וכדומה כי **באשר תליני אלין**, אלין כגר בארץ וכצדיקים שהעה"ז הוא אצלם רק כמלון אורחים, כי מגמת הליכתי הוא מצד כי **עמך עמי ואלהיך אלהי**, שכבר תפשתי תורת אלהיך ומנהגי בני עמך ואני כאחת מבני עמך:


**“Do not urge me”**—[The Midrash explains: “Do not sin against me; do not withdraw your affection from me.” The term *pegi‘ah* (“encounter” or “entreaty”) is used in three senses: killing, as in **“he struck him and he died”**; meeting, as in **“he came upon Jericho”**; and supplication, as in **“intercede for me with Ephron.”** According to the Midrash, supplication and encounter are related in meaning, referring to one who requests and hopes that another will willingly come forward to fulfill the request. Thus Ruth intentionally uses the term *pegi‘ah*: if in the sense of killing, then by Naomi abandoning her she would suffer a spiritual death—hence, “do not sin against me”; and if in the sense of meeting or supplication, then: do not think that my heart merely entreats you in this matter and that I will simply yield to your wishes—*tesubon* meaning “withdraw your entreaty from me.”] **“To leave you, to turn back from following after you”**—That is, not only do I refuse to leave you, for I do not wish to part from you, but even if I were somehow forced to separate from you, I would still not **“turn back from following after you”** to the land of Moab. Rather, I would still go to the land of Judah and convert there through others. [So too the Midrash: **“to turn back from following after you”**—even so, my intention is to convert, though it is preferable to do so through you rather than through others.] **“For wherever you go, I will go”**—Do not think that the purpose of my journey differs from yours: that you are going for the sake of your faith, in order to observe the commandments dependent on the Land and the Torah and its commandments, while I go only for some personal benefit. Rather, the very purpose of my journey is the same as yours. Likewise, do not think that I hope for some worldly success, such as marrying a wealthy man or the like, for **“wherever you lodge, I will lodge”**—I will dwell as a stranger in the land, and like the righteous, for whom this world is merely a temporary lodging. For the goal of my journey is that **“your people shall be my people, and your God my God”**—I have already embraced the teaching of your God and the customs of your people, and I am as one of your people.

**“Where you die”**—The primary purpose of my journey is this: **“Where you die, I will die”**—that I may die the death of the upright, as you will die, with the spirit returning to the bundle of life. And **“there I will be buried”**—so that I may be buried in the place where you are buried, in the Holy Land and among the graves of the righteous, who are destined to rise again in the resurrection. **“Thus may God do to me, and more as well”**—Ruth swore an oath to uphold her words, affirming that this was her true intention: **“for only death shall separate me from you”**—that is, the reason I wish to journey with you is because I have seen and understood that death creates a great distinction between us. Though in life I was attached to you, and although religion separated us, we nevertheless remained bound together in affection; yet after death we would be separated, for you would cleave to the God of spirits, while I would be cast away among the ranks of idol worshippers. Therefore, I seek to convert, so that even in death we will not be separated. Through these words Ruth revealed that the foundations of faith were already firmly established in her heart: belief in the existence and unity of God, as implied by **“your God shall be my God”**; acceptance of the Torah of Israel and their way of life, as implied by **“wherever you go, I will go”**—meaning, I will walk in the path of the righteous and in the ways of the Torah—and likewise by **“your people shall be my people.”** She also believed in the immortality of the soul, reward and punishment in the World to Come, and the resurrection of the dead, as implied by **“where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried.”** Our Sages also derived from these words that Ruth accepted the practical commandments, interpreting **“wherever you go, I will go”** as referring, for example, to the laws of Sabbath observance. They further said: whatever commandments and good deeds one can acquire must be acquired in this world, for in the World to Come, **“death shall separate me from you.”**

**“And she said to them: Do not call me Naomi”**—Consider a person who once possessed immense wealth, like the riches of Ahasuerus, and then lost nearly all of it, retaining only a thousand gold coins. One might say that he had fallen from his estate and become poor, but this description would make sense only if one recalled the scale of his former wealth. Relative to his previous riches, he would now be poor and diminished; yet if one ignored his earlier condition, a person with a thousand gold coins would still be considered wealthy. However, if that same great rich man lost everything and was left without even a single coin or a piece of bread to quiet his hunger, then he could be called poor and destitute even without reference to his former standing. The people of the city, seeing that Naomi and her daughter-in-law had arrived on foot with no attendants to serve them, understood that they had lost the wealth they once possessed. Yet they still assumed that Naomi retained some gold and jewelry—enough that an ordinary person would have been content to possess such means. Therefore they exclaimed: **“Is this Naomi?”**—thereby invoking her former standing, meaning that in comparison with her previous wealth she had fallen astonishingly far. Naomi replied to them that things were not as they supposed. It was not the case that she could be called **“Mara”** (“bitter”) only by comparison to her former identity as **“Naomi”** (“pleasant”), such that only relative to her previous prosperity did her decline become evident. Rather, she informed them that there was no need to mention her former condition at all, for she was now so impoverished that nothing remained in her possession [after all, her daughter-in-law had to glean among the poor]. Therefore, they could call her **“Mara”** even without recalling that she had once been Naomi. Thus she said: **“Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara”**—meaning, you may call me **“Mara”** without first calling me **“Naomi,”** that is, without recalling my former name and standing, **“for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me.”**

**שאני מלאה הלכתי** בכ"ז ריקם השיבני ה' ואין בידי מאומה, וא"כ תוכלו לקרוא לי בשם מרה עניה ואביונה גם מבלי הזכיר שמי נעמי שהיה לי מקודם בעת הצלחתי, למה תקראנה לי נעמי, הנה עד עתה לא השיבה רק שאין מן הצורך להזכיר שמה נעמי שהיה לה מקודם ועתה חדשה דבר אחר ותאמר כי עתה נתגלה לה כי לא היה ראוי כלל לקראה בשם נעמי שעז"א למה תקראנה לי נעמי כאומר גם מה שאתם אומרים שהייתי נקראת מקודם בשם נעמי הוא שקר, והוא עפ"מ שהתבאר אצלנו פעמים רבות שלפעמים כשירצה ה' להעניש בעונש גדול מאד, יגביה את האדם בגובה המעלה ורום ההצלחה כדי שאם יורידנו משם אל העוני והריש ירגיש כאבו וצערו יותר ותהיה מפלתו יותר גדולה כמ"ש (איוב כ׳:ו׳) אם יעלה לשמים שיאו וגו' כגללו לנצח יאבד, ואמר (עובדיה) ואם בין כוכבים שים קנך, הוא כדי שמשם אורידך, ותהיה המפלה גדולה יותר, וכן חשבה שמה שהצליחה ה' בזמן הקודם בעושר גדול וכבוד היה כדי שיורידה מן המעלה הגדולה הזאת אל העוני והשפלות שבזה הירידה קשה יותר ומפלתה גדולה יותר, וא"כ אחר שההצלחה הגדולה הקדומה היה רק כעין התראה שתשוב אל ה' כי הגביה אותה להשליכה משם אל עמקי בור א"כ שם נעמי שנקראת בו בעת הזאת לא היה בצדק כי ההצלחה הזאת היתה מרה מאד שהוא עליה לצורך ירידה, וז"ש אחר שאני מלאה הלכתי כדי שריקם השיבני ה', וא"כ מה שהייתי מלאה אז זה מגדיל הצער ביותר עתה, א"כ למה תקראנה לי נעמי, גם מה שקראתם לי שהייתי נקראת בתחלה נעמי הוא שקר כי ה' ענה בי, ר"ל ההצלחה הקודמת היתה רק התראה שבזה התרה בי שאיטיב את דרכי כי ישליכני ממרום ההצלחה הזאת לעומק, וכן היה שבהצלחה הזאת שדי הרע לי שעי"כ גדול צערי ונפילתי כפלים, ובמדרש אומר הזאת נעמי לשעבר היתה מהלכת באיסקפיטיאות ועכשיו יחפה, לשעבר בבגדי מילתן ועכשיו בסמרטוטין הזאת נעמי, ואומרת אליהן אל תקראנה לי נעמי קראן לי מרה [מבואר שזה פי' המדרש עמ"ש אל תקראנה לי נעמי נגד מה שהם הזכירו את העבר שלא לצורך] בר קפרא אומר לפרה הדיוטית שהעמידוה בעליה בשוק אמר רדינית היא [פי' פרה חורשת] אמרין אם רדינית היא אלין מכותיה דאית בה מה אינון, כך נעמי למה תקראנה לי נעמי וה' ענה בי [זה פי' ע"מ שאמרה שנית למה תקראנה לי נעמי שהוא שגם קודם לכן לא היתה נעמי כי היתה מוכנת למכות ולעוני]:


**“I went away full”**—yet **“Hashem has brought me back empty,”** and I have nothing left in my possession. Therefore, you may call me **“Mara”**—poor and destitute—even without recalling my former name, **“Naomi,”** which I bore in my time of prosperity. **“Why call me Naomi?”**—Until this point, Naomi had only answered that there was no need to mention her former name, Naomi, which she had borne in the past. But now she adds something further: she realized that it had never truly been appropriate to call her Naomi at all. Thus she says: **“Why call me Naomi?”**—as if to say: even what you say, that I was formerly called Naomi, is false. This follows a principle explained elsewhere: sometimes, when God intends to punish a person with an especially severe punishment, He first raises that person to great heights of success and honor, so that if He later casts him down into poverty and humiliation, the pain and anguish will be felt more sharply, and the fall will be far greater. As it says in Job (20:6): **“Though his height rise to heaven… like his own refuse he shall perish forever,”** and in Obadiah: **“Though you set your nest among the stars”**—it is for the purpose that **“from there I will bring you down,”** making the downfall more severe. Thus Naomi came to think that the great wealth and honor God had granted her in earlier days had been given only so that He might cast her down from that lofty position into poverty and degradation, making her descent all the more painful and devastating. If so, since that earlier prosperity was merely a kind of warning to return to God—He raised her up only in order to cast her down into the depths—then the name **“Naomi”**, by which she had been called at that time, had never been truly fitting, for that prosperity itself was bitter, being merely an ascent for the sake of a greater fall. Thus she says: **“I went away full”**—only so that **“Hashem has brought me back empty.”** Therefore, the very fact that I was once full only increases my pain now. Thus, **“Why call me Naomi?”** Even your statement that I was once rightly called Naomi is false, for **“Hashem has testified against me”**—meaning that my earlier prosperity was itself a warning, through which God cautioned me to improve my ways, lest He cast me down from the height of that success into the depths. And so it happened: through that very prosperity, **“the Almighty has afflicted me,”** for it made my sorrow and downfall doubly painful. The Midrash says: **“Is this Naomi?”** Formerly she walked in fine carriages, and now barefoot; formerly in garments of fine wool, and now in rags—**“Is this Naomi?”** And she replies to them: **“Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara”**—this, the Midrash explains, corresponds to Naomi’s rejection of their unnecessary invocation of her former state. Bar Kappara gave a parable: a common cow was set up in the marketplace by its owner, and he declared, “It is a plowing cow.” People responded: “If it truly is a plowing cow, then what are all these wounds upon it?” So too Naomi says: **“Why call me Naomi, when Hashem has testified against me?”**—meaning, even previously she had not truly been Naomi, for she had already been destined for suffering and poverty.

**ותשב נעמי**, ר"ל כי בצאתה מבית לחם עזבה שדותיה [ויוכל להיות שבתוך כך החזיקו אחרים בחלקת השדה וכמו שהיה בהאשה השונמית בצאתה לארץ פלשתים שהוצרכה לצעוק אל המלך אל ביתה ואל שדה] והשתדלה עד ששבה אל נחלתה, ואמר ורות כלתה עמה שגם היא היה לה זכיה בהשדה מצד בעלה יורש אביו, ועז"א שנית השבה משדי מואב, שאם היתה נשארת בשדי מואב היתה מוחלת על חלקה ובפרט למ"ש חז"ל שמחלה על כתובתה, לא כן עתה ששבה משדי מואב היה לה זכיה בשדה כמ"ש לקמן (סי' ד'). ובמדרש אומר זו היא השבה משדה מואב ר"ל שהיא האשה הראשונה שבאה משדי מואב להתגייר כי עד עתה לא דרשו עמוני ולא עמונית. והמה באו בית לחם בתחלת קציר שעורים. זה הצעה למה שיספר שהלכה לקחת מתנת עניים הגם שהיה לה חלק בחלקת השדה כי באו בעת קציר שא"א אז לזרוע את השדה והיו עניים באותה שעה:


**“And Naomi returned”**—That is, when Naomi departed from Bethlehem, she had abandoned her fields. It is possible that in the meantime others had taken possession of her parcel of land, just as happened with the Shunammite woman when she left for the land of the Philistines and later had to cry out to the king concerning her house and field. Naomi therefore made efforts until she regained possession of her inheritance. Scripture adds: **“and Ruth her daughter-in-law with her”**—for Ruth also had a claim to the field through her husband, as an heir to his father. Thus Scripture again says: **“the one who returned from the fields of Moab”**—for had Ruth remained in the fields of Moab, she would have relinquished her share, especially according to the Sages’ teaching that she waived her marriage settlement. But now that she returned from the fields of Moab, she retained a claim to the field, as will later be explained (chapter 4). The Midrash says concerning **“the one who returned from the fields of Moab”**: this means that Ruth was the first woman to come from the fields of Moab in order to convert, for until that time the verse **“An Ammonite or Moabite shall not enter the congregation”** had not yet been interpreted as excluding women (*“an Ammonite, but not an Ammonitess; a Moabite, but not a Moabitess”*). **“And they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest”**—This serves as preparation for what follows, explaining why Ruth went to gather the gifts for the poor, even though she had a share in the field: they arrived during the harvest season, when it was no longer possible to sow the land, and at that time they were counted among the poor.

**ולנעמי מודע לאישה**, יודיעני שהגם שנעמי לפי עניה עתה היה טוב לה אם היתה מבקשת משען לחם מבועז קרובה העשיר בחרה יותר שכלתה תביא לחמה מלקט עניים שזכתה לה התורה והוא כאלו היא פרנסתה ממתנת ה' בעת עוני מלפנות לקבל חסד מקרובה אשר היה מכירה בעת עשרה ובושה לגלות לפניו מחסורה ובשגם כבר היה בלבה כי הוא יגאל את השדה ואת רות אהובתה, ולבל נאמר שלכן לא פנתה אל בועז מפני שיראה שישיב פניה ריקם ע"ז הקדים שבועז היה מנהל אותה בלחם אם מצד שהי' מודע ומכיר ואוהב בין לנעמי בין לאישה עז"א ולנעמי מודע לאישה [ומודע ר"ל אוהבה ומכירה היטב לא אח רחוק] וגם היה ממשפחת אלימלך והוא בעצמו היה איש גבור חיל שכבר בארתי (בפ' יתרו י"ח כ"ה) ששם אנשי חיל כולל כל המדות הטובות ובכללם הנדיבות ושנאת הבצע, והיה איש גדול נודע בשם שעז"א ושמו בועז וא"כ בודאי לא היה מעלים עיניו משאר בשרו:


**“Now Naomi had a kinsman of her husband”**—Scripture informs us that although, given Naomi’s poverty, it would have been better for her to seek support and sustenance from Boaz, her wealthy relative, she preferred instead that her daughter-in-law obtain food through the gleanings granted to the poor by the Torah. In this way, it would be as though her livelihood came as a gift from God in her time of distress, rather than from the kindness of a relative who had known her in her days of wealth, before whom she would feel ashamed to reveal her need. Furthermore, Naomi had already hoped in her heart that Boaz would redeem both the field and her beloved Ruth. Lest one suppose that Naomi refrained from approaching Boaz because she feared he would turn her away empty-handed, Scripture first explains that Boaz would certainly have sustained her. This was true, first, because he was a **“kinsman”**—that is, one who knew and cared deeply for both Naomi and her husband, as indicated by **“Naomi had a kinsman of her husband”**; and *moda‘* here means not merely a distant relative, but one who was well acquainted with and devoted to them. Second, he was **“of the family of Elimelech.”** Moreover, he himself was **“a man of valor”** (*ish gibbor chayil*). As explained elsewhere (Exodus 18:25), the term *anshei chayil* encompasses all noble qualities, including generosity and freedom from greed. Finally, he was a prominent and distinguished man, as indicated by **“and his name was Boaz.”** Surely, then, he would not have ignored the needs of his own kin.

**ותאמר רות**, מספר צדקת רות וטיב מדותיה שהגם שהיתה אמונה עלי תולע ולדעת חז"ל היתה בת מלך מואב, הסכימה ללכת ללקוט כאחד העניים, ב] שלא חפצה שגם חמותה תלך עמה שדי בזיון וכלמה אחר שנודעה בעיר הזאת לגברת ועשירה, וקבלה ע"ע שהיא תלך בשאין מכירים אותה, שעז"א ותאמר רות המואביה ר"ל שהיא ממדינה אחרת ולא תבוש והיא תפרנס את חמותה בכבוד, ואמרה אלכה נא השדה, ר"ל לא בכרם שיש שם סכנה לעלות באילנות, ואמרה ואלקטה בשבלים ר"ל שלא תקח רק לקט שאין העניים מקפידים ע"ז שיש שדות הרבה וכ"א מלקט בשדה מיוחד, משא"כ פאה שכל אחד חוטף מחברו שהדין הוא אם צ"ט אומרים לחלק וא' אומר לבוז שומעים למי שאומר לבוז, וא"כ יבוזו העניים בזרוע כחם ואם תרצה לבוז יפגעו בה מרי נפש, וגם בהלקט אמרה אחר אשר אמצא חן בעיניו, שאם אראה בעל השדה מביט עלי בעין רעה לא אלקט שם, עד שאראה שמצאתי חן ושלא יגיע לי בזיון מהקוצרים או יתר העניים:


**“And Ruth said”**—Scripture relates Ruth’s righteousness and noble character. Although she had been accustomed to luxury and delicacy, and according to the Sages was the daughter of the king of Moab, she nevertheless agreed to go and glean like one of the poor. Secondly, she did not wish her mother-in-law to accompany her, for it would have been disgraceful and humiliating for Naomi, who had once been known in that city as a wealthy and distinguished woman. Ruth therefore took it upon herself to go alone, where she would not be recognized. Thus it says: **“And Ruth the Moabite said”**—meaning, since she came from another land, she would not be ashamed, and she would provide for her mother-in-law honorably. She said: **“Let me go now to the field”**—meaning, not to a vineyard, where there would be danger in climbing among the vines. She added: **“and glean among the ears of grain”**—meaning, she would gather only *leket* (gleanings), over which the poor generally did not quarrel, since there were many fields and each poor person gleaned in a different field. This differed from *pe’ah* (the corner of the field left for the poor), where the poor would snatch from one another, for the law is that if ninety-nine say the produce should be divided and one says it should be seized freely, the ruling follows the one who says it should be seized. Thus the poor would struggle forcefully over it, and if Ruth sought to take part she might be harmed by embittered people. Even regarding gleanings, she said: **“after him in whose sight I shall find favor”**—meaning, if she saw that the owner of the field looked upon her unfavorably, she would not glean there, but would remain only where she found favor and would not suffer humiliation from the harvesters or the other poor.

**ותלך**, וספר שלא הלכה דרך רב רק תכף שהלכה ותבוא [בשדה] ותלקט בשדה, כי השדה של בועז היה קרוב לביתה ותבוא שם תכף, והגם שהיה בבוקר שאז מחלקים פאה כמ"ש (בפ"ד דפאה) שלש אבעיות ביום, לא הלכה אל מקום הפאה שהוא בסוף השדה רק ותבוא ותלקט, שהלכה תכף ללקט לקט לבד, וע"כ לקטה אחרי הקוצרים לא אחרי עניים המלקטים כי לקטה היא לבדה. ויקר מקרה, המקרה הוא דבר שאינו מחויב מסבת הרגילות, ויספר הכתוב שהגם שנראה שמה שבאת אל שדה הזה דוקא הוא מקרה גמור, באמת היא מקרה, ר"ל מקרה מיוחד לצרכה, ולפ"ז היה מקרה השגחיית כי בענינים שירצה ה' להוציא ענין כולל לתועלת הכלל שם תלוה ההשגחה אל מעשים המקריים, כמ"ש אליעזר הקרה נא לפני היום, שרצה בזה שלא ימתין ה' עד יקדמו הסבות הממציאות והמחייבות את הדבר רק שיבא הדבר בדרך מקרה בלתי נתלה מסבות ממציאות ומחייבות אותו, וכן יאמר שהמקרה הזה היה השגחיי והיה מקרה מכוון לצרכה, שה' הזמין שהשדה שתבוא בו ראשונה ושתבחר ללקט שם יהיה השדה אשר לבועז וזה סבב ה' בעבור שבועז היה ממשפחת אלימלך, והיה מוכן להיות הגואל וליבמה שתצא מזה מלכות בית דוד:


**“And she went”**—Scripture relates that Ruth did not travel a long distance, for as soon as she went, **“she came and gleaned in the field”**; Boaz’s field was near her home, and she arrived there immediately. Although it was morning—the time when *pe’ah* (the corner of the field for the poor) was distributed, as taught in the Mishnah (Pe’ah 4), that there were three appointed times during the day—she did not go to the place of the *pe’ah* at the edge of the field. Rather, **“she came and gleaned”**—meaning, she immediately went only to gather *leket* (gleanings). Therefore, she gleaned **“after the reapers”** and not after other poor people who were gathering, because she alone was gleaning there. **“And her chance chanced”**—A *mikreh* (“chance occurrence”) is something not necessitated by the normal course of events. Scripture relates that although it appeared entirely coincidental that she happened to come to this particular field, in truth it was *mikreh*—that is, a special occurrence directed for her benefit. Thus, this was providential chance. For in matters through which God intends to bring about something of broad consequence for the benefit of the community, Divine Providence accompanies seemingly accidental events. Thus Eliezer prayed: **“Cause it to happen before me today”** (Genesis 24:12), meaning that God should not wait for the ordinary causes and conditions that naturally bring about an event, but should instead cause it to occur through what appears to be chance, independent of ordinary causal chains. So too here: this occurrence was providential and deliberately directed for Ruth’s sake. God arranged that the first field she entered and chose for gleaning would be the field of Boaz. God brought this about because Boaz was from the family of Elimelech and was destined to be the redeemer and to marry her in levirate-like redemption, from whom the Davidic monarchy would emerge.

**והנה בועז בא**, כ"מ שנאמר והנה מורה דבר חדש, שבועז לא היה רגיל לבא אל השדה והיה גם זה מקרה חדש בהשגחת ה' שבועז בא מבית לחם, ויאמר לקוצרים ה' עמכם, חז"ל אמרו שבועז ובית דינו תקנו שיהיו שואלים שלום חברו בשם, והיה בעצת הסנהדרין כמ"ש בברכות (דף סג) וכי תימא בועז מדעתא דנפשיה עביד וכו' כי אין להזכיר ש"ש לבטלה וכמ"ש בספרא (ויקרא סי' יד) דלא לימא לה' קרבן וכו', אך בראותם אז שהי' ביניהם גזל משפט וצדק וגם אל שופטיהם לא שמעו כמ"ש ויהי בימי שפוט השופטים, וכאשר נתמנה בועז לשופט תקן שיהיו שואלים שלום חברו בשם, לקבוע בלבם כי ה' משמים משקיף עליהם ועל שלומם, ושלום הקבוץ משתתף עם השגחת ה' עד שה' הנקרא שלום משתתף עם שלום שבין אדם לחברו, וגם התעוררות שישוו ה' לנגדם תמיד וגם בדברים שבינם לבין רעיהם ודרישת שלומם וטובתם, ועז"א והנה בועז בא מבית לחם שמספר שנעשה דבר חדש בבית לחם במושב הזקנים שע"ז מורה מלת והנה, ובועז בא משם בדבר החדש שאמר לקוצרים ה' עמכם, ועפ"ז הזכירו גם הם ש"ש שיברכהו ה', וכמ"ש ולא אמרו העוברים ברכת ה' עליכם וכו' וכמש"פ שם:


**“And behold, Boaz came”**—Wherever Scripture says **“and behold”** (*ve-hinei*), it signals something new. Boaz was not accustomed to coming to the field, and this too was a new occurrence brought about through Divine Providence: **“Boaz came from Bethlehem.”** **“And he said to the reapers: Hashem be with you”**—Our Sages said that Boaz and his court instituted the practice of greeting one another using the Divine Name. This was done with the approval of the Sanhedrin, as explained in Berakhot (63a): **“And lest you say that Boaz acted on his own initiative…”** For one may not invoke God’s Name unnecessarily, as taught in the Sifra (Leviticus, §14), that one should not say, **“To Hashem, a sacrifice,”** and the like. However, seeing that in those days robbery, injustice, and corruption prevailed, and that people did not even heed their judges—as Scripture says, **“And it came to pass in the days when the judges judged”**—when Boaz was appointed as a judge, he instituted the practice that people greet one another in the Name of God. This was to establish firmly in their hearts that Hashem watches over them from heaven and cares for their well-being, and that the peace of society is joined with Divine Providence, such that God—who is Himself called **“Peace”**—is joined with the peace between one person and another. It also served to awaken people to keep God continually before them, even in matters between themselves and their fellows, and to seek one another’s welfare and good. Thus Scripture says: **“And behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem”**—indicating that something new had taken place in Bethlehem, among the council of elders, as signaled by the word **“and behold.”** Boaz came from there carrying this new practice, as shown when he said to the reapers: **“Hashem be with you.”** Therefore, they too invoked the Divine Name in reply: **“May Hashem bless you,”** as in the verse: **“Nor have those who pass by said: ‘The blessing of Hashem be upon you…’”** (Psalms 129:8), as explained there.

**ותאמר**, זה אמר לו מה שלא שאל, שלא לקחה לא חלק בפאה ובשכחה רק לקט לבד וז"ש שאמרה אלקטה בשבלים אחרי הקוצרים ואספתי בעמרים, ר"ל שהעניים אחר שלקחו חלקם בפאה הלכו להם לשדות אחרים כי יש שדות רבות כמספר העניים, וכשרואים שיש בשדה זו מלקט א' הולך כ"א לשדה אחרת ששם לוקט לבדו, והיא נשארה פה ללקט אחרי הקוצרים (לא אחרי המלקטים שלא נמצא זולתה), ואמרה ואספתי בעמרים, ר"ל שכל מה שאספה בכדי עומר עשתה ממנו עומר, ולא תאמר שגנבה עומר מן העמרים של השדה כי אספה אחרי הקוצרים וסמוך להם, וראו שלא לקחה דבר שלא כשורה, ולא תתפלא שיש לה עמרים רבים כי ותבוא ותעמוד ומפרש ותבוא מאז הבקר ותעמוד עד עתה שעד עתה לא נחה רגע רק עתה בבא בועז ישבה לנוח מעט בבית שהי' שם בשדה שיאכלו הפועלים וינוחו שם, אמר זה שבתה הבית מעט, זה רגע שישבה לנוח בבית שהיה שם וישבה רק מעט ועד עתה לא הרגיעה רגע וע"כ אספה אלומות:


**“And she said”**—This was information the servant volunteered, beyond what Boaz had asked. He explained that Ruth had not taken a share from the *pe’ah* (the corner of the field) or from forgotten sheaves, but only gleanings (*leket*). Thus he reported that she had said: **“Let me glean, I pray, among the ears of grain after the reapers, and gather among the sheaves.”** That is, after the poor had taken their portion from the *pe’ah*, they departed for other fields, since there were many fields corresponding to the number of poor people. Whenever they saw that one poor person was already gleaning in a field, each would go elsewhere so as to glean alone. But Ruth remained here to gather **“after the reapers”**—not after other gleaners, since there were none besides her. And when she said: **“and gather among the sheaves”**, the meaning is that whatever she collected, once it amounted to the size of a sheaf, she tied into a bundle. One should not suspect that she had stolen a sheaf from the stacked produce of the field, for she gathered immediately behind the reapers, where all could see that she took nothing improperly. Nor should it seem surprising that she had gathered so many sheaves, for **“she came and has remained”**—that is, **“from the morning until now.”** She had not rested even for a moment; only now, when Boaz arrived, had she sat briefly in the shelter in the field, where the workers would eat and rest. Thus the servant said: **“her sitting in the house was but for a little”**—this refers to the brief moment she sat to rest in the shelter. Until now, she had not paused even for an instant, and therefore she had gathered so much.

Since the city gate was the seat of the Sanhedrin, young women were not permitted to draw water there. Rather, the young men drew water from that place for drinking. Because it was burdensome to go there, they would not ordinarily give the poor from that water, but instead supplied them with water drawn from a nearer source. Boaz therefore told Ruth that she could drink from the water that the young men had drawn, for he had instructed them not to prevent her from doing so.

**ישלם**, נגד מה שעשית עם חמותך ישלם ה' פעלך, ונגד מה שהתגיירת תהי משכרתך שלמה מעם ה', מצד שהוא אלהי ישראל ומשגיח עליהם ועל הגרים הנלוים עליהם. ובמדרש אמר ר' חסא אשר באת לחסות תחת כנפיו, והוא עפ"י מה שכתבתי בפי' התו"ה (קדושים סי' למ"ד) שיש הבדל בין שכר ובין פעולה, ובין שכיר ובין פועל, שהפועל או אומן יקבל שכר בעד המלאכה אשר עשה, למשל החייט עשה בגד ומקבל שכר מלאכתו, וזה נקרא פעולה שלוקח שכר פעולתו, אבל השכיר מושכר לזמן קצוב למשל שלש שנים, בין יעשה איזה מלאכה בין לא יעשה. והנה על כל מצוה ומצוה יקבל שכר בפ"ע כפועל ועז"א ישלם ה' פעלך, אולם על מה שבאה להתגייר שאז קבלה עליה לעבוד עבודת ה' ולקיים כל מצותיו והיא כמושכרת לו כל ימי חייה לעבוד עבודתו, ומאז מגיע לה שכר שכיר שישלם לה שכר תמידי בין תעשה בין לא תעשה [שאף שלא תגיע איזו מצוה לידה בכ"ז תקבל שכרה משלם] ועז"א ותהי משכרתך שלמה ר"ל שכר תמידי זה בעבור אשר באת לחסות שאז נעשית שכירה לה' על כל ימי חייך ומגיע לך שכר תמידי, וע"ז אמר ר' חסא אשר באת לחסות ר"ל שעל שעה זו מגיע לך השכר התמידי וחוץ מזה בכל פעם שתקיים מצוה בפועל מגיע שכר פעולה ביחוד חוץ מן השכר הכולל. והנה כבר חקרתי בדרושי אה"ש שאחר שזה בעצמו שיזכה האדם לעשות מצות ה' ולעבוד עבודת המלך הגדול ית' הוא השכר היותר גדול, אם מצד השלמות הנפשי שיקנה ע"י עשית המצוה אם מצד שבזה הוא עבד מלך גדול בורא כל, וא"כ יפלא למה הבטיח בכל התורה שכר מצות הלא קיום המצוה בעצמה הוא השכר היותר גדול [עד"ש שכר מצוה מצוה שהמצוה בעצמה היא השכר היותר גדול של המצוה], ואמרתי שבאמת למי שיש לו לב להבין ונפש להרגיש נועם המצוה ועריבותה א"צ להבטיח לו שכר זולת המצוה בעצמה, אבל באשר רוב בני אדם אין משיגים נועם המצוה והאושר הגדול שיקנו על ידה הוצרך להבטיח שכר עה"ז שזה מורגש אצלם כדי שיעשו המצוה בעבור תקות השכר עד יגיעו למדרגה להרגיש ולהבין לעשות המצוה לשמה מצד המצוה בעצמה, וז"ש ישלם ה' פעלך היינו שה' יתן לך שכר פעולתך אבל האמת היא ותהי משכרתך שלמה מעם ה', ר"ל שעיקר השכר בשלמות יהיה זה בעצמו מה שבאת לחסות תחת כנפיו, שזה עצמו הוא השכר השלם בלא חסרון כלל ועז"א ר' חסא אשר באת לחסות ר"ל שזה עצמו הוא עיקר השכר:


**“May Hashem repay”**—Corresponding to what you have done for your mother-in-law, **“May Hashem repay your deed”**; and corresponding to your conversion, **“May your reward be complete from Hashem”**, insofar as He is the God of Israel, who watches over Israel and over the converts who join themselves to them. The Midrash cites Rabbi Chasa: **“under whose wings you have come to take refuge.”** This follows a distinction explained elsewhere (Torat HaKohanim, Kedoshim §340) between *sachar* (“wages” or “reward”) and *pe‘ulah* (“work” or “deed”), and between a *sachir* (“hired laborer”) and a *po‘el* (“worker” or “artisan”). A worker or artisan receives payment for a particular task completed—for example, a tailor makes a garment and receives payment for that specific work. This is called *pe‘ulah*, for he receives compensation for his labor. By contrast, a hired laborer is engaged for a fixed period of time—say, three years—whether or not he performs any particular task. Thus, for each individual commandment, a person receives reward separately, like a worker; this is the meaning of **“May Hashem repay your deed.”** However, by converting, Ruth accepted upon herself the service of God and the obligation to fulfill all His commandments, becoming, as it were, hired to Him for the rest of her life to perform His service. From that point onward, she merits the wages of a hired servant—a continual reward, whether or not any specific commandment comes before her [for even if no particular mitzvah presents itself, she still receives her full reward]. Therefore, **“May your reward be complete”** refers to this continual reward, because **“you have come to take refuge”**—through conversion, you became, as it were, God’s servant for all your days, and thus merit a continual recompense. Hence Rabbi Chasa emphasizes: **“you have come to take refuge”**—meaning, for this very act you merit an enduring reward, in addition to the separate reward granted each time you actively fulfill a commandment. Furthermore, I have already explained elsewhere (in my discourses on *Ahavat HaShem*) that since the very privilege of meriting to perform God’s commandments and serve the great King, blessed be He, is itself the greatest reward—whether because of the spiritual perfection attained through the mitzvah, or because through it one becomes a servant of the Creator—one might wonder why the Torah promises external rewards for mitzvot at all. After all, as the Sages said: **“The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah”**—the commandment itself is its own greatest reward. I explained that in truth, for one who possesses the heart to understand and the soul to feel the sweetness and delight of a mitzvah, no reward beyond the mitzvah itself is needed. But since most people do not perceive the delight of mitzvot or the profound happiness attained through them, the Torah had to promise worldly rewards that people can readily appreciate, so that they would perform the commandments in hope of reward until they rise to the level of serving God for the sake of the mitzvah itself. Thus, **“May Hashem repay your deed”** means that God will grant reward for your actions; but in truth, **“May your reward be complete from Hashem”** means that the highest and most perfect reward is this itself: that you have come to take refuge beneath His wings. That alone is the complete reward, lacking nothing. Thus Rabbi Chasa says: **“you have come to take refuge”**—meaning that this itself is the essential reward.

You spoke to their hearts by assuring them that reward for their conversion was guaranteed by God, and so too you have spoken to me. This is a great honor in my eyes, for I do not consider myself worthy to be as one of your maidservants—I regard myself as lower and lesser than they—yet you have elevated me to this status, to be as one of your maidservants.

Certainly, Boaz reclined with them in order to obligate them in the full Grace after Meals. Boaz sat at the head, while the reapers sat below. Therefore he said to Ruth: **“Come here”**—meaning, sit in a higher place beside Boaz. [The word *halom* (“here”) consistently denotes a place elevated above one’s normal station; thus the Sages said: **“‘Halom’ refers only to royalty.”**] **“And eat of the bread”**—*lechem* (“bread”) refers to a whole loaf, whereas *pat* refers to bread broken into pieces (as explained in Torat HaKohanim on Leviticus). Boaz gave her a whole loaf to belong to her personally [which is why she later brought the leftovers home], and told her to eat from the loaf. The piece that she cut off to eat during the meal, **“dip your morsel in the vinegar.”** **“And she sat”**—yet Ruth did not sit beside Boaz, but lower down among the reapers. Boaz further extended roasted grain to her as a delicacy and gave to her generously, until **“she ate, was satisfied, and had some left over.”**

**“And Naomi said to her daughter-in-law”**—Naomi first blessed him: **“Blessed be he of Hashem, who has not forsaken His kindness”**—for she understood that Boaz had acted in this way in order to provide honorably for Naomi and Ruth, who were living and members of his family. **“Toward the living”**—meaning, Naomi and Ruth; **“and toward the dead”**—for Naomi also understood that Boaz intended to show kindness to the dead through levirate-like redemption, by which he would benefit the soul of the deceased. Naomi then explained her words. Regarding **“His kindness toward the living”**, she said: **“The man is a close relative of ours”**—and therefore he shows kindness to his living relatives. Regarding **“toward the dead”**, she said: **“He is one of our redeemers”**—for the redeemer has the responsibility to perform levirate-like redemption, thereby redeeming the soul of the deceased so that his name not be erased from Israel. She said: **“He is one of our redeemers”**—for there was another redeemer as well, and Boaz was one among them.

As the Sages said concerning the woman who married a tax collector and ended up tying the tax-collector’s knots on his behalf. Naomi therefore says: I will seek for you a form of rest in which **“it may be well with you”**—true and genuine good.

**“And now, is not Boaz our kinsman?”**—That is, the threshing floor was located at the edge of the field, and the winnowing of the barley did not take place directly on the threshing floor but in the field before it, where the wind was favorable. Certainly, all the workers—the young men and young women engaged in the labor—would be there, and once Ruth entered the field it would be impossible for them not to see her. Naomi therefore reassured her that there was no concern about being seen in the field before the threshing floor, for **“Boaz is our kinsman”**, and everyone knew that Ruth was connected to that household because of family ties. They would naturally assume that she had come to observe the threshing and winnowing, as relatives often rejoice in the success of their kin. Furthermore, **“you have been with his young women”**, and people would think she had come to visit the young women whom she had not seen since the harvest ended. **“Behold, he is winnowing the barley tonight”**—thus people would conclude that she had come at night specifically to watch the winnowing and to see her companions among the young women.

**“And wash yourself”**—Naomi meant that the essential preparation Ruth should make consisted of washing, anointing herself, and putting on her **“garments”**—that is, festive clothing. [As explained elsewhere (Mishpatim II, §187), the term *simlah* differs from *beged* (“garment”) or *levush* (“clothing”), referring specifically to fine garments worn on Sabbaths and festivals.] This preparation was to be made in order that **“you may go down to the threshing floor.”** Naomi did not explicitly specify when Ruth should dress, for she trusted Ruth to understand this through her own wisdom. The main instruction was that after Ruth waited there until the completion of the winnowing work, when the laborers had begun returning home and Boaz sat down to eat, only then should she quietly descend to the threshing floor itself in such a way that from that point onward **“you should not make yourself known to any man”**—everyone would assume that she had gone home, while she remained there concealed until Boaz had finished eating and drinking. Furthermore, the phrase **“put your garments upon yourself”** does not mean that Ruth was to wear them immediately—for had that been intended, Scripture would have said: **“and dress yourself in your garments.”** Rather, it means that she should carry them on her shoulders and have them with her, to put on only once she reached the threshing floor, not beforehand, since wearing festive garments earlier might expose her to suspicion.

**ויהי בשכבו**, ר"ל ובועז בודאי לא ישכב בשדה רק ישכב בגורן שהוא מוקף מחיצות, ואחר שאת תהי מסתתרת בגורן בודאי תדע את המקום אשר ישכב שם בגרן, ובאת וגלית מרגלותיו ושכבת, פי' כי הם בושו מלתבוע את בועז בפה שישא אותה ויעצה אותה שתתבע זאת ממנו ע"י סימנים כמאן דמחזי במחוג, שכבר בארנו במק"א מטעם המקובלים בענין היבום וחליצת הנעל, שהגוף הוא נעל של הנפש שא"א לנשמה הרוחנית לעמוד בעולם הגופים מבלי גוף כמו שא"א להענוג והרך לעמוד במקום רפש וטיט בלא נעל שלא יטנפו רגליו, ועל סוד זה אמר ה' של נעלך מעל רגלך, והאיש המת בלא בנים ואין לנפשו מרגוע והיא עודנה מקשקשת באשתו אשר לא עשה עמה פרי וימחה שמו מישראל, כשיבם אותה אחיו ויתן זרע לאחיו הוא כאלו נפש המת באה שנית לעולם כי הנולד הוא עצמו האח המת ויקרא על שמו והוא יקום תחתיו לנחלה וכמ"ש יולד בן לנעמי, כי עובד בן בועז היה בעצמו מחלון בן נעמי, ואז יש לנפש המת נעל, ר"ל גוף שבו תעמוד שנית בעולם הגופים, אולם אם לא יחפוץ ליבמה, תשאר נפש המת בלא נעל ולא תוכל לעמוד שנית בעולם הגופים לכן ירמזו זה במה שתשלוף נעלו מעל רגלו ויקרא שמו בישראל בית חלוץ הנעל, כי כן חלץ נעל של אחיו המת ר"ל שנשמתו היא חלוץ הנעל, ר"ל מחוסרת גוף וגויה, וזה רמזה לבועז במה שגלתה מרגלותיו ותשכב, ר"ל רמזה לו אחר שאתה הגואל ליבם, א"כ או תגלה רגליך להיות בלא נעל ותקרא בית חלוץ הנעל, או שאשכב בצדך להקים לקרובך שם בישראל ואז תכסה רגלך בנעל, והוא יגיד לך את אשר תעשין דהיינו או שיצוה אותך לתבוע גואל הקרוב יותר או שהוא ידבר עם הגואל:


**“And when he lies down”**—Boaz would certainly not sleep out in the open field, but rather in the threshing floor itself, which was enclosed. Since Ruth would be hiding there, she would surely know the place where he lay down in the threshing floor. **“Then you shall go, uncover his feet, and lie down”**—Naomi and Ruth were embarrassed to ask Boaz openly to marry Ruth, and Naomi therefore advised her to communicate the request through symbolic actions, hinting at it indirectly. As explained elsewhere, according to the teachings of the mystics concerning levirate marriage (*yibbum*) and the removal of the shoe (*chalitzah*), the body is like a shoe for the soul. Just as a delicate person cannot walk through mud and filth without shoes lest his feet become soiled, so too the spiritual soul cannot exist in the physical world without a body. This is the secret behind God’s command: **“Remove your shoes from your feet”** (Exodus 3:5). When a man dies childless, his soul finds no rest, still attached to the wife with whom he produced no offspring, and his name risks being erased from Israel. But when his brother marries the widow and produces offspring in the deceased brother’s name, it is as though the soul of the dead man returns to the world, for the child born is regarded as the deceased brother himself, bearing his name and standing in his place with regard to inheritance. Thus Scripture says: **“A son has been born to Naomi”** (Ruth 4:17), for Oved, the son of Boaz, was in a sense Mahlon, the son of Naomi. In this way, the soul of the deceased gains a *shoe*—that is, a body through which it may again stand in the physical world. However, if the redeemer refuses to perform levirate marriage, the deceased soul remains *without a shoe*, unable to stand again in the physical world. Therefore, this is symbolized by removing the man’s shoe from his foot, and he is called in Israel: **“the house of him whose shoe was removed”** (Deuteronomy 25:10), for he has removed the shoe of his deceased brother—meaning, the brother’s soul remains deprived of body and physical form. This is what Ruth hinted to Boaz by **“uncovering his feet and lying down.”** Her meaning was: since you are the redeemer who may perform levirate-like redemption, either you will uncover your feet and remain without a shoe, thereby becoming **“the house of him whose shoe was removed,”** or I will lie beside you so that you may raise up a name in Israel for your relative, and thereby your foot will again be covered with a shoe. **“And he will tell you what you shall do”**—that is, either he will instruct you to seek the nearer redeemer, or he himself will speak with that redeemer on your behalf.

**“And she said: I am Ruth, your maidservant”**—That is, do not think this an act of impropriety, that a woman should approach a man in this manner, for **“I am your maidservant”**—just as there exists a bond between a Hebrew maidservant and her master, who is obligated to designate her in marriage (*yi‘ud*), so too there is a connection between you and me through the commandment of levirate-like redemption. Therefore: **“Spread your wing over your maidservant”**—this is a metaphor borrowed from birds, which spread their wings over their mates at the time of union. Ruth also hinted that this was not an act of forbidden intimacy contrary to Torah law, for the corners (*kanfot*) of a garment bearing *tzitzit* protect against such sinful relations, as in the story mentioned in the Sifrei at the end of Shelach concerning the harlot in the distant cities who charged four hundred zuz for her services. On the contrary, in this union, **“spread the wing of your garment over me”**—for this is a union of mitzvah, **“for you are a redeemer.”**

All the more so, you could surely have found a wealthy young man willing to marry you because of your beauty. Yet you chose an older man in order to establish a name for your deceased husband. This is a great kindness that you are showing to your husband.

### Verse 1

**ובועז עלה השער**, ששם מושב הסנהדרין, והיה דעתו להזמין את הגואל ע"י שלוחא דבי דינא והזמין ה' שהגואל עבר שם ועז"א והנה הגואל עובר, שמלת הנה מורה דבר חדש כי לא היה דרכו לעבור דרך שם רק אשר דבר בועז מצד שדבר בועז שי"ל ענין עם הגואל הזמין ה' שיעבור, וע"כ אמר סורה שבה פה שיסור מדרכו שרוצה ללכת ולישב, וקראו פלוני אלמוני ר"ל שיש עמך דבר פלא, כמו לפלא נדר, שמורה על בחירת המחשבה, וענין אלמוני היינו קשר כמו מאלמים אלומים, כי דבר מן הקשר שיש לו עם גאולתו שיפלא ויברר דבר זה:


**“And Boaz went up to the gate”**—for there was the seat of the Sanhedrin. Boaz intended to summon the redeemer through an agent of the court, but God arranged matters such that the redeemer himself happened to pass by there. Thus Scripture says: **“and behold, the redeemer was passing by”**—the word **“behold”** indicates something new, for it was not ordinarily his custom to pass that way. Rather, because Boaz had business with the redeemer, God arranged for him to pass there. Therefore Boaz said: **“Turn aside, sit here”**—meaning, turn from your path, which you intended to continue, and sit down. He called him **“Ploni Almoni”**—meaning, “So-and-so,” but Malbim explains the phrase as implying: there is a matter of significance and wonder (*pele*) concerning you, as in **“to utter a wondrous vow”** (Numbers 6:2), which denotes deliberate choice and consideration. The term *Almoni* is related to binding or connection, as in **“binding sheaves”** (*me’almim alumim*). Thus, Boaz hinted that there was an important matter concerning the bond or connection involved in his act of redemption, one that required clarification.

**ויקח עשרה אנשים**, חז"ל דרשו שהכין אותם ודרש לפניהם עמוני ולא עמונית, והבין הוא דת"ח שאמר דבר הלכה אם קודם מעשה אמרה שומעים לו ואם אחר מעשה אמר אין שומעים לו כי נוגע בדבר, וע"כ הקדים לקחתם עתה שאין נוגע בדבר כי עתה הגאולה והיבום שייך אל טוב והוא אינו נוגע בדבר, ועוד אמרו חז"ל שברכת חתנים בעשרה ורצה שיהיו מזומנים תכף אל ברכת חתנים שיהיו שם בכל אופן:


**“And he took ten men”**—Our Sages expounded that Boaz assembled them and taught before them the ruling: **“an Ammonite, but not an Ammonitess; a Moabite, but not a Moabitess.”** Boaz understood the principle that when a Torah scholar states a legal ruling, if he says it *before* the matter concerns him personally, his words are accepted; but if he says it *after* the matter concerns him, his ruling is not accepted, since he is then considered personally interested in the outcome. Therefore, Boaz gathered these men now, before he himself had any personal stake in the matter, because at this point the redemption and levirate-like marriage still belonged to the nearer redeemer, and Boaz was not yet involved personally. Furthermore, our Sages said that the blessing of bridegrooms requires ten men, and Boaz wished that they already be present so that the wedding blessings could be recited immediately, whatever the outcome might be.

**“And he said to the redeemer: Naomi has offered for sale the parcel of land that belonged to our brother Elimelech”**—This cannot mean that Naomi had already sold the field to another person and Boaz sought for the redeemer to buy it back from the purchaser, for ancestral land cannot be redeemed within less than two years. Furthermore, Scripture later says: **“on the day you acquire the field from the hand of Naomi,”** implying that the purchase had not yet taken place. Therefore, the meaning is that Naomi had agreed to sell it, and it had already been publicly announced that this field was available for purchase, such that it was as though she had already sold it to whoever would choose to buy it. [Alternatively, the meaning may be: Naomi wished to sell it, and I told her, “I will first reveal the matter to you and say to you: buy it.” According to this interpretation, the phrase **“if he will not redeem”** (spoken in the third person) refers back to what Boaz had said to Naomi: “I told her that I would first inform you and say before the elders of my people: ‘Acquire it; if you will redeem, redeem.’” Thus Boaz had promised Naomi that he would speak to the redeemer, and when he said: **“if the redeemer will not redeem”**, the remainder is left unstated because it is obvious from the context.] From the phrase **“the parcel of land”** it appears that this was only part of the field, not the whole of it. [It is strained to say, as Ibn Ezra does, that Elimelech did not own an entire field.] Rather, after the deaths of Elimelech and his sons, Naomi took a portion of the field as part of her marriage settlement, and Ruth likewise took a portion through her own marriage settlement. Boaz first informed the redeemer about Naomi’s portion—namely, that she wished to sell the half of the field belonging to her from Elimelech’s estate.

**ויאמר בועז**. אז גלה לו בועז שא"א לגאול חלק של נעמי לבד כי הגואל הקונה חלק של נעמי יצטרך לגאול גם החלק של רות, [ויש הבדל ביניהם שחלק של נעמי יקנה באחד מדרכי הקניה שקונים בהם שדה, אבל חלק השייך לרות לא יקנה באופן זה רק בדרך יבום שייבם את רות וממילא יזכה בהשדה, רק שאז צריך להקים שם המת על נחלתו אחר שבא לזכות בחלק זה ע"י יבום שעי"כ יקום ע"ש המת לנחלה, וז"ש ביום קנותך השדה ר"ל לא תקנה חלקת השדה דהיינו רק חלק של נעמי לבד רק תקנה השדה כלה בשני חלקיו, ויש חילוק בדרכי קניתם שחלק של נעמי תקנה מיד נעמי בדרכי הקניה, ותתן כסף מחירו, אמנם החלק השני שתקנה מאת רות המואביה [בזה לא אמר מיד כי זה לא נקנה מיד ליד ע"י כסף] אשת המת קנית בחלק הזה צריך אתה לקנות אשת המת ע"י יבום ועמה תזכה גם בחלק השדה, ובזה יש תנאי להקים שם המת על נחלתו, שנחלה זו תקרא ע"ש אשת המת לא על שמך כדין יבום:


**“And Boaz said”**—At that point, Boaz revealed to him that it was impossible to redeem Naomi’s portion alone, because the redeemer who purchased Naomi’s share would also be required to redeem Ruth’s share. There was a distinction between them: Naomi’s portion could be acquired through one of the standard legal methods of purchasing land. However, the portion belonging to Ruth could not be acquired in that manner, but only through levirate-like marriage—by marrying Ruth, and thereby automatically obtaining the field. Yet in that case, there was an obligation **“to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance,”** since one acquires this portion through levirate-like redemption, whereby the deceased’s name is established through the inheritance. Thus Boaz said: **“On the day you acquire the field”**—meaning, you will not acquire merely **“the parcel of land”**, that is, Naomi’s portion alone, but the entire field in both of its shares. However, the methods of acquisition differ: Naomi’s portion you will acquire **“from the hand of Naomi”** through ordinary legal means of purchase, paying its price in money. But the second portion, which you acquire **“from Ruth the Moabite”**—here Scripture does not say **“from the hand of”**, because this is not transferred directly by payment—rather, since she is **“the wife of the deceased,”** you must acquire that share by marrying the deceased’s widow through levirate-like redemption, and together with her you gain the field. But this comes with the condition: **“to establish the name of the dead upon his inheritance”**—meaning, this inheritance will be reckoned in the name of the deceased husband through his wife, not in your own name, according to the law of levirate marriage.

It may therefore be that he repeated: **“for I cannot redeem it”** because of these two reasons together.

מבואר שקנין זה נבטל אח"כ, וי"ל הטעם עפמ"ש התוס' בערכין (דף למ"ד) שכל דבר החוזר לבעלים כגון מתנה ע"מ להחזיר או שאלה או שכירות אינו נקנה בחליפין, ויקשה ע"ז הא קנין חליפין למדינן מבועז ושלף איש נעלו ושם יורש הוי וסופו לחזור ביובל כמ"ש ר' יוחנן דאחין שחלקו לקוחות הם ומחזירים זל"ז ביובל ואז היה היובל נוהג, וי"ל עפמ"ש בגיטין (דף מ"ח) אמר ר' חסדא מחלוקת ביובל שני אבל ביובל ראשון ד"ה מביא וקורא דאכתי לא סמיך דעתיה וע"כ כל זמן יובל ראשון היו קונים בקנין סודר אבל אחר היובל הראשון לא יכלו לקנות בק"ס שכבר ידעו שיוחזר ביובל וסמכו דעתם ע"ז ולכן תקנו קנין קצצה, אבל בקנין זה של בועז לא יכלו לקנות בקצצה, דהא הגואל הראשון לא רצה לגאול ואמר פן אשחית את נחלתי שלא דרש עמוני ולא עמונית ואם הי' מביאים חבית ושוברה לפני התינוקות היו אומרים שאינו מפני שמכרה אחוזתה רק מפני שנשא אשה שאינה הוגנת שע"ז היו ג"כ עושין קצצה וכמ"ש במד' וכמ"ש בכתובות (דף כח) כיצד קצצה אחד מן האחין שנשא אשה שאינה הוגנת וכו', ולכן חזר ונהג קנין חליפין שהיה נהוג לפנים בישראל בזמן היובל הראשון, כי אף שעתה היה היובל שאחר הראשון, הלא השדה שקנה ע"י יבום ג"כ אינו חוזר ביובל וכמ"ש בבכורות (דף נ"ב) ואלו שאין חוזרים ביובל וכו' והמיבם, וז"ש וזאת לפנים בישראל וגו' וזאת התעודה בישראל, ר"ל אחר שקנין זה נהג לפנים בזמן היובל הא' לכן שם זאת התעודה גם עתה שלא יכול לקנות על ידי קצצה:


**“Now this was formerly the custom in Israel”**—The Midrash says: At first, acquisitions were made through a shoe or sandal, as it says: **“a man drew off his shoe.”** Later, they came to make acquisitions through a *ketzatzah*. What was the law of *ketzatzah*? Rabbi Yose bar Avin said: Whenever a person sold his field [to a gentile—this wording appears to be a textual error], his relatives would bring a barrel full of roasted grain and nuts, break it before the children, and the children would gather them while proclaiming: **“So-and-so has been cut off from his inheritance!”** Likewise, if someone married a woman unfit for him, the relatives would bring a barrel full of roasted grain and nuts, break it before the children, and they would proclaim: **“So-and-so has been lost to his family!”** Afterward, they again made acquisitions through a shoe or sandal, as it says: **“A man drew off his shoe and gave it to his fellow.”** Later still, they came to make acquisitions through money, document, and possession. According to this, the meaning of **“formerly in Israel”** is: in ancient days, such as the era of Joshua, acquisitions were made through symbolic exchange (*chalipin*), whereas afterward they began using *ketzatzah*. For there is a distinction between *lifanim* (“formerly”) and *milfanim* (“from ancient times”): the term *milfanim* indicates a practice that continues from antiquity until the present, whereas *lifanim* indicates something practiced in earlier times but not later, as in: **“The Emim formerly dwelt there”** (Deuteronomy 2:10). Thus it is clear that this mode of acquisition later ceased. One may explain the reason based on Tosafot in Arakhin (30b), who state that anything destined to return to its owner—such as a gift on condition of return, a loaned item, or a rental—cannot be acquired through symbolic exchange (*chalipin*). One might object: but the law of *chalipin* is learned from Boaz, where it says: **“a man drew off his shoe,”** and there the property was inherited land, which ultimately returned in the Jubilee, as Rabbi Yochanan said that brothers who divide an inheritance are like purchasers and restore the land to one another in the Jubilee—and the Jubilee was in force then. It may be explained through what is stated in Gittin (48b): Rabbi Chisda said that the dispute concerns the second Jubilee, but regarding the first Jubilee all agree that one brings first fruits and recites the declaration, because people had not yet fully accepted that the land would revert. Therefore, throughout the first Jubilee period, acquisitions could still be made through symbolic exchange. But after the first Jubilee, once people knew the land would certainly return in the Jubilee and relied on that expectation, symbolic exchange was no longer effective, and they instituted *ketzatzah*. However, in the case of Boaz, acquisition through *ketzatzah* was impossible. For the nearer redeemer had refused redemption, saying: **“lest I impair my inheritance,”** because he did not interpret **“an Ammonite, but not an Ammonitess; a Moabite, but not a Moabitess.”** If they had brought a barrel and broken it before the children, people would have assumed that the issue was not the sale of inherited land but rather marriage to an unsuitable woman, for *ketzatzah* was also performed in such cases, as the Midrash states and as explained in Ketubot (28a): **“What is ketzatzah? One of the brothers who married an unsuitable woman…”** Therefore, the earlier practice of symbolic exchange was revived—the method used **“formerly in Israel”** during the first Jubilee period. For although this occurred after the first Jubilee, the field acquired through levirate-like marriage did not revert in the Jubilee, as taught in Bekhorot (52b): **“These do not return in the Jubilee… including one who acquires through levirate marriage.”** Thus Scripture says: **“Now this was formerly the custom in Israel”** and **“this was the manner of attestation in Israel”**—meaning, since this form of acquisition had once been practiced in the era of the first Jubilee, it was employed again here, because acquisition through *ketzatzah* was not feasible.

**“And the redeemer said to Boaz”**—After authorizing Boaz to redeem, the nearer redeemer transferred the right to him through a symbolic acquisition (*kinyan sudar*). The Rashba writes in Kiddushin (21a), concerning the statement there: **“as Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said: the nearer relative takes precedence”**—that one might ask: why is a verse needed to establish the priority of the closer relative, since this can already be derived from **“the nearest relative to him”**? He answers that the primary function of the verse is to make the order mandatory—that redemption must proceed specifically in this sequence. Thus, if an uncle is not financially able to redeem but a cousin is, redemption nevertheless cannot bypass the uncle. This appears to contradict the case of Boaz, where the nearer redeemer refused to redeem and the redemption passed to the more distant relative, Boaz. One must therefore say that this itself is the reason the nearer redeemer executed a formal acquisition: by means of the symbolic transfer, Boaz legally stood in the redeemer’s place, such that his power became as the redeemer’s own power.

**ויקח בעז**, מספר שלא נהג כמיבם את אשת אח שאז יבמה יבא עליה ויבמה וקדושי כסף ושטר אין קונים רק עושים מאמר שהוא מדרבנן, כי יבום הזה לא היה אלא מצד המנהג לבד וקדש אותה בקדושי כסף או שטר שעז"א ויקח בעז את רות ותהי לו לאשה, קודם שבא עליה, ואח"כ ויבא אליה, ויתן ה' לה הריון עפ"י השגחתו הרתה תכף הגם שעם בעלה הראשון שהיה בחור לא ילדה:


**“And Boaz took”**—Scripture relates that Boaz did not conduct himself as one performing formal levirate marriage with a brother’s widow, in which case **“his brother-in-law shall come to her”** and consummate the union, while monetary or documentary betrothal does not effect acquisition, and only a rabbinic declaration (*ma’amar*) is made. Rather, this form of levirate-like redemption existed here only by custom. Therefore, Boaz formally betrothed Ruth through money or document, as implied by: **“And Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife”**—that is, she first became his wife before he came to her. Only afterward does Scripture say: **“and he came to her.”** **“And Hashem gave her conception”**—through Divine Providence she conceived immediately, even though with her first husband, who had been young, she had borne no child.

**ותאמרנה הנשים וגו' ברוך ה' אשר לא השבית לך גואל היום**, ר"ל מה שנוגע אל היום צריך ברכה לה' שלא השבית לך גואל, כי בנך הוא הנולד בילד הזה כנודע מוסד היבום שהוא המת בעצמו והוא הגואל שלך שלא תמות ערירי, ומה שנוגע על העתיד יקרא שמו בישראל יהיה מגדולי ישראל ומאנשי השם:


**“And the women said… Blessed be Hashem, who has not left you without a redeemer this day”**—That is, with regard to the present moment, there is reason to bless Hashem, **“who has not left you without a redeemer”**, for the child born is, in a sense, your own son, according to the principle underlying levirate-like redemption: the deceased himself is regarded as returning through the child. As for the future, **“may his name be proclaimed in Israel”**—meaning, may he become one of the great men of Israel, renowned among people of name and distinction.

###### Malbim on Ruth 4:16:1

**ותקח נעמי את הילד**, עפ"ז היתה לו לאומנת כי הוא בנה ממש ותשא אותו כאם האומנת את בנה, והשכנות אמרו שיולד בן לנעמי כי כן הוא האמת שהוא בן נעמי מצד היבום וכן קראו שמו שהוא בן נעמי, ושמו העצמי קראו עובד על שעתיד לעבוד את ה' והוא אבי ישי שממנו נצמח מלכות בית דוד, וכבר באר האר"י ז"ל בספר הגלגולים (ע"פ עת אשר שלט האדם באדם לרע לו) שהנשמות העשוקות ביד עושקיהם כח בטקלא דעשקית נשמתין א"א להוציא את הנשמה מהם כי הם יונקות שפע על ידם, ומרמים אותם שמראים להם מקום פגום וחושבים ששם תשאר הנשמה בידם, ולבסוף תתגבר הנשמה על עושקיה, וכן נשמת השבטים נתנו ברחל ולאה שהיו אחיות וחשבו כי ישארו הנשמות בטומאה אחר שנשאו באיסור וכן נשמת פרץ בתמר ונשמת רות ונעמי בעמון ומואב, וע"כ אמר דוד בני איש עד מה כבודי לכלמה, ואמר במד' שהיו אומרים שבא מרות המואביה והוא אומר להם אמרו בלבבכם על משכבכם ואתם לא באתם מאיסור שתי אחיות ודומו, וזאת הרגישו העם והזקנים ואמרו יתן ה' את האשה וכו' כרחל וכלאה, ויהי ביתך כבית פרץ אשר ילדה תמר, שכ"ז היה מוכרח כדי להוציא הנפשות האלה במרמה זו מיד עושקיהם כח:


**“And Naomi took the child”**—Accordingly, Naomi became his nurse, for he was considered truly her son, and she carried him as a nurse carries her own child. The neighboring women said: **“A son has been born to Naomi”**—for this was regarded as the truth according to the principle of levirate-like redemption: he was Naomi’s son. Thus they called him by a name identifying him as Naomi’s son. His personal name, however, they called **“Oved”** (“Servant”), because he was destined to serve God. He became the father of Jesse, from whom the royal house of David emerged. The Ari z”l explains in *Sefer HaGilgulim* (on the verse **“the time when one man rules over another to his harm”**) that souls oppressed in the hands of those who exploit them cannot easily be extracted, because their oppressors draw spiritual sustenance through them. These oppressors deceive them by presenting them with a flawed place, imagining that the souls will remain under their control there; yet ultimately the souls overcome those who oppress them. Thus, according to this teaching, the souls of the tribes were placed through Rachel and Leah, who were sisters, with the thought that the souls would remain in impurity because the marriages were forbidden; likewise, the soul of Perez through Tamar, and the souls connected with Ruth and Naomi through Ammon and Moab. Therefore David said: **“O sons of men, how long shall my honor be turned into shame?”** (Psalms 4:3). The Midrash says that people would disparage David for descending from Ruth the Moabite, and David responded to them: **“Speak in your hearts upon your beds and be silent”**—meaning: did you yourselves not descend from the matter of two sisters (Rachel and Leah), which also involved difficulty? This, says Malbim, is what the people and elders sensed when they blessed: **“May Hashem make the woman… like Rachel and Leah”** and **“May your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore”**—for all of this, according to this mystical interpretation, was necessary in order to draw forth these souls through such means from the grasp of their spiritual oppressors.
