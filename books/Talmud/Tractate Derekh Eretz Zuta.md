# Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta

מסכת דרך ארץ זוטא

Link: [https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta)

הדרכות מוסר לתלמידי חכמים, דרשות על ימות המשיח וכן פרק העוסק בשבח השלום.

Characteristics of a scholar, teachings about the messianic age, and a chapter extolling peace.

Sections: Chapter-Halakhah

Location: Talmud/Bavli/Minor Tractates

## Chapter 1

###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 1:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/1:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/1:1)


דרכן של תלמידי חכמים עניו ושפל רוח זריז ממולא עלוב ואהוב לכל אדם. שפל לאנשי ביתו ירא חטא ודורש את האדם לפי מעשיו. ואומר כל מה שיש בעוה״ז אין לי חפץ בהם [לפי שאין העוה״ז שלי] יושב ומשנה ומטנף כסותו לפני רגלי תלמידי חכמים. ואין איש רואה בו דבר רע. שואל כענין ומשיב כהלכה.


The characteristics of a scholar are that he is meek, humble,¹ alert, filled [with a desire for learning],², ‘bright’, as in Ḳid. 29b (Sonc. ed., p. 141). Cf. KR III, 2, p. 443, n. 13. modest, beloved by all, humble to the members of his household and sin-fearing. He judges a man [fairly] according to his deeds,³ and says ‘I have no desire for all the things of this world because this world is not for me’. He sits and studies,⁴ soiling his cloak at the feet of the scholars.⁵ H reads *mith‘aṭṭef*, ‘wrapped in his cloak’, perhaps in the same sense as in Aboth I, 4 (Sonc. ed., p. 3), ‘Suffer thyself to be covered by the dust of their feet’, i.e. sit in the dust on the ground at their feet while they teach. GRA reads *maṭṭil*, ‘and throws his cloak in front of the feet of scholars’, perhaps for them to sit on. For a different explanation, cf. KR III, p. 444, n. 17. In him no one sees any evil. He questions according to the subject-matter⁶ and answers to the point.⁷

footnotes:
¹ Cf. Aboth IV, 4 (Sonc. ed., p. 45).
² H reads memullaḥ
³ He does not, e.g., interpret a man’s good actions as determined by ulterior motives and does not show favour where it is not deserved.
⁴ H omits ‘and studies’.
⁵ For meṭannef
⁶ Cf. Shab. 3b (Sonc. ed., pp. 5f.), ‘R. Ḥiyya said to Rab: “When Rabbi is occupied with one tractate, do not ask him a question relating to another” ’.
⁷ lit. ‘in accordance with the accepted decision’.


###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 1:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/1:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/1:2)


הי' כנוד בקוע שנתפתח להכניס הרוח וכערוגה עמוקה שמחזקת את מימיה. וכקנקנה זפותה שמשמרת את יינה. כספוג שהוא סופג את הכל. אל תהיה כפתח גדול שמכניס את הרוח ולא כפתח קטן שמנבל את היקרים. היה כאסקופה התחתונה שהכל דשין בה וכיתד הנמוכה שהכל תולין בה:


Be like a gourd split open that the wind may enter,⁸ like a deep furrow which retains its water,⁹ like a jar coated with pitch which preserves its wine,¹⁰ and like a sponge which absorbs all.¹¹
Be not like a wide opening which allows the wind to rush in,¹² or like a narrow opening which injures the valuable [as they press through].¹³ Be like a doorstep on which everyone treads¹⁴ and like a low peg which is within the reach of all.¹⁵

footnotes:
⁸ Let your mind be open to receive instruction. For readings which add the negative, cf. ARN p. 85 and KR p. 449.
⁹ Such a bed yields good produce; so must a scholar study continuously and raise good disciples.
¹⁰ i.e. he must have a retentive memory.
¹¹ Cf. Aboth V, 18 (Sonc. ed., V, 15, p. 69).
¹² i.e. do not cultivate associates indiscriminately, yet do not be so unapproachable that worthy companionships cannot get near to you. Or, do not create a stir on entering and leaving; or, do not allow petty and frivolous ideas to enter your mind.
¹³ By having to stoop or squeeze in; i.e. do not cause people worthy or your association to demean themselves or hold aloof.
¹⁴ i.e. be ready to learn from all.
¹⁵ lit. ‘on which all hang [their things]’.

Do not turn away from meritorious deeds and do not be attracted to heresy.²⁰, a term for the teachings of the early Jewish-Christians. Do not inquire of Sadducees²¹ lest you be drawn into Gehinnom.²²

## Chapter 2

###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 2:1

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:1](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:1)


כל דרכיך יהיו לשם שמים. הוי אוהב את השמים וירא מן השמים. הוי חרד ושש על כל המצות.


Let all your undertakings be for the name of Heaven.² Love Heaven and fear Heaven. Hurry to perform in gladness all the precepts.

footnotes:
² Having a sincere and high purpose. ‘Heaven’ is a term for God.


###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 2:2

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:2](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:2)


שב לפני הזקנים והטה אזנך לשמוע את דבריהם והקשב אזניך לדברי חבירך. אל תהי נבהל להשיב. ותהא מחשב דברים כענין. ואומר על ראשון ראשון ועל אחרון אחרון. והוי מודה על האמת. ואל תדבר בפני מי שגדול ממך בחכמה.


Sit before the Elders and incline your ear to hear their words. Listen carefully to the words of your fellow; be not hasty to answer but give proper thought to your words. ###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 2:3

[https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:3](https://torahapp.org/share/book/Tractate%20Derekh%20Eretz%20Zuta/r/2:3)


ואם חפצת ללמד אל תאמר על מה שלא שמעת שמעתי. ואם שאלוך דבר קטן ואי אתה יודע בו אל תבוש לומר איני יודע. ואם שנית לך ולא שמעת אל תבוש לומר שנה לי. ואל תשא פנים לעצמך לומר (לא) שמעתי.


If you desire to study,³ do not say regarding what you do not understand, ‘I understand’.⁴ If you are questioned on [even] a trivial matter and you are not acquainted with it, be not ashamed to say, ‘I do not know’. If you were taught something which you did not grasp, be not ashamed to say [to the teacher], ‘Repeat it to me’ and do not pretend to yourself ‘I understood it’.

###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 2:4

עשה דברים לשום פעולתן לדבר בהם לשמים: אל תשימם כעטרה להתעטר בהן. ולא כקורדום לאכול בהן ולא כמר לחפור בהן. קבל עליך דברי תורה בצער. ואל תהי מבקש עלבונך: חשבון יפה ופרק טוב יש הבטחה ויש אמתי:


Do [good] deeds for the sake of their Maker⁵, interprets, ‘for the performance of them’, i.e. doing good for its own sake and not from an interested motive. and speak of them⁶ for their own sake.⁷ Make not of them a crown wherewith to adorn yourself, an axe with which to chop or a spade with which to dig.⁸ Undertake to study the Torah [even] in straitened circumstances and disregard any insult. A well-ordered [moral] stock-taking and a virtuous adolescence [yield] security and truth.⁹

⁶ viz. words of Torah.

עשה תורתך בחנם ולא תטול עליה שכר שהמקום נתנה בחנם. ואין נוטלין שכר על דברי תורה. ואם אתה נוטל שכר על דברי תורה נמצאת מחריב את כל העולם כולו: אל תאמר אין לי ממון. שכל הממון שלו הוא שנאמר (חגי ב׳:ח׳) לי הכסף ולי הזהב (אמר) [נאום] ה׳ צבאות. אם עשית צדקה תזכה לממון. ואם זכית לממון עשה ממנו צדקה בעוד שהוא בידך קנה ממנו העולם הזה ותנחול העוה״ב שאם אין אתה עושה ממנו צדקה יעוף פתאום שנאמר (משלי כ״ג:ה׳) התעיף עיניך בו ואיננו:


Do your [teaching of] Torah gratuitously and accept no remuneration for it; because the Omnipresent gave it gratuitously⁴, this does not apply to Scripture, for the teaching of which a fee is allowed. and one may not take a fee for the [teaching of] words of Torah. If you should accept remuneration for the [teaching of] words of Torah, you will consequently destroy [the moral constitution of] the entire world. Say not, ‘I have no money’, since all money is His,⁵ as it is stated, *Mine is the silver, and Mine the gold, saith the Lord of hosts*.⁶
If you practise charity you will merit wealth; and if you merited wealth practise charity with it. While you still possess it [live in such a manner that you] gain through it this world and also inherit the World to Come; for if you do not practise charity with it, it will suddenly disappear, as it is stated, *Wilt thou set thine eyes upon it? It is gone*.⁷

footnotes:
⁴ Cf. Ned. 36b-37a (Sonc. ed., p. 113). As Moses taught the Elders gratuitously so must they pass their teaching on to others without pay. According to the Talmud, loc. cit.
⁵ He will provide you with sustenance without having to take payment for teaching.
⁶ Hag. 2, 8.
⁷ Prov. 23, 5.

[אל תאמר איש פלוני צדיק ואני איני צדיק ששניכם עתידים ליתן את הדין ס״א] אל תאמר איש פלוני גבור ואני חלש. שאין גבורה אלא בתורה שנאמר (תהילים ק״ג:כ׳) ברכו ה' מלאכיו גבורי כח עושי דברו לשמוע בקול דברו.


Say not, ‘That man is wise⁸ but I am not wise’,⁹ because you did not attend [upon scholars] as he did.¹⁰ Say not, ‘That man is rich but I am not rich’,¹¹ because not everyone has the privilege to enjoy two tables.¹² Say not, ‘That man is handsome but I am ugly’, because at the hour of death man becomes carrion; furthermore [animal] carrion may be sold to a Gentile or may be given as a gift, but no one cares for human carrion.¹³ Say not, ‘That man is righteous but I am not righteous, because both of you are destined to give an account before the Most High. Say not, ‘That man is strong but I am weak’,¹⁴ because strength is only with [them who fulfil] the Torah, as it is stated, *Bless the Lord, ye angels of His, ye mighty in strength, that fulfil His word, hearkening unto the voice of His word*.¹⁵

footnotes:
⁸ Endowed by nature with great intellectual gifts.
⁹ i.e. I am not so endowed and therefore it is useless for me to study.
¹⁰ One must make every effort to gain knowledge; cf. Meg. 6b (Sonc. ed., p. 30).
¹¹ Although I am more God-fearing than he.
¹² i.e. enjoy prosperity in this world and the bliss of the hereafter.
¹³ So in death the difference between the handsome and the ugly ceases to exist.
¹⁴ And therefore unable to control my sinful desires.
¹⁵ Ps. 103, 20. So occupy yourself with the study of the Torah and you will increase moral strength.

## Chapter 7

###### Tractate Derekh Eretz Zuta 7:1

רשב״ג אומר גדול הוא השלום שהרי אהרן הכהן לא נשתבח אלא בשביל השלום שהיה אוהב שלום ורודף שלום ומקדים שלום ומשיב שלום שנאמר (מלאכי ב׳:ו׳) בשלום ובמישור הלך אתי (מה כתיב בתריה ורבים השיב מעון.אלא) מלמד כשהיה רואה שני בני אדם שונאין זה את זה הולך אצל אחד מהן ואמר לו למה שונא אתה את פלוני כבר בא אלי לביתי ונשתטח לפני ואמר לי חטאתי לפלוני לך ופייס עליו ומניח לזה והולך אצל השני ואומר לו כראשון והיה משים שלום ואהבה וריעות בין אדם לחבירו ורבים השיב מעון: א״ר יהושע הנביא נקרא מלאך והחכם נקרא מלאך. הנביא נקרא מלאך שנאמר (חגי א׳:י״ג) ויאמר חגי מלאך יי׳. והחכם נקרא מלאך שנאמר (מלאכי ב׳:ז׳) מלאך יי׳ צבאות הוא: יכול יהא כהן עם הארץ ת״ל (שם) ותורה יבקשו מפיהו :


Rabban Simeon b. Gamaliel said: Great is peace, for Aaron the [High] Priest was praised only for peace; for he loved peace, pursued peace, greeted with the salutation of peace and responded with it, as it is stated, *He walked with Me in peace and uprightness*.⁵⁶ It teaches that when he noticed two persons at enmity one with the other, he used to go first to one of them and say to him, ‘Why do you hate So-and-so? He has already come to my house, prostrated himself before me and said to me, “I sinned against So-and-so”. Go and pacify him’. When he left this one, he went to the other and spoke to him similarly, and so made peace, love and friendship between a man and his fellow. What is written subsequently? *For he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts*.⁵⁷ 7. The text of GRA has been followed. [Cf. above, p. 599.]
R. Joshua said: The prophet is called ‘messenger [of the Lord]’ and the priest⁵⁸ is called ‘messenger [of the Lord]’. The prophet is called ‘messenger [of the Lord]’, as it is stated, *Then spoke Haggai the Lord’s messenger;*⁵⁹ and the priest is called ‘messenger [of the Lord]’, as it is stated, *For he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts*.⁶⁰ Lest you think that this applies even to a priest who is an *‘am ha-’areẓ*, the text declares, *And they should seek the law at his mouth*.⁶¹

ר׳ יהושע דסכנין בשם רבי לוי אמר גדול השלום שכל הברכות והתפלות חותמין בשלום. ק״ש חותמין בשלום ופרוס סכת שלומיך. ברכת כהנים חותמין בשלום וישם לך שלום (במדבר ו׳:כ״ו) (כל הברכות חותמים בשלום עושה השלום):
